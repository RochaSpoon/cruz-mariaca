## The short version

Addiction is a real illness and also a spiritual struggle. The Church says sin "creates a proclivity to sin" (CCC 1865), and that Christ's grace is enough (2 Corinthians 12:9). Get both medical and spiritual help.

## What the Church teaches

"Sin creates a proclivity to sin; it engenders vice by repetition of the same acts. This results in perverse inclinations which cloud conscience and corrupt the concrete judgment of good and evil" (CCC 1865).

The Catechism also says that "the promptings of feelings and passions can also diminish the voluntary and free character of the offense, as can external pressures or pathological disorders" (CCC 1860).

Paul describes the struggle in Romans 7: he does the evil he does not want (Romans 7:15-25).

Christ tells Paul, "My grace is sufficient for thee: for power is made perfect in infirmity" (2 Corinthians 12:9).

The Church offers the sacraments, especially Confession and the Eucharist, as ways of grace (CCC 1458, 1999).

## Scripture

> "And he said to me: My grace is sufficient for thee; for power is made perfect in infirmity. Gladly therefore will I glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may dwell in me. For which cause I please myself in my infirmities, in reproaches, in necessities, in persecutions, in distresses, for Christ. For when I am weak, then am I powerful."
*2 Corinthians 12:9-10 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says he gladly boasts in his infirmities so that the power of Christ may dwell in him.

## Why this matters today

Addiction is both a disease and a pattern of choices. The Church does not say it is only one or the other.

This lesson is not medical advice. A doctor, a counselor and a recovery group are real help, and so is a priest.

## What's at stake

Shame keeps people hidden. The Church says there is forgiveness, and that help is available.

## How to live it

- **Tell someone** you trust today.
- **See a doctor** or a counselor.
- **Go to a recovery group.**
- **Go to Confession** as often as you need.
