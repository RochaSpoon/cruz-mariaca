## The short version

The Church allows medical procedures that heal or restore, and rejects the idolizing of physical perfection. Direct mutilation is wrong except for strictly therapeutic reasons (CCC 2289, 2297).

## What the Church teaches

"Life and physical health are precious gifts entrusted to us by God. We must take reasonable care of them" (CCC 2288).

The Catechism rejects "the cult of the body" and the idolizing of "physical perfection" (CCC 2289).

"Except when performed for strictly therapeutic medical reasons, directly intended amputations, mutilations, and sterilizations performed on innocent persons are against the moral law" (CCC 2297).

Reconstructive surgery after injury or illness is therapeutic. Whether a given cosmetic procedure is acceptable depends on its purpose, its risks and the motive behind it.

The Catechism's general test for medical risk is that the dangers must be proportionate to the good sought (CCC 2296 gives this test for organ donation, and the same logic applies to prudence generally).

## Scripture

*1 Corinthians 6:19-20 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit and that you are not your own.

## Why this matters today

Many procedures are routine and some are driven by pressure and insecurity. The Church asks the motive question: what are you trying to fix?

The Church does not give a list of permitted procedures. Prudence and a good doctor are the guides.

## What's at stake

If you change your body to please other people, it may never be enough. If you change it to heal, it can be a good.

## How to live it

- **Ask why** you want it, and whether it will fix that.
- **Talk to someone** who is not selling the procedure.
- **Count the risks.**
- **Pray** before you decide.
