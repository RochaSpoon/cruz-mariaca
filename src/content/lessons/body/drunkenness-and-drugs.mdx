## The short version

Wine is a gift. Jesus' first miracle made about 150 gallons of it (John 2). Drinking in moderation is fine. Getting drunk is a sin, and getting deliberately drunk to the point of losing your reason is grave. Using drugs (including marijuana) to get high is a grave offense. Drinking underage breaks a just law and is disobedience.

## What the Church teaches

"The virtue of temperance disposes us to avoid every kind of excess: the abuse of food, alcohol, tobacco, or medicine. Those incur grave guilt who, by drunkenness or a love of speed, endanger their own and others' safety on the road, at sea, or in the air" (CCC 2290).

"The use of drugs inflicts very grave damage on human health and life. Their use, except on strictly therapeutic grounds, is a grave offense. Clandestine production of and trafficking in drugs are scandalous practices" (CCC 2291).

## Scripture

> "And be not drunk with wine, wherein is luxury; but be ye filled with the Holy Spirit."
*Ephesians 5:18 (Douay-Rheims)*

Drunkards are listed among those who "shall not possess the kingdom of God" (1 Corinthians 6:10; Galatians 5:21). Proverbs 23:29-35 describes the hangover, the bruises, the confusion, and the craving for another drink. It sounds like it was written last weekend.

## Is marijuana really the same as drugs?

The Catechism makes no exception for recreational marijuana. Getting high on purpose clouds your reason and judgment, which is exactly what makes drunkenness wrong. Medical use under a doctor's care for real treatment is a different question (CCC 2291, "strictly therapeutic grounds"). Legal in California doesn't make it moral.

## What about vaping or nicotine?

Nicotine doesn't intoxicate like alcohol or weed, so it's not the same category. But it's highly addictive and harms your health, and your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians 6:19). Temperance applies. If you're underage, it's also disobedience to just law and to your parents.

## Why this hits hard today

Weed is legal in many states, and THC vapes and edibles are everywhere. Fentanyl contamination has made experimenting with pills deadly: one counterfeit pill can kill. Party culture treats getting blackout drunk as normal fun.

## What's at stake

Deliberate drunkenness and drug use are grave matter. Beyond that: car crashes, sexual assault, overdoses, addiction, and doing things you'd never do sober. You can't pray, think clearly, or resist temptation when your reason is gone.

## How to live it

- **If you're underage, don't drink.** It's that simple.
- **If you're of age:** know your limit and stay well under it. Never drive after drinking.
- **Have an exit plan** for parties.
- **Never take pills that weren't prescribed to you,** from anyone.
- **If you're addicted,** get help. Alcoholics Anonymous and Narcotics Anonymous work, and Venerable Matt Talbot, a recovered alcoholic from Dublin, is a patron for those who struggle.
