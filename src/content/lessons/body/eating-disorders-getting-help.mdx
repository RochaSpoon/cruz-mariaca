## The short version

Eating disorders are real illnesses that need real care, not shame. The Church teaches that your body is a gift to be cared for (CCC 2288), and Jesus invites the weary to come to him (Matthew 11:28).

## What the Church teaches

"Life and physical health are precious gifts entrusted to us by God. We must take reasonable care of them" (CCC 2288).

The Catechism rejects the cult of the body and the idolizing of physical perfection (CCC 2289).

Paul says your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians 6:19).

Jesus says, "Come to me, all you that labour, and are burdened, and I will refresh you" (Matthew 11:28).

This lesson is not medical advice. An eating disorder is a medical condition, and the right first step is a doctor or a counselor who knows the area.

## Scripture

> "Come to me, all you that labour, and are burdened, and I will refresh you. Take up my yoke upon you, and learn of me, because I am meek, and humble of heart: and you shall find rest to your souls. For my yoke is sweet and my burden light."
*Matthew 11:28-30 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus' invitation is to people who are tired. The Church takes it as meant for anyone struggling.

## Why this matters today

Eating disorders often hide behind discipline or diet. The Church's talk about fasting and temperance is not meant for them. If you struggle, do not use fasting as an excuse.

If you are in danger, tell an adult or call a crisis line right away.

## What's at stake

This is not a sin to hide. It is an illness to treat.

## How to live it

- **Tell a doctor** or a parent today.
- **Do not fast** from food for spiritual reasons if you struggle with this.
- **Find a therapist** who treats it.
- **Pray** with someone who knows.
