## The short version

Fasting is prayer with the body. The Catechism says Scripture and the Fathers insist on "fasting, prayer, and almsgiving" as the three forms of penance (CCC 1434).

## What the Church teaches

"The interior penance of the Christian can be expressed in many and various ways. Scripture and the Fathers insist above all on three forms, fasting, prayer, and almsgiving, which express conversion in relation to oneself, to God, and to others" (CCC 1434).

"The seasons and days of penance in the course of the liturgical year (Lent, and each Friday in memory of the death of the Lord) are intense moments of the Church's penitential practice" (CCC 1438).

The Church's fast and abstinence days: Ash Wednesday and Good Friday are days of fasting and abstinence (can. 1251). The law of fasting binds adults until the beginning of their sixtieth year, and abstinence binds those who have completed their fourteenth year (can. 1252).

Jesus says "when you fast," not "if," and says to do it without display (Matthew 6:16-18).

Joel calls Israel to turn back "in fasting, and in weeping, and in mourning" (Joel 2:12).

## Scripture

> "And when you fast, be not as the hypocrites, sad. For they disfigure their faces, that they may appear unto men to fast. Amen I say to you, they have received their reward. But thou, when thou fastest anoint thy head, and wash thy face; That thou appear not to men to fast, but to thy Father who is in secret: and thy Father who seeth in secret, will repay thee."
*Matthew 6:16-18 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "Now therefore saith the Lord: Be converted to me with all your heart, in fasting, and in weeping, and in mourning. And rend your hearts, and not your garments, and turn to the Lord your God: for he is gracious and merciful, patient and rich in mercy, and ready to repent of the evil."
*Joel 2:12-13 (Douay-Rheims)*

Both texts treat fasting as a way of turning toward God, not as an end in itself.

## Why this matters today

Fasting is now a wellness trend. The Church's version is about conversion and the poor, and it includes giving what you save (CCC 1434).

The Church does not ask people to fast if it would harm their health. Ask your doctor, and ask a priest about a substitute.

## What's at stake

If you cannot say no to your own appetites, it is hard to say it to anything else.

## How to live it

- **Start small:** one meal on Fridays in Lent.
- **Pray** when you feel hungry.
- **Give** what you saved.
- **Keep it quiet** (Matthew 6:16-18).
