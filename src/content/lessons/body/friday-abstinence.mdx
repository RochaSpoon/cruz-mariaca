## The short version

Catholics abstain from meat on Ash Wednesday, Good Friday and the Fridays of Lent. On other Fridays the bishops' conference can permit another penance (can. 1250-1253; CCC 2043).

## What the Church teaches

"The penitential days and times in the universal Church are every Friday of the whole year and the season of Lent" (can. 1250).

"Abstinence from meat, or from some other food as determined by the Episcopal Conference, is to be observed on all Fridays, unless a solemnity should fall on a Friday. Abstinence and fasting are to be observed on Ash Wednesday and Good Friday" (can. 1251).

The law of abstinence binds those who have completed their fourteenth year (can. 1252).

"The conference of bishops can determine more precisely the observance of fast and abstinence as well as substitute other forms of penance, especially works of charity and exercises of piety, in whole or in part, for abstinence and fast" (can. 1253).

The fifth precept of the Church is "You shall observe the prescribed days of fasting and abstinence" (CCC 2043).

## Scripture

> "And Jesus said to them: Can the children of the bridegroom mourn, as long as the bridegroom is with them? But the days will come, when the bridegroom shall be taken away from them, and then they shall fast."
*Matthew 9:15 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus says the days will come when the bridegroom is taken away, and then they will fast. The Church treats Friday as the day in memory of his death (CCC 1438).

## Why this matters today

Many people think Catholics must abstain every Friday of the year. The law names Fridays, and allows the bishops to substitute another penance outside Lent. In the United States the bishops' 1966 statement does this.

Fish is not the point. The Church asks for a real penance.

## What's at stake

A day that is different keeps a memory alive.

## How to live it

- **Skip meat on Fridays in Lent.**
- **On other Fridays,** choose another penance and do it.
- **Eat simply** and give the difference.
- **Tell people** why, if they ask.
