## The short version

The Catechism says the use of drugs, "except on strictly therapeutic grounds, is a grave offense" (CCC 2291). It does not name marijuana, and legality does not change the moral question.

## What the Church teaches

"The use of drugs inflicts very grave damage on human health and life. Their use, except on strictly therapeutic grounds, is a grave offense" (CCC 2291).

"Clandestine production of and trafficking in drugs are scandalous practices. They constitute direct co-operation in evil, since they encourage people to practices gravely contrary to the moral law" (CCC 2291).

The Catechism lists "the abuse of food, alcohol, tobacco, or medicine" as failures of temperance (CCC 2290).

The Catechism speaks of drugs in general and does not list marijuana. Medical use under a doctor's care is a therapeutic question. Recreational use, to get high, is the case the Catechism has in view.

Whether the state permits it is a separate matter. A lawful act can still be immoral (CCC 2409).

## Scripture

> "And be not drunk with wine, wherein is luxury; but be ye filled with the holy Spirit,"
*Ephesians 5:18 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says not to be drunk with wine, but to be filled with the Spirit. The Church uses the verse for any substance that clouds judgment.

## Why this matters today

Legalization has made many people think the question is closed. The Catechism's test is the effect on health and freedom, not the law.

People differ about what marijuana does to the user. This lesson makes no medical claim, and invites you to talk to a doctor about your own case.

## What's at stake

If a habit dulls your mind or runs your life, it takes what you were given to use.

## How to live it

- **Ask what it does** to your motivation and prayer.
- **Talk to a doctor** about any medical use.
- **Do not use** to escape something you need to face.
- **Get help** if you cannot stop.
