## The short version

Performance-enhancing drugs raise issues of health, fairness and honesty. The Catechism rejects the "cult of the body" and warns against abuse of medicine (CCC 2289-2290).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism rejects "a neo-pagan notion that tends to promote the cult of the body, to sacrifice everything for its sake, to idolize physical perfection and success at sports" (CCC 2289).

It says "by its selective preference of the strong over the weak, such a conception can lead to the perversion of human relationships" (CCC 2289).

Temperance avoids "the abuse of food, alcohol, tobacco, or medicine" (CCC 2290).

Where rules forbid a substance, using it in secret is cheating, which is lying (CCC 2482-2483) and an injustice to the other athletes (CCC 1807).

Therapeutic use under a doctor's care is a different matter (CCC 2291).

## Scripture

> "And every one that striveth for the mastery, refraineth himself from all things: and they indeed that they may receive a corruptible crown; but we an incorruptible one."
*1 Corinthians 9:25 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says athletes strive for a corruptible crown, but we for an incorruptible one.

## Why this matters today

Pressure to win pushes many athletes toward shortcuts. The Catechism names the underlying problem as idolizing success (CCC 2289).

The health risks are the doctor's field. The moral question is whether the use is honest, fair and safe.

## What's at stake

If you win by cheating, you have not won, and you may have damaged your health.

## How to live it

- **Follow the rules** of your sport.
- **Ask a doctor** about any supplement.
- **Ask yourself** what winning is for.
- **Respect** the athletes who did not cheat.
