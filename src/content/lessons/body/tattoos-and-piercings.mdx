## The short version

The Church has no official teaching on tattoos or piercings. Leviticus 19:28 forbids marks on the body, and the Catechism condemns direct mutilation except for therapeutic reasons (CCC 2297). Prudence, motive and permanence are the questions.

## What the Church teaches

Leviticus 19:28 forbids making cuttings in the flesh for the dead and printing marks on the body. The verse sits in a passage about pagan mourning and religious practices.

"Except when performed for strictly therapeutic medical reasons, directly intended amputations, mutilations, and sterilizations performed on innocent persons are against the moral law" (CCC 2297).

The Catechism does not name tattoos or piercings. Whether they fall under this is a matter of judgment.

The tradition would ask about the motive, the content and the permanence. A tattoo that is blasphemous, vulgar or tied to occult practice is a problem on any reading (CCC 2116, 2148).

The Catechism's concern about excess applies to the body in general: modesty and temperance guide the use of the body (CCC 1809, 2521).

## Scripture

> "You shall not make any cuttings in your flesh, for the dead, neither shall you make in yourselves any figures or marks: I am the Lord."
*Leviticus 19:28 (Douay-Rheims)*

Many Christians treat Leviticus 19:28 as tied to pagan mourning customs and not binding. Others take it more strictly. The Church has not issued a ruling.

## Why this matters today

Tattoos are very common now, and many Catholics have them, including religious ones.

Because they are permanent, they raise the question of regret. Thinking it through before you get one is the practical part of the advice.

## What's at stake

This is a question of prudence, not of a defined rule. Be honest about why you want it.

## How to live it

- **Wait** six months with the design in mind.
- **Ask what it will say** about you in twenty years.
- **Avoid** anything occult, blasphemous or degrading.
- **Talk to a priest** if you are unsure.
