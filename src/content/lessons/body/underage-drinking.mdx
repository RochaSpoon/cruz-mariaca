## The short version

The Church teaches temperance in drink and respect for just laws. Drunkenness is condemned, and so is endangering yourself and others (CCC 2290, 2240).

## What the Church teaches

"The virtue of temperance disposes us to avoid every kind of excess: the abuse of food, alcohol, tobacco, or medicine" (CCC 2290).

"Those incur grave guilt who, by drunkenness or a love of speed, endanger their own and others' safety on the road, at sea, or in the air" (CCC 2290).

Paul says to be subject to higher authorities (Romans 13:1). The Catechism says citizens owe "submission to authority" in just matters (CCC 2240).

The Catechism does not say that moderate drinking is a sin. It condemns abuse and drunkenness (CCC 2290).

The law sets a minimum age. A reasonable age law is a just law, and so there is a duty to obey it.

## Scripture

> "Let every soul be subject to higher powers: for there is no power but from God: and those that are, are ordained of God. Therefore he that resisteth the power, resisteth the ordinance of God. And they that resist, purchase to themselves damnation."
*Romans 13:1-2 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says all authority is from God. The Church applies this to just laws and does not say it covers unjust ones (CCC 2242).

## Why this matters today

Many teenagers drink and many parents quietly allow it. The Church's answer is temperance and respect for law, not a guess about what is tolerated.

Alcohol is also a real danger for young people. Poisoning, accidents and addiction are the worries behind the law.

## What's at stake

One night can end a life. A habit can run it.

## How to live it

- **Wait until you are legal.**
- **Never drive** after drinking or ride with someone who has.
- **Ask what you want** from the drink.
- **Find friends** who do not push it.
