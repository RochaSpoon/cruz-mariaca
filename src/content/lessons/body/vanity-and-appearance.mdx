## The short version

Caring how you look is fine. The Catechism says modesty "inspires one's choice of clothing" and resists "the allurements of fashion," and Peter says real adornment is of the heart (CCC 2522-2523; 1 Peter 3:3-4).

## What the Church teaches

"Modesty protects the mystery of persons and their love... Modesty is decency. It inspires one's choice of clothing" (CCC 2522).

"There is a modesty of the feelings as well as of the body." It "inspires a way of life which makes it possible to resist the allurements of fashion and the pressures of prevailing ideologies" (CCC 2523).

"Purity requires modesty, an integral part of temperance" (CCC 2521).

Peter says that adornment should not be only outward, the braiding of hair or the putting on of fine clothing, but the hidden person of the heart (1 Peter 3:3-4).

The Catechism's concern is not looking good. It is the cult of appearance (CCC 2289).

## Scripture

> "Whose adorning let it not be the outward plaiting of the hair, or the wearing of gold, or the putting on of apparel: But the hidden man of the heart in the incorruptibility of a quiet and a meek spirit, which is rich in the sight of God."
*1 Peter 3:3-4 (Douay-Rheims)*

Peter is writing to wives, and the principle applies to everyone.

## Why this matters today

Fashion, filters and fitness trends make appearance a full-time project for many people. The Catechism's answer is moderation and attention to the person behind the look (CCC 2523).

Looking nice, grooming and dressing well are not condemned. Making them the center of your life is.

## What's at stake

If you live for looks, your sense of self depends on other people's eyes.

## How to live it

- **Dress to fit the occasion,** and then stop thinking about it.
- **Limit time** on appearance apps and mirrors.
- **Compliment character** as often as looks.
- **Ask what you are hoping people will see.**
