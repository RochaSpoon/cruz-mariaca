## The short version

The Catechism lists the "abuse of... tobacco" under intemperance (CCC 2290). Vaping and nicotine addiction fall under the same virtue of temperance and care for health (CCC 2288).

## What the Church teaches

"The virtue of temperance disposes us to avoid every kind of excess: the abuse of food, alcohol, tobacco, or medicine" (CCC 2290).

"Life and physical health are precious gifts entrusted to us by God. We must take reasonable care of them" (CCC 2288).

Paul says that all things are lawful for him, but that he will not be brought under the power of any (1 Corinthians 6:12).

The Catechism treats the habit of something that has a hold on you as a matter of freedom, and says sin creates a proclivity to sin (CCC 1865).

Nicotine is addictive. This lesson does not go into medical details, and a doctor can help you quit.

## Scripture

> "All things are lawful to me, but all things are not expedient. All things are lawful to me, but I will not be brought under the power of any."
*1 Corinthians 6:12 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says he will not be brought under the power of anything. The Church uses this to talk about habits that take freedom away.

## Why this matters today

Vapes are designed to be easy to use and to hide. Many users are young.

If you want to stop and cannot, that is addiction, not a moral failing alone. Ask for help.

## What's at stake

A habit that you cannot stop is a chain.

## How to live it

- **Ask a doctor** about quitting aids.
- **Tell someone** you trust.
- **Pray** when the craving comes.
- **Replace the habit** with something that takes the hands and mouth.
