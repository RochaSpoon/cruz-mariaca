## The short version

Adultery is sex with someone to whom one of the partners is not married. The Catechism calls it "an injustice" that does injury "to the sign of the covenant which the marriage bond is" (CCC 2380-2381).

## What the Church teaches

"Adultery refers to marital infidelity. When two partners, of whom at least one is married to another party, have sexual relations - even transient ones - they commit adultery" (CCC 2380).

"Christ condemns even adultery of mere desire. The sixth commandment and the New Testament forbid adultery absolutely" (CCC 2380).

"Adultery is an injustice. He who commits adultery fails in his commitment. He does injury to the sign of the covenant which the marriage bond is, transgresses the rights of the other spouse, and undermines the institution of marriage" (CCC 2381).

It also "compromises the good of human generation and the welfare of children who need their parents' stable union" (CCC 2381).

## Scripture

> "Thou shalt not commit adultery."
*Exodus 20:14 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "You have heard that it was said to them of old: Thou shalt not commit adultery. But I say to you, that whosoever shall look on a woman to lust after her, hath already committed adultery with her in his heart."
*Matthew 5:27-28 (Douay-Rheims)*

The commandment is short. Jesus extends it to the heart.

## Why this matters today

Many people treat infidelity as a private matter between two adults. The Church says it wrongs the spouse, the children and the covenant itself.

The Church also teaches that sin is not the last word. Repentance and Confession are open to the unfaithful and to the betrayed who struggle with anger (CCC 1446).

## What's at stake

Adultery breaks a promise made before God. Its damage reaches spouses, children and families.

## How to live it

- **Cut off** the contact that is leading you there.
- **Tell** a priest or a trusted counselor early.
- **Go** to Confession.
- **If you were betrayed**, you are not at fault. Seek counseling and support.
