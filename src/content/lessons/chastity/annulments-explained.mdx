## The short version

An annulment is not a Catholic divorce. The Church declares, after a tribunal examines the case, "that the marriage never existed" (CCC 1629).

## What the Church teaches

"The Church holds the exchange of consent between the spouses to be the indispensable element that 'makes the marriage.' If consent is lacking there is no marriage" (CCC 1626).

"The consent must be an act of the will of each of the contracting parties, free of coercion or grave external fear... If this freedom is lacking the marriage is invalid" (CCC 1628).

"The Church, after an examination of the situation by the competent ecclesiastical tribunal, can declare the nullity of a marriage, i.e., that the marriage never existed" (CCC 1629).

In that case "the contracting parties are free to marry, provided the natural obligations of a previous union are discharged" (CCC 1629).

## Scripture

> "Therefore now they are not two, but one flesh. What therefore God hath joined together, let no man put asunder."
*Matthew 19:6 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus says what God has joined, man must not separate. The Church's claim in an annulment is that, in a particular case, God did not join the couple, because something essential was missing at the start.

## Why this matters today

Critics say annulments are divorce with a different name. The Church answers that a tribunal examines evidence about the moment of consent. It does not end a valid marriage.

An annulment does not make children illegitimate. It also does not say the relationship was meaningless.

## What's at stake

The question is whether a valid marriage existed. If one did, the Church holds it cannot be dissolved.

## How to live it

- **Talk** to your parish priest.
- **Ask** about the process and the tribunal in your diocese.
- **Be honest** in what you tell the tribunal.
- **Keep** praying and receiving the sacraments you are able to receive.
