## The short version

Custody of the eyes is a traditional practice. The Catechism says modesty "guides how one looks at others," and Job says he "made a covenant with my eyes" (CCC 2521; Job 31:1).

## What the Church teaches

"Purity requires modesty, an integral part of temperance. Modesty protects the intimate center of the person... It guides how one looks at others and behaves toward them in conformity with the dignity of persons and their solidarity" (CCC 2521).

"Purity of heart is the precondition of the vision of God. Even now it enables us to see according to God, to accept others as 'neighbors'; it lets us perceive the human body - ours and our neighbor's - as a temple of the Holy Spirit" (CCC 2519).

Jesus says anyone who looks at a woman lustfully has already committed adultery with her in his heart (Matthew 5:28).

Job says he made a covenant with his eyes not to look on a virgin (Job 31:1).

Jesus also says the eye is the lamp of the body (Matthew 6:22-23).

## Scripture

> "I made a covenant with my eyes, that I would not so much as think upon a virgin."
*Job 31:1 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "The light of thy body is thy eye. If thy eye be single, thy whole body shall be lightsome. But if thy eye be evil thy whole body shall be darksome. If then the light that is in thee, be darkness: the darkness itself how great shall it be!"
*Matthew 6:22-23 (Douay-Rheims)*

Both passages treat the eye as the gate of the heart.

## Why this matters today

Beaches, gyms and screens make looking constant. The practice is simple: a first glance is not a sin, and looking again on purpose is the problem (CCC 2847 distinguishes being tempted from consenting).

The goal is to see people, not bodies. That is what purity of heart is for (CCC 2519).

## What's at stake

What you look at trains what you want.

## How to live it

- **Look away** quickly and calmly.
- **Say** a short prayer.
- **Choose** places you can control.
- **See** a person.
