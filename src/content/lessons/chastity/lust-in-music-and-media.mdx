## The short version

What you consume shapes what you desire. The Catechism says modesty "protests... against the solicitations of certain media that go too far in the exhibition of intimate things" (CCC 2523), and Paul says to think on what is pure (Philippians 4:8).

## What the Church teaches

"There is a modesty of the feelings as well as of the body. It protests, for example, against the voyeuristic explorations of the human body in certain advertisements, or against the solicitations of certain media that go too far in the exhibition of intimate things" (CCC 2523).

Modesty "inspires a way of life which makes it possible to resist the allurements of fashion and the pressures of prevailing ideologies" (CCC 2523).

Paul says to think about whatever is true, honorable, just, pure, lovable and of good repute (Philippians 4:8).

The psalmist says he will set no unjust thing before his eyes (Psalm 100:3, Douay-Rheims numbering).

Vatican II asked viewers to choose media "outstanding for their moral goodness" (Inter Mirifica 9).

## Scripture

> "For the rest, brethren, whatsoever things are true, whatsoever modest, whatsoever just, whatsoever holy, whatsoever lovely, whatsoever of good fame, if there be any virtue, if any praise of discipline, think on these things."
*Philippians 4:8 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul gives a test for what to let into your mind.

## Why this matters today

Much popular music and many shows treat sex casually. You cannot avoid all of it. You can choose.

This is a matter of prudence. If a song or show leaves you restless, it is worth asking why.

## What's at stake

What you take in repeatedly becomes what you assume is normal.

## How to live it

- **Notice** what a song or show does to you.
- **Replace** a few things with better.
- **Talk** with a friend about it.
- **Do not** be self-righteous.
