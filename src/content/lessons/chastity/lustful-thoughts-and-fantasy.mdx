## The short version

You cannot stop thoughts from appearing, and you can refuse to entertain them. The Catechism says concupiscence "cannot harm those who do not consent" (CCC 1264), and distinguishes being tempted from consenting (CCC 2847).

## What the Church teaches

"Concupiscence is left for us to wrestle with, it cannot harm those who do not consent but manfully resist it by the grace of Jesus Christ" (CCC 1264).

"We must also discern between being tempted and consenting to temptation" (CCC 2847).

Jesus says that looking at a woman lustfully is adultery of the heart (Matthew 5:28).

Paul speaks of bringing every thought into obedience to Christ (2 Corinthians 10:5).

"Baptism confers on its recipient the grace of purification from all sins. But the baptized must continue to struggle against concupiscence" (CCC 2520).

## Scripture

> "And every height that exhalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every understanding unto the obedience of Christ;"
*2 Corinthians 10:5 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul talks about bringing every thought into captivity to obey Christ.

## Why this matters today

Many people feel guilty for thoughts that arrive unbidden. The Catechism says the pull is not the sin. Choosing to entertain it is.

If thoughts become compulsive or distressing, a counselor can help alongside a priest.

## What's at stake

If every thought is a sin, you will despair. If none matters, you will drift.

## How to live it

- **Notice** the thought and do not feed it.
- **Redirect** your mind to something else, including a short prayer.
- **Tell** someone you trust if it persists.
- **Go** to Confession.
