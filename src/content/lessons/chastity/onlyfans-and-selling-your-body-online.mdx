## The short version

The Church says selling sexual content falls under pornography and prostitution. The Catechism says prostitution does injury to the dignity of the person, "reducing the person to an instrument of sexual pleasure," and that those who pay or buy "sin gravely" (CCC 2354-2355).

## What the Church teaches

"Pornography... does grave injury to the dignity of its participants (actors, vendors, the public), since each one becomes an object of base pleasure and illicit profit for others. It is a grave offense" (CCC 2354).

"Prostitution does injury to the dignity of the person who engages in it, reducing the person to an instrument of sexual pleasure. The one who pays sins gravely against himself" (CCC 2355).

The Catechism says "the imputability of the offense can be attenuated by destitution, blackmail, or social pressure" (CCC 2355).

Paul says your body is a member of Christ (1 Corinthians 6:15).

The Church's concern is for the person selling as well as the buyer. Many who sell are poor, pressured or exploited.

## Scripture

Or know you not, that he who is joined to a harlot, is made one body? For they shall be, saith he, two in one flesh. But he who is joined to the Lord, is one spirit."
*1 Corinthians 6:15-17 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says the one joined to the Lord is one spirit with him.

## Why this matters today

The platform presents it as a business and as freedom and independence. The Church looks at what is being sold and who is being used.

If you are in it because of need, the Church's message is not contempt. It is that you are worth more, and help is available.

## What's at stake

You are not your content. A person is not a product.

## How to live it

- **If you are in it,** talk to someone you trust about a way out.
- **Do not buy** it.
- **Support** groups that help people leave.
- **Go** to Confession and begin again.
