## The short version

Watching pornography is grave matter. Done with full knowledge and consent, it's a mortal sin. It trains you to see people as objects, it gets worse over time, and the industry runs on exploitation. Many people struggle with it. Freedom is possible, but not on willpower alone. You need grace, a plan, and other people.

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism says pornography "offends against chastity because it perverts the conjugal act, the intimate giving of spouses to each other. It does grave injury to the dignity of its participants (actors, vendors, the public), since each one becomes an object of base pleasure and illicit profit for others." It calls it "a grave offense" and says civil authorities should prevent its production and distribution (CCC 2354).

Notice who gets hurt: not just the viewer. The people on screen, the people selling it, and you.

## The Fathers and saints

> "For of a perverse will was a lust made; and a lust served became custom; and custom not resisted became necessity."
*St. Augustine, Confessions VIII.5.10 (Pusey translation)*

That's addiction, described in the 4th century. A choice becomes a habit. A habit becomes a chain. Augustine broke the chain. So can you.

St. John Paul II taught that the opposite of love is using a person. Porn is pure using: no name, no relationship, no commitment, just a body on a screen for your pleasure (*Love and Responsibility*).

## Why this hits hard today

- **Kids see it early.** A 2023 Common Sense Media survey found most U.S. teens have seen pornography, with an average first exposure around age 12. [VERIFY figures]
- **It's built to hook you.** Free, endless, and always one click more extreme. Many users report needing more extreme content over time.
- **It's tied to abuse.** In 2020 the New York Times reported that Pornhub hosted videos of minors and of rape victims. Visa and Mastercard then cut off payments to the site. When you click, you can't know whether the person on screen consented, was trafficked, or was a minor.
- **It wrecks relationships.** Men and women who use porn often report less satisfaction with real partners, and many spouses experience it as betrayal.

## What's at stake

Your soul, first. Grave matter plus knowledge plus consent equals mortal sin, and Jesus says plainly that it's better to lose an eye than for your whole body to be thrown into hell (Matthew 5:29).

Your future second. Porn shapes what you think a man or woman is for. It will follow you into dating and marriage. Your future spouse is affected by what you do now.

The Church also teaches that "force of acquired habit," anxiety, and immaturity can lessen culpability (CCC 2352). That is not permission. It means you shouldn't despair, and you should keep fighting and confessing. It does not mean the act is okay.

## The plan

**1. Confess.** Go to Confession now, and go regularly. Weekly is not too often while you're fighting this. Find a priest you can return to.

**2. Tell someone.** Shame keeps it secret, and secrecy keeps it alive. Tell a priest, a parent, a mentor, or a trusted friend of the same sex who'll ask you about it every week.

**3. Make it hard to access.**
- Use accountability or filtering software (Covenant Eyes, Ever Accountable, Canopy) with someone else as your partner.
- No phone in the bedroom or bathroom. Buy a cheap alarm clock.
- Put screen time limits and content restrictions on your devices, and let someone else hold the passcode.
- Delete apps that are triggers. That includes social media if that's where it starts.

**4. Know your triggers.** HALT: hungry, angry, lonely, tired. Late nights. Being alone. Boredom. Stress. Write down your last three falls: when, where, and how you felt. You'll see the pattern.

**5. Have a plan for the moment.** When the temptation hits: get up, leave the room, go where people are, do pushups, call someone, pray a Hail Mary out loud. Don't argue with the temptation. Run (1 Corinthians 6:18).

**6. Replace it.** Sleep, exercise, real friendships, work, hobbies with your hands. You can't just remove something. You have to fill the space.

**7. Pray daily.** A Rosary, a decade, or the St. Michael prayer. Ask St. Joseph and Mary for purity.

**8. Get help if it's an addiction.** If you've tried and keep failing, get professional help. Catholic resources include Integrity Restored and Strive21. A Catholic counselor who specializes in compulsive sexual behavior can help a lot.

## If you've fallen again

Don't wait. Go back to Confession. The devil's line is "You've done it again, you're hopeless, why bother." It's a lie. Every time you get up, the chain gets weaker. Count your days, but don't make your streak your god. Make Christ your God.
