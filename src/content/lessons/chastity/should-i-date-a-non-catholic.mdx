## The short version

The Church allows it. The Catechism says "difference of confession between the spouses does not constitute an insurmountable obstacle for marriage," but also says "the difficulties of mixed marriages must not be underestimated" (CCC 1634).

## What the Church teaches

"A case of marriage with disparity of cult (between a Catholic and a nonbaptized person) requires even greater circumspection" (CCC 1633).

"Difference of confession between the spouses does not constitute an insurmountable obstacle for marriage, when they succeed in placing in common what they have received from their respective communities" (CCC 1634).

"Differences about faith and the very notion of marriage, but also different religious mentalities, can become sources of tension in marriage, especially as regards the education of children" (CCC 1634).

A mixed marriage needs "the express permission of ecclesiastical authority," and a marriage with a non-baptized person needs a dispensation for validity (CCC 1635).

The Catholic party's obligations include baptism and education of children in the Catholic Church (CCC 1635).

## Scripture

> "Bear not the yoke with unbelievers. For what participation hath justice with injustice? Or what fellowship hath light with darkness?"
*2 Corinthians 6:14 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says not to bear the yoke with unbelievers. Interpreters read this in different ways and the Church does not use it as a simple rule against dating.

## Why this matters today

Dating someone is not marrying them. The Church's concern is where it leads.

The Catechism also says a Catholic spouse can be a witness. It describes "the free conversion of the other spouse" as a joy (CCC 1637).

## What's at stake

You will share a life, a home and children. If faith matters to you, ask early how it will fit.

## How to live it

- **Talk** about faith, Mass and children early.
- **Be honest** about what you would not compromise.
- **Talk** to a priest before an engagement.
- **Pray** about it.
