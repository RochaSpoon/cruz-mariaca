## The short version

The Church teaches that direct sterilization is not a morally acceptable means of regulating births. Paul VI wrote that it is "equally to be condemned," "whether of the man or of the woman, whether permanent or temporary" (Humanae Vitae 14; CCC 2399).

## What the Church teaches

"Equally to be condemned, as the magisterium of the Church has affirmed on many occasions, is direct sterilization, whether of the man or of the woman, whether permanent or temporary" (Humanae Vitae 14).

"Legitimate intentions on the part of the spouses do not justify recourse to morally unacceptable means (for example, direct sterilization or contraception)" (CCC 2399).

The Catechism teaches that every action that, "as an end or as a means," proposes to "render procreation impossible" is intrinsically evil (CCC 2370).

The teaching concerns direct sterilization. It is not about surgery that treats a disease and has sterility only as a side effect. Ask a priest or a Catholic medical ethicist about a particular case.

## Why this matters today

Many Catholics have had these procedures, often under medical or social pressure, and did not know the Church's teaching. This is a matter for Confession, not for despair.

Some couples ask about reversal. Talk to a priest and a doctor you trust.

## What's at stake

The Church says the question is not only the goal of spacing births but what the act itself does.

## How to live it

- **Learn** about NFP before choosing a procedure.
- **Talk** to a priest if you have already had one.
- **Go** to Confession.
- **Ask** a Catholic doctor about options that treat a disease.
