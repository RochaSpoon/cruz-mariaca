## The short version

Telling a parent is hard and often helps. Scripture tells believers to confess their sins to one another (James 5:16), and the Catechism says parents have the first responsibility for the education of their children (CCC 2223).

## What the Church teaches

"Parents have the first responsibility for the education of their children" (CCC 2223).

"By knowing how to acknowledge their own failings to their children, parents will be better able to guide and correct them" (CCC 2223).

Parents "should teach their children to subordinate the 'material and instinctual dimensions to interior and spiritual ones'" (CCC 2223).

Confession to a priest is the sacramental path. Talking to a parent is a human path that can sit beside it (CCC 1446).

## Scripture

> "Confess therefore your sins one to another: and pray one for another, that you may be saved. For the continual prayer of a just man availeth much."
*James 5:16 (Douay-Rheims)*

James says to confess your sins to one another and pray for one another that you may be saved.

## Why this matters today

Fear of punishment keeps many teens silent. Not every parent will react well, and if home is unsafe, talk to a priest, school counselor or another trusted adult.

If you are a parent whose child told you, the first thing to say is thank you for telling me.

## What's at stake

Shame keeps a habit alive. Telling one safe person weakens it.

## How to live it

- **Choose** a calm time.
- **Start** by saying you need help with something.
- **Go** to Confession.
- **Ask** for filters and a plan, not only a lecture.
