## The short version

The Church asks whether a drug acts after conception. Dignitas Personae (2008) says that anyone who seeks to prevent implantation of an embryo that may have been conceived "generally intends abortion" (Dignitas Personae 23; CCC 2270-2271).

## What the Church teaches

"Human life must be respected and protected absolutely from the moment of conception" (CCC 2270).

"Direct abortion, that is to say, abortion willed either as an end or a means, is gravely contrary to the moral law" (CCC 2271).

Dignitas Personae divides post-intercourse methods into those that "interfere with the embryo before implantation" (interceptive) and those that "cause the elimination of the embryo once implanted" (contragestative) (Dignitas Personae 23).

It says that "scientific studies indicate that the effect of inhibiting implantation is certainly present, even if this does not mean that such interceptives cause an abortion every time they are used" (Dignitas Personae 23).

It concludes that "anyone who seeks to prevent the implantation of an embryo which may possibly have been conceived and who therefore either requests or prescribes such a pharmaceutical, generally intends abortion" (Dignitas Personae 23).

## Why this matters today

The science of how particular pills work is debated and changes. The Church's principle is that you may not act to destroy a possible embryo, and the intention matters.

Cases after a sexual assault raise separate questions. Talk to a priest or a Catholic hospital ethicist.

## What's at stake

If a new life may exist, the Church asks for the caution one would take with any human life.

## How to live it

- **Do not** panic. If you took one, Confession is open to you.
- **Ask** your doctor about how a specific drug works.
- **Learn** NFP.
- **Get** support if you are in a crisis.
