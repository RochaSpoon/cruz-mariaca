## The short version

Catholics believe God created everything from nothing, and the Church allows the study of evolution for the human body, provided the soul is believed to be created directly by God (CCC 366, 338; Humani Generis 36).

## What the Church teaches

"Nothing exists that does not owe its existence to God the Creator." The world began "when God's word drew it out of nothingness" (CCC 338).

The Catechism welcomes the sciences: they "have splendidly enriched our knowledge of the age and dimensions of the cosmos, the development of life-forms and the appearance of man," and invite "even greater admiration for the greatness of the Creator" (CCC 283).

It says the deeper question is one science cannot answer: the meaning of the origin, and whether the universe is governed by chance or by a Creator (CCC 284).

Scripture presents creation "symbolically as a succession of six days." The sacred text "teaches the truths revealed by God for our salvation" (CCC 337).

In 1950, Pius XII wrote that the Church "does not forbid" research and discussion on evolution "as far as it inquires into the origin of the human body as coming from pre-existent and living matter." He added a condition: "the Catholic faith obliges us to hold that souls are immediately created by God" (Humani Generis 36). The Catechism repeats this about the soul (CCC 366).

Pius XII also asked that both sides of the question be weighed "with the necessary seriousness, moderation and measure," and that all be ready to submit to the Church's judgment (Humani Generis 36).

## Scripture

> "In the beginning God created heaven, and earth. And the earth was void and empty, and darkness was upon the face of the deep; and the spirit of God moved over the waters. And God said: Be light made. And light was made."
*Genesis 1:1-3 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And God saw all the things that he had made, and they were very good. And the evening and morning were the sixth day."
*Genesis 1:31 (Douay-Rheims)*

Genesis 1 says over and over that God saw what he made and that it was good. The Catechism says the sacred text presents creation symbolically, in six days of work and a day of rest (CCC 337).

## Why this matters today

The loud version of this argument sets "science" against "faith." The Catechism says faith and science do not truly contradict (CCC 159), and that Catholics need not choose a young Earth.

Young-earth Christians read Genesis literally. Catholics can respect the sincerity of that view, but the Catechism treats the six days as symbolic (CCC 337). Neither side is stupid.

## What's at stake

If you are told you must pick between God and science, you may give up one of them. The Church says you do not have to.

## How to live it

- **Read Genesis 1 for what it says about God,** before you read it as a timeline.
- **Learn what the Church requires:** a Creator who made everything, and a soul made directly by him.
- **Do not use evolution as a weapon** against believers or against science.
- **Ask the question science cannot:** why is there anything at all?
