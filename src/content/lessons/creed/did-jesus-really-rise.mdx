## The short version

The Church says the Resurrection is "a real event" that was "historically verified" and the center of the faith (CCC 638-639). Paul said in about A.D. 56 that if it did not happen, the faith is in vain (CCC 651).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism calls the Resurrection "the crowning truth of our faith in Christ" (CCC 638) and "a real event, with manifestations that were historically verified" (CCC 639).

It points to Paul, who in about A.D. 56 wrote to the Corinthians that he had received, and handed on, that Christ died, was buried, and was raised on the third day (CCC 639; 1 Corinthians 15:3-8).

The Catechism is honest about what the evidence does. The empty tomb "is not a direct proof," since the body's absence "could be explained otherwise" (CCC 640). The appearances carry the weight. Women were the first witnesses, then Peter, then the Twelve (CCC 641).

Nobody saw the Resurrection itself. "No one was an eyewitness" and "no evangelist describes it" (CCC 647). The Church says what was seen was the risen Jesus.

The Catechism also notes that the disciples were doubtful. They thought they were seeing a ghost, and "some doubted." It concludes that the idea that the Resurrection was produced by their faith "is untenable" (CCC 644).

## Scripture

> "For I delivered unto you first of all, which I also received: how that Christ died for our sins, according to the scriptures: And that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day, according to the scriptures: And that he was seen by Cephas; and after that by the eleven. Then he was seen by more than five hundred brethren at once: of whom many remain until this present, and some are fallen asleep. After that, he was seen by James, then by all the apostles. And last of all, he was seen also by me, as by one born out of due time."
*1 Corinthians 15:3-8 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "Now whilst they were speaking these things, Jesus stood in the midst of them, and saith to them: Peace be to you; it is I, fear not. But they being troubled and frightened, supposed that they saw a spirit. And he said to them: Why are you troubled, and why do thoughts arise in your hearts? See my hands and feet, that it is I myself; handle, and see: for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as you see me to have. And when he had said this, he shewed them his hands and feet. But while they yet believed not, and wondered for joy, he said: Have you any thing to eat? And they offered him a piece of a broiled fish, and a honeycomb. And when he had eaten before them, taking the remains, he gave to them."
*Luke 24:36-43 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says he is handing on what he himself received, and the Catechism dates his letter to about A.D. 56 (CCC 639).

## Why this matters today

The usual alternatives are that the disciples hallucinated, that they made it up, or that it was a myth that grew. The Catechism answers the first and the last directly, by pointing to how stunned and doubtful the disciples were (CCC 643-644).

Many modern Christians treat the Resurrection as a symbol. The Church says it is "an historical fact" and that "it is impossible not to acknowledge it" as one (CCC 643).

## What's at stake

Paul said it plainly: "If Christ has not been raised, then our preaching is in vain and your faith is in vain" (CCC 651; 1 Corinthians 15:14). The Church stakes everything on this one event.

## How to live it

- **Read 1 Corinthians 15 in one sitting.** It is the oldest written summary of the faith.
- **Weigh the options honestly.** If it did not happen, you need a better explanation than a hallucination.
- **Talk to someone who is not sure.** Doubt is not a sin. Even the Twelve had it (CCC 644).
- **Go to an Easter Vigil Mass.** It is the Church's longest night of prayer.
