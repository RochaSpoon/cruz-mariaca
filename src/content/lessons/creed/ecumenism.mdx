## The short version

The Catholic Church says Christians are divided and that the division is a wound. She accepts other baptized Christians "with respect and affection as brothers" and prays for unity (CCC 817-822).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism admits that "rifts" began early and that "much more serious dissensions" followed. It says "men of both sides were to blame" (CCC 817).

"One cannot charge with the sin of the separation those who at present are born into these communities." The Catholic Church "accepts them with respect and affection as brothers" (CCC 818).

"Many elements of sanctification and of truth" exist outside the Catholic Church, including "the written Word of God; the life of grace; faith, hope, and charity" (CCC 819).

Unity "subsists in the Catholic Church as something she can never lose," and the Church must "pray and work" for it to grow (CCC 820).

Unity requires "conversion of heart," prayer, and mutual knowledge. It "transcends human powers and gifts," so the Church places her hope in "the prayer of Christ for the Church" (CCC 821-822).

## Scripture

> "And not for them only do I pray, but for them also who through their word shall believe in me; That they all may be one, as thou, Father, in me, and I in thee; that they also may be one in us; that the world may believe that thou hast sent me. And the glory which thou hast given me, I have given to them; that they may be one, as we also are one: I in them, and thou in me; that they may be made perfect in one: and the world may know that thou hast sent me, and hast loved them, as thou hast also loved me."
*John 17:20-23 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus prays that his followers "may be one." Every Christian tradition takes this seriously, and they disagree about what the unity requires.

## Why this matters today

Many people are tired of Christian divisions. The Catechism does not play them down. It says that rifts began early and that men of both sides were to blame (CCC 817).

At the same time, the Catholic claim that unity "subsists" in the Catholic Church is one that the Orthodox and Protestants do not accept. Honest ecumenism does not hide that.

## What's at stake

If unity is just friendliness, the doctrinal differences do not matter. If unity is truth, the differences matter a great deal and must be worked through with patience.

## How to live it

- **Pray with Christians from another tradition** where it is allowed.
- **Learn what Orthodox and Protestant Christians actually believe** from their own writers.
- **Do not water down** what you believe to make a friend feel at ease. Say it plainly and kindly.
- **Pray for unity** with the words of John 17.
