## The short version

The Church reads Scripture literally first, for what the human authors meant, and then spiritually, in the light of Christ and the whole Church (CCC 109-119).

## What the Church teaches

God speaks "in a human way." The reader must be attentive to "what the human authors truly wanted to affirm, and to what God wanted to reveal to us by their words" (CCC 109).

That means paying attention to "the conditions of their time and culture, the literary genres in use at that time" (CCC 110; Dei Verbum 12).

Three further rules apply. Read the text with "the content and unity of the whole Scripture," read within "the living Tradition of the whole Church," and be attentive to the "analogy of faith," the coherence of the truths of faith (CCC 112-114).

There are two main senses of Scripture, the literal and the spiritual. The spiritual sense has three parts: allegorical, moral and anagogical (CCC 115).

The literal sense is the foundation: "All other senses of Sacred Scripture are based on the literal" (CCC 116). The spiritual sense recognizes how events point to Christ, such as the crossing of the Red Sea (CCC 117).

Finally, all of this is "ultimately subject to the judgement of the Church" (CCC 119).

## Scripture

> "And he said to them: These are the words which I spoke to you, while I was yet with you, that all things must needs be fulfilled, which are written in the law of Moses, and in the prophets, and in the psalms, concerning me. Then he opened their understanding, that they might understand the scriptures."
*Luke 24:44-45 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "For I would not have you ignorant, brethren, that our fathers were all under the cloud, and all passed through the sea. And all in Moses were baptized, in the cloud, and in the sea: And did all eat the same spiritual food, And all drank the same spiritual drink; (and they drank of the spiritual rock that followed them, and the rock was Christ.)"
*1 Corinthians 10:1-4 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says the rock in the desert "was Christ." Catholics call this kind of reading typology. Most Christians read the Old Testament in light of Christ.

## Why this matters today

Literalists read everything at face value, and skeptics treat the Bible as a collection of old myths. The Church says neither works. Literary form matters (CCC 110).

Protestants and Catholics share much on the first rules. They differ mainly on who has the last word on interpretation (CCC 119).

## What's at stake

If you read the Bible with no rules, you can make it say anything. If you read it with only one rule, you miss most of what it says.

## How to live it

- **Ask what kind of writing it is** before you ask what it means.
- **Read a passage in its context** before you quote one verse.
- **Read the Old Testament with the New.** Try Luke 24 and 1 Corinthians 10.
- **Compare a Catholic and a Protestant commentary** on the same chapter.
