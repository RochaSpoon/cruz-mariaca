## The short version

The Church says private revelations do not belong to the deposit of faith. Catholics are free to accept an approved apparition or to leave it (CCC 67).

## What the Church teaches

"Throughout the ages, there have been so-called 'private' revelations, some of which have been recognized by the authority of the Church. They do not belong, however, to the deposit of faith" (CCC 67).

Their role is not "to improve or complete Christ's definitive Revelation, but to help live more fully by it in a certain period of history" (CCC 67).

The Church judges them. In 2024 the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith issued norms with six possible final decisions. The usual verdict is a "Nihil obstat," which means there is nothing against it and the faithful may follow it prudently.

A Nihil obstat does not declare the event supernatural. Under the Norms, the faithful "are not obliged to give an assent of faith" to the phenomenon.

Guadalupe, Lourdes and Fatima are among the best-known apparitions that the Church has recognized.

## Scripture

> "Dearly beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits if they be of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world."
*1 John 4:1 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "God, who, at sundry times and in divers manners, spoke in times past to the fathers by the prophets, last of all, In these days hath spoken to us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the world."
*Hebrews 1:1-2 (Douay-Rheims)*

Hebrews 1:1-2 says God has now spoken to us by his Son. The Catechism treats Christ's Revelation as definitive, so that nothing needs to be added (CCC 67).

## Why this matters today

A lot of people treat apparitions as the heart of Catholicism. The Church says they are help, not the faith itself, and that using them "is not obligatory."

Skeptics point out that some alleged apparitions have been hoaxes or mistaken. The Church agrees and has a process for judging them, which includes saying no.

## What's at stake

If an apparition replaced Christ's Revelation, it would be a different religion. The Church's rule keeps it in its place.

## How to live it

- **Check who has judged an apparition,** and what the bishop or the Dicastery said.
- **Stay with the Gospel first.** Everything private is secondary.
- **You can skip them.** A Catholic can ignore every apparition and be fully Catholic.
- **Be wary of messages** that predict dates or demand money.
