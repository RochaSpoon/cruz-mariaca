## The short version

Catholics honor Mary as the Church's Mother and model. They say her role depends on Christ and does not replace him (CCC 963-970).

## What the Church teaches

Mary is "truly the Mother of God and of the redeemer," and "clearly the mother of the members of Christ." The Catechism says she "cooperated by her obedience, faith, hope, and burning charity in the Savior's work" (CCC 963, 968).

She is a mother "in the order of grace." That motherhood goes back to the Annunciation and the cross and "continues uninterruptedly" (CCC 968-969).

The Catechism puts a clear limit on this: "Mary's function as mother of men in no way obscures or diminishes this unique mediation of Christ, but rather shows its power." Her influence "flows forth from the superabundance of the merits of Christ" (CCC 970).

Mary is "the Church's model of faith and charity" and the "exemplary realization" of the Church (CCC 967).

"The Church's devotion to the Blessed Virgin is intrinsic to Christian worship" (CCC 971).

## Scripture

> "When Jesus therefore had seen his mother and the disciple standing whom he loved, he saith to his mother: Woman, behold thy son. After that, he saith to the disciple: Behold thy mother. And from that hour, the disciple took her to his own."
*John 19:26-27 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And Mary said: My soul doth magnify the Lord. And my spirit hath rejoiced in God my Saviour. Because he hath regarded the humility of his handmaid; for behold from henceforth all generations shall call me blessed. Because he that is mighty, hath done great things to me; and holy is his name."
*Luke 1:46-49 (Douay-Rheims)*

Many Protestants admire Mary as a model of faith and still object to praying to her. The Catechism asks believers to honor, not worship, her (CCC 971).

## Why this matters today

The usual accusation is that Catholics worship Mary. The Catechism says her power draws "all its power" from Christ and that "no creature could ever be counted along with the Incarnate Word and Redeemer" (CCC 970).

A kinder Protestant worry is that Mary can crowd out Christ in practice. That is a real risk, and the Church's own teaching tells her people to keep her beneath him.

## What's at stake

If Mary replaces Christ, it is idolatry. If she is simply forgotten, the Church ignores what Jesus did from the cross. The Catechism claims she points to him.

## How to live it

- **Pray the Magnificat** (Luke 1:46-55). It is her own prayer, and it is about God.
- **Ask how each practice leads to Christ.** If it does not, set it aside.
- **Read John 19:26-27** and decide what you think Jesus is doing.
- **Learn the difference between honor and worship.** The next lessons spell it out.
