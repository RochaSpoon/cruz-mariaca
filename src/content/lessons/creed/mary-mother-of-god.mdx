## The short version

Catholics call Mary the Mother of God because the child she bore was God the Son. The title is about who Jesus is, not about where God came from (CCC 495).

## What the Church teaches

The Gospels call Mary "the mother of Jesus," and Elizabeth, "at the prompting of the Spirit," calls her "the mother of my Lord" before Jesus is born (CCC 495).

The Catechism's reasoning is short. The one she conceived "was none other than the Father's eternal Son, the second person of the Holy Trinity." So she is rightly called the Mother of God (CCC 495).

The title was defended by the third ecumenical council, at Ephesus in 431, against the view that Christ was "a human person joined to the divine person of God's Son." The Council confessed that the Word "became man" in the flesh Mary gave him (CCC 466).

Mary did not create God or come before him. She is Mother of God in the sense that Jesus is one Person, and that Person is divine.

The Eastern churches use the Greek word Theotokos, "God-bearer," for the same reason.

## Scripture

> "And it came to pass, that when Elizabeth heard the salutation of Mary, the infant leaped in her womb. And Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Ghost: And she cried out with a loud voice, and said: Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb. And whence is this to me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?"
*Luke 1:41-43 (Douay-Rheims)*

Elizabeth is moved by the Spirit when she says this, before Jesus is born (CCC 495).

## Why this matters today

The usual objection is that Mary can only be the mother of Jesus' human nature. The Church replies that the child Mary bore is one Person, the eternal Son of God (CCC 466, 495).

Some Christians hesitate over the phrase because it can sound as if Mary were divine. The title is about who Christ is, not about Mary's own nature (CCC 495).

## What's at stake

The title protects the faith about Jesus. If Mary is only the mother of a man, then the man and the Son of God are two persons, which is the Nestorian error the Council rejected (CCC 466).

## How to fight it

- **Do not argue the title as being about Mary.** Argue it as being about Jesus.
- **Read Luke 1:39-45** and notice what Elizabeth says.
- **Remember that the Orthodox use the same title.** The two Churches share it.
- **Ask what you mean by "mother."** The answer changes the argument.
