## The short version

The Creed names four marks of the Church: one, holy, catholic and apostolic. The Church says she has them from Christ, not from herself (CCC 811).

## What the Church teaches

"This is the sole Church of Christ, which in the Creed we profess to be one, holy, catholic and apostolic." The Church does not possess these marks "of herself." Christ makes her so through the Holy Spirit (CCC 811).

**One.** She acknowledges one Lord, one faith, one Baptism (CCC 866). The visible bonds of unity are one faith received from the Apostles, common worship, and apostolic succession (CCC 815). She has great diversity inside that unity (CCC 814).

**Holy.** God is her author, and Christ gave himself to make her holy. She includes sinners, so she is "the sinless one made up of sinners." Her holiness shines in the saints (CCC 867).

**Catholic.** The word means "universal," or "according to the totality." The Church is catholic because Christ is present in her and because she is sent to all people (CCC 830, 868).

**Apostolic.** She is built on the apostles, keeps and hands on their teaching, and is guided by their successors, the bishops (CCC 857, 869).

## Scripture

> "One body and one Spirit; as you are called in one hope of your calling. One Lord, one faith, one baptism. One God and Father of all, who is above all, and through all, and in us all."
*Ephesians 4:4-6 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And the wall of the city had twelve foundations, and in them, the twelve names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb."
*Apocalypse 21:14 (Douay-Rheims)*

Many Christian churches say the Nicene Creed, including the words "one, holy, catholic and apostolic." They disagree about what these words require.

## Why this matters today

The Church is divided into many denominations, and Catholics say she is one. The Catechism does not deny the divisions. It says the Church subsists in the Catholic Church and also that "many elements of sanctification and of truth" exist outside her (CCC 870).

Many Protestants understand "catholic" in the lower-case sense of "universal" and apply it to all believers. That is close to what the word means in the Catechism (CCC 830).

## What's at stake

If these four marks are only ideals, there is no way to tell the Church from any other group. If they are real, then you can look for them.

## How to live it

- **Check each mark yourself.** Where is the one faith, the holy life, the universal reach, the link back to the apostles?
- **Hold the sinners and the saints together.** Both are in the Church (CCC 867).
- **Read the Creed slowly** and notice how much of it is about the Church.
- **Talk to a Christian from another tradition** about which mark matters most to them.
