## The short version

The Church says other religions contain real goodness and truth, and also that she must proclaim Christ. She regards Jews and Muslims in a particular way (CCC 839-845).

## What the Church teaches

"Those who have not yet received the Gospel are related to the People of God in various ways" (CCC 839).

The Church's link to the Jewish people is special. They are "the first to hear the Word of God," and the Old Covenant "has never been revoked" (CCC 839, 121).

For Muslims, the Church says "the plan of salvation also includes those who acknowledge the Creator, in the first place amongst whom are the Muslims; these profess to hold the faith of Abraham, and together with us they adore the one, merciful God" (CCC 841). The Council also said the Church "regards with esteem" Muslims (Nostra Aetate 3).

The Church recognizes in other religions the search "among shadows and images" for God, and considers all the goodness and truth found there "a preparation for the Gospel" (CCC 843).

The Church also says religions show "limits and errors that disfigure the image of God in them" (CCC 844). She "decries hatred, persecutions, displays of anti-Semitism, directed against Jews at any time and by anyone" (Nostra Aetate 4).

## Scripture

> "But Paul standing in the midst of the Areopagus, said: Ye men of Athens, I perceive that in all things you are too superstitious. For passing by, and seeing your idols, I found an altar also, on which was written: To the unknown God. What therefore you worship, without knowing it, that I preach to you: God, who made the world, and all things therein; he, being Lord of heaven and earth, dwelleth not in temples made with hands; Neither is he served with men's hands, as though he needed any thing; seeing it is he who giveth to all life, and breath, and all things: And hath made of one, all mankind, to dwell upon the whole face of the earth, determining appointed times, and the limits of their habitation. That they should seek God, if happily they may feel after him or find him, although he be not far from every one of us: For in him we live, and move, and are; as some also of your own poets said: For we are also his offspring."
*Acts 17:22-28 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "For the gifts and the calling of God are without repentance."
*Romans 11:29 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul in Athens begins with what the Athenians already half-know, and then proclaims Christ. The Church uses this as a model.

## Why this matters today

Two simple views are common. One says all religions are the same. The other says all other religions are false and bad. The Church rejects both (CCC 843-844).

A Muslim, a Jew or a Hindu will notice if you describe their faith unfairly. The Church asks Catholics to treat the faiths of others with esteem, as the Council did for Muslims (Nostra Aetate 3).

## What's at stake

If all religions are the same, then Christ's claims mean nothing. If the others are worthless, then you cannot talk with anyone. The Church tries to hold both truth and respect.

## How to live it

- **Ask a friend of another faith what they believe,** and listen to the answer.
- **Read Nostra Aetate.** It is short.
- **State Christian claims plainly,** and also say what you admire.
- **Never mock a religion** you have not studied.
