## The short version

Infallibility does not mean the Pope is always right. It means that when he defines a doctrine of faith or morals for the whole Church, it will not be false (CCC 891).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism says Christ "willed to confer on her a share in his own infallibility" to keep the faith pure (CCC 889).

The Pope "enjoys this infallibility in virtue of his office" when, "as supreme pastor and teacher of all the faithful," he "proclaims by a definitive act a doctrine pertaining to faith or morals" (CCC 891).

Infallibility is also present in the body of bishops when they exercise supreme teaching together with the Pope, especially in an ecumenical council (CCC 891).

That is a narrow claim. The Catechism ties it to "a definitive act" proclaiming "a doctrine pertaining to faith or morals" (CCC 891). A passing remark, a sermon or a political opinion is not such an act.

Other papal teaching still calls for respect. When the bishops, "without arriving at an infallible definition," teach in the ordinary Magisterium, the Church asks for a religious assent (CCC 892).

## Scripture

> "And the Lord said: Simon, Simon, behold Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat: But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and thou, being once converted, confirm thy brethren."
*Luke 22:31-32 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "But when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will teach you all truth. For he shall not speak of himself; but what things soever he shall hear, he shall speak; and the things that are to come, he shall shew you."
*John 16:13 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Catechism ties infallibility to Christ's promise to Peter and to the Spirit who guides the Church into truth. Others read these verses as limited to the apostles.

## The Fathers and saints

The First Vatican Council defined papal infallibility in 1870 in the constitution Pastor Aeternus. The Catechism's wording in 891 quotes the Second Vatican Council, Lumen Gentium 25.

## Why this matters today

The usual objection is that popes in history made terrible decisions, so infallibility must be false. That is a different claim. The Church says nothing about popes being sinless or wise, only that definitions of doctrine will not be false.

Another objection is that the doctrine is a power grab. Critics who say so deserve a hearing, and the Catechism's reply is that the gift serves the faithful and is aimed at keeping the faith pure (CCC 889-890).

## What's at stake

If a pope could define false doctrine, no teaching would be secure. If everything a pope says is infallible, the Church would be a dictatorship. The Church claims one narrow thing and nothing wider.

## How to live it

- **Ask which kind of statement it is** before you decide how much weight to give it.
- **Do not use infallibility to end an argument** about a pope's opinion on economics or war.
- **Read the myth page** on this site for the strongest objections and the replies.
- **Check the conditions in CCC 891:** the Pope speaks as supreme teacher, on faith or morals, by a definitive act.
