## The short version

A relic is the body of a saint or something that touched one. The Church venerates relics as part of popular piety, and says nothing in them works by magic (CCC 1674).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism lists "the veneration of relics" among the forms of popular piety around the Church's sacramental life, along with pilgrimages, processions and the rosary (CCC 1674).

It describes these devotions as the religious sense of the people finding expression. They are not sacraments.

The Bible has examples. A dead man was revived when he touched the bones of Elisha (4 Kings 13:21), and cloths that had touched Paul healed the sick (Acts 19:11-12).

The Church's reasoning about images is similar: honor given to an image "passes to its prototype" (CCC 2132).

## Scripture

> "And Eliseus died, and they buried him. And the rovers from Moab came into the land the same year. And some that were burying a man, saw the rovers, and cast the body into the sepulchre of Eliseus. And when it had touched the bones of Eliseus, the man came to life, and stood upon his feet."
*4 Kings 13:20-21 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And God wrought by the hand of Paul more than common miracles. So that even there were brought from his body to the sick, handkerchiefs and aprons, and the diseases departed from them, and the wicked spirits went out of them."
*Acts 19:11-12 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Douay-Rheims calls what most Bibles name 2 Kings "4 Kings." The two Bible traditions number the books differently.

## Why this matters today

Relics strike many people as morbid or superstitious. The Catechism treats them as one of the forms of popular piety, not as a requirement (CCC 1674).

The Church condemns the buying and selling of holy things as simony (CCC 2121), and canon law says "it is absolutely forbidden to sell sacred relics" (can. 1190 §1).

## What's at stake

If the body does not matter, relics are silly. If the body is destined for resurrection, as the Church teaches, then a saint's body is worth honoring.

## How to live it

- **Visit a church that displays a relic** and see how it is treated.
- **Do not buy relics.** Canon law forbids selling them (can. 1190 §1).
- **Read the two Bible passages** and decide what you think they show.
- **Ask what you believe about the body.** The Church takes it seriously (see the resurrection of the body).
