## The short version

Catholics do not hold to the Bible alone. They hold that Scripture and Tradition flow "from the same divine well-spring" and must be honored with equal reverence (CCC 80, 82).

## What the Church teaches

God wants "all men to be saved." The Gospel was handed on in two ways: orally, by the apostles' preaching and example, and in writing (CCC 74-76).

Tradition is this "living transmission, accomplished in the Holy Spirit," and the Church "perpetuates and transmits to every generation all that she herself is, all that she believes" (CCC 78).

The Catechism says Scripture and Tradition are "flowing out from the same divine well-spring" and come together "to form one thing" (CCC 80). Scripture is "the speech of God as it is put down in writing" (CCC 81).

Because of that, the Church "does not derive her certainty about all revealed truths from the holy Scriptures alone." Both "must be accepted and honoured with equal sentiments of devotion and reverence" (CCC 82).

The first Christians had no New Testament. The Catechism says the New Testament itself "demonstrates the process of living Tradition" (CCC 83). The Church also says Tradition is how she knew which books belong in the Bible (CCC 120).

## Scripture

> "Therefore, brethren, stand fast; and hold the traditions which you have learned, whether by word, or by our epistle."
*2 Thessalonians 2:14 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "All scripture, inspired of God, is profitable to teach, to reprove, to correct, to instruct in justice, That the man of God may be perfect, furnished to every good work."
*2 Timothy 3:16-17 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "But if I tarry long, that thou mayest know how thou oughtest to behave thyself in the house of God, which is the church of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth."
*1 Timothy 3:15 (Douay-Rheims)*

Protestants point to 2 Timothy 3:16-17 as showing the sufficiency of Scripture. Catholics read the same verses and note that Paul also tells the Thessalonians to hold to what was passed on "whether by word, or by our epistle." The disagreement is about what these passages say, and both sides cite them.

## Why this matters today

The Protestant principle of sola scriptura, Scripture alone, has real strengths. It takes the Bible seriously and puts every church under it. Some Protestants who hold it also value the creeds and the early councils.

The Catholic reply is that the Bible does not list its own books, and that the apostles taught by word as well. The Catechism also insists that Tradition is the apostles' deposit handed on to the whole Church (CCC 83-84).

## What's at stake

If Scripture alone is enough, the question is who decides what it means. If Tradition and a teaching office are needed, the question is how to know they have not drifted. Each side has to answer one hard question.

## How to live it

- **Read the Bible and ask who first told you what it means.** Everyone learned from someone.
- **Try to state the other side fairly** before you answer it.
- **Look up the early Fathers** on a doctrine you doubt and see what the first centuries believed.
- **Keep both** in your hands: the text, and the community that handed it on.
