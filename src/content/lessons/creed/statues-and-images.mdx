## The short version

Catholics venerate images and do not worship them. The Church says "the honor rendered to an image passes to its prototype," and the honor is "respectful veneration," not the adoration due to God alone (CCC 2132).

## What the Church teaches

The first commandment forbids making a "graven image" of God, and the Catechism takes that seriously. God is "absolutely transcendent" (CCC 2129).

But "already in the Old Testament, God ordained or permitted the making of images that pointed symbolically toward salvation," such as the bronze serpent, the ark of the covenant and the cherubim (CCC 2130).

The seventh ecumenical council, at Nicaea in 787, "justified against the iconoclasts the veneration of icons" because the Son of God became visible in the Incarnation (CCC 2131).

"The Christian veneration of images is not contrary to the first commandment." The honor given to an image "passes to its prototype." It is "a respectful veneration, not the adoration due to God alone" (CCC 2132).

Sacred images "truly signify Christ, who is glorified in them," and "make manifest the cloud of witnesses" (CCC 1161).

## Scripture

> "Thou shalt not make to thyself a graven thing, nor the likeness of any thing that is in heaven above, or in the earth beneath, nor of those things that are in the waters under the earth. Thou shalt not adore them, nor serve them: I am the Lord thy God, mighty, jealous, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children, unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me:"
*Exodus 20:4-5 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "Thou shalt make also two cherubims of beaten gold, on the two sides of the oracle. Let one cherub be on the one side, and the other on the other. Let them cover both sides of the propitiatory, spreading their wings, and covering the oracle, and let them look one towards the other, their faces being turned towards the propitiatory wherewith the ark is to be covered."
*Exodus 25:18-20 (Douay-Rheims)*

Both verses are in Exodus. In the first, God forbids images made for worship. In the second, he orders gold cherubim made for the ark. The Church reads the ban as being about idols.

## The Fathers and saints

The Second Council of Nicaea (787) is the seventh ecumenical council, recognized by both Catholics and the Eastern Orthodox. The Orthodox have a rich tradition of icons and hold the same distinction between veneration and worship (CCC 2131).

## Why this matters today

The strongest Protestant objection is that the second commandment bans the practice. Many Protestant churches have no images at all, and that is an honest reading of the text.

Catholics reply that the Word became visible in Christ, which changes what an image can mean (CCC 1159, 2131).

## What's at stake

If images are idols, much of Christian history is idolatry. If they are not, the ban on them is a misreading. Both sides take the first commandment seriously.

## How to live it

- **Ask what a statue is for.** A statue in church is a reminder, like a photo of someone you love.
- **Notice if you are ever tempted to treat an object as magic.** The Church warns against it.
- **Learn about Byzantine icons.** They are written as prayer.
- **Read Exodus 20 and Exodus 25 together.**
