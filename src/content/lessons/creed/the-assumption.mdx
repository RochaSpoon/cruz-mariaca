## The short version

The Church teaches that when Mary's earthly life was finished, she was taken body and soul into heavenly glory (CCC 966). It was defined as dogma in 1950.

## What the Church teaches

"The Immaculate Virgin, preserved free from all stain of original sin, when the course of her earthly life was finished, was taken up body and soul into heavenly glory" (CCC 966).

The Catechism calls the Assumption "a singular participation in her Son's Resurrection and an anticipation of the resurrection of other Christians" (CCC 966).

Pope Pius XII defined it in the apostolic constitution Munificentissimus Deus, on 1 November 1950 (CCC 966, note).

The Catechism's wording, "when the course of her earthly life was finished," does not say how her life ended. It also quotes an Eastern prayer that uses the word "Dormition" (CCC 966).

## Scripture

> "And a great sign appeared in heaven: A woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars:"
*Apocalypse 12:1 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Bible does not describe the Assumption. Revelation 12:1 speaks of "a woman clothed with the sun," which many readers connect to Mary, but the doctrine does not rest on that verse alone. The Church defined it through her teaching office (CCC 891).

## Why this matters today

For many Protestants this is the hardest Marian teaching, since it is not in the Bible. The Catholic reply rests on the Church's Tradition and the teaching office (CCC 82, 891).

The Orthodox keep a feast of the Dormition and believe Mary was taken to heaven. They do not accept the Western dogmatic definition.

## What's at stake

The Assumption is about what happens to bodies. The Church says Mary's body shows what the resurrection means for all believers.

## How to live it

- **Compare it with the resurrection of the body.** See that lesson.
- **Look at the Eastern prayer** the Catechism quotes: "you did not leave the world, O Mother of God, but were joined to the source of Life" (CCC 966).
- **Be honest about the sources.** This is a doctrine of Tradition.
- **Keep the feast.** The Catholic feast of the Assumption is on August 15.
