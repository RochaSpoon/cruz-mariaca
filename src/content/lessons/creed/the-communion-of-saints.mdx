## The short version

The communion of saints is the whole Church: believers on earth, those being purified, and those in glory, joined in Christ (CCC 946, 954).

## What the Church teaches

"What is the Church if not the assembly of all the saints?" The Creed's "communion of saints" is a further explanation of "the holy catholic Church" (CCC 946).

The phrase has two linked meanings: communion in holy things, above all the Eucharist, and communion among holy persons (CCC 948, 960).

The Church has "three states." Some disciples "are pilgrims on earth," others "have died and are being purified," and others "are in glory" (CCC 954).

The union between these three groups "is in no way interrupted." It is "reinforced by an exchange of spiritual goods" (CCC 955).

What each one does or suffers in Christ "bears fruit for all" (CCC 961).

## Scripture

> "And therefore we also having so great a cloud of witnesses over our head, laying aside every weight and sin which surrounds us, let us run by patience to the fight proposed to us:"
*Hebrews 12:1 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And when he had opened the book, the four living creatures, and the four and twenty ancients fell down before the Lamb, having every one of them harps, and golden vials full of odours, which are the prayers of saints:"
*Apocalypse 5:8 (Douay-Rheims)*

Hebrews 11 lists the faithful of the Old Testament, and chapter 12 calls them a "cloud of witnesses." The Church takes this to mean that those who have died in Christ are still part of the family.

## Why this matters today

A lot of people feel alone in their faith. The Church's answer is that you belong to a very large family that includes the dead (CCC 955).

Many Protestants share the idea of fellowship among believers. The disagreement is mainly about whether we can ask the dead for prayer. The next lesson takes that up.

## What's at stake

If the dead are gone and the Church is only the people in the building, faith is a private matter. If the Church includes the dead, you are never praying alone.

## How to live it

- **Learn a saint's name** and read about one real life.
- **Pray for someone who has died.** The Church has done so "from the beginning" (CCC 1032).
- **Notice the Eucharist** as the "holy thing" that joins everyone (CCC 960).
- **Stop treating saints as statues.** They were people who struggled.
