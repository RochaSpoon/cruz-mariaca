## The short version

The Holy Spirit is the third Person of the Trinity, God and not a force. He is "Lord and giver of life," and he is how Christ stays with us (CCC 245, 683).

## What the Church teaches

At the second ecumenical council, in 381, the Church confessed: "We believe in the Holy Spirit, the Lord and giver of life, who proceeds from the Father" (CCC 245). The Spirit is God, "one and equal with the Father and the Son."

"Holy Spirit" is his proper name, received from the Lord and professed at Baptism (CCC 691).

We do not meet him directly. "The Spirit does not speak of himself." He makes us hear the Father's Word and makes Christ known (CCC 687).

"No one can say 'Jesus is Lord' except by the Holy Spirit." The Catechism says that to be in touch with Christ we must first have been touched by the Spirit, who kindles faith in us (CCC 683).

Jesus promised the Spirit when his hour had come, and the Spirit was poured out at Pentecost (CCC 729, 731). The first gift is love: "God's love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit" (CCC 733).

The Spirit is also the "master of prayer." When we do not know how to pray, "the Spirit himself intercedes with sighs too deep for words" (CCC 741).

## Scripture

> "If you love me, keep my commandments. And I will ask the Father, and he shall give you another Paraclete, that he may abide with you for ever. The spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it seeth him not, nor knoweth him: but you shall know him; because he shall abide with you, and shall be in you."
*John 14:15-17 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And when the days of the Pentecost were accomplished, they were all together in one place: And suddenly there came a sound from heaven, as of a mighty wind coming, and it filled the whole house where they were sitting. And there appeared to them parted tongues as it were of fire, and it sat upon every one of them: And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and they began to speak with divers tongues, according as the Holy Ghost gave them to speak."
*Acts 2:1-4 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "Likewise the Spirit also helpeth our infirmity. For we know not what we should pray for as we ought; but the Spirit himself asketh for us with unspeakable groanings."
*Romans 8:26 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Eastern and Western Churches word the Spirit's origin differently. The East says he proceeds from the Father, and the West adds "and the Son." See the lesson on the Trinity.

## Why this matters today

Many people talk about "the Spirit" as an energy or a vibe. The Church says he is a Person, with the Father and the Son (CCC 245).

Some people look for the Spirit mainly in dramatic experiences. The Catechism puts his main work in love and in the life of the Church (CCC 733, 737).

## What's at stake

If the Spirit is only a feeling, faith is as steady as your mood. If he is a Person, he is with you in the dry and dull stretches too.

## How to fight it

- **Pray to him by name.** "Come, Holy Spirit" is a perfectly good prayer.
- **Do not wait for a feeling.** The Spirit works through the sacraments whether or not you feel anything.
- **Read John 14-16.** It is Jesus' own explanation of who is coming.
- **Look for love, not fireworks.** The Catechism calls love the first gift (CCC 733).
