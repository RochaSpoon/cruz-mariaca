## The short version

The Immaculate Conception is Mary being preserved from original sin from the first moment of her existence, by the merits of Christ. It is not about Jesus' conception (CCC 491-492).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism says Mary, "full of grace" through God, "was redeemed from the moment of her conception." That is what the dogma confesses, as Pope Pius IX proclaimed in 1854 (CCC 491).

Her holiness comes "wholly from Christ." She is "redeemed, in a more exalted fashion, by reason of the merits of her Son" (CCC 492). She needed a savior like everyone else, and she received the saving grace ahead of time.

The Catechism says that in order for Mary "to be able to give the free assent of her faith," she had to be "wholly borne by God's grace" (CCC 490).

It also notes that the Eastern Fathers call her "the All-Holy" and say she was "free from any stain of sin," and that by God's grace she "remained free of every personal sin her whole life long" (CCC 493).

## Scripture

> "And in the sixth month, the angel Gabriel was sent from God into a city of Galilee, called Nazareth, To a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David; and the virgin's name was Mary. And the angel being come in, said unto her: Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with thee: blessed art thou among women."
*Luke 1:26-28 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Catechism reads Gabriel's greeting, "full of grace," as a sign of the gifts God gave her for her role (CCC 490). Many Protestants read the phrase more simply, as a statement of favor. That is a real disagreement over a Greek word.

## Why this matters today

People often confuse this with the virgin birth. The Immaculate Conception is about Mary's own conception, not Jesus'.

The usual Protestant objection is that Paul says "all have sinned" (Romans 3:23). The Church replies that this is a unique grace given for the sake of Christ, and that Mary's holiness is still a gift from him (CCC 492).

## What's at stake

If Mary's holiness did not come from Christ, it would compete with him. The Church says it is the first fruit of what he does for everyone.

## How to live it

- **Read Luke 1:26-38** as if you had never heard of the dogma.
- **Separate this from the virgin birth.** They are two doctrines.
- **Ask where the doctrine comes from.** The Church says it came from a growing awareness over centuries (CCC 491).
- **Hear the Eastern view.** The Orthodox honor Mary as "All-Holy" but do not accept the Western definition.
