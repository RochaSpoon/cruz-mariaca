## The short version

The Magisterium is the Church's teaching office, the Pope and the bishops in communion with him. It interprets Scripture and Tradition and is bound by them (CCC 85-86).

## What the Church teaches

"The task of giving an authentic interpretation of the Word of God, whether in its written form or in the form of Tradition, has been entrusted to the living teaching office of the Church alone" (CCC 85). That means the Pope and the bishops in communion with him (CCC 100).

The Catechism puts a limit on this. "This Magisterium is not superior to the Word of God, but is its servant. It teaches only what has been handed on to it" (CCC 86).

Its job is to "preserve God's people from deviations and defections" and to guarantee "the objective possibility of professing the true faith without error" (CCC 890).

The faithful "receive with docility the teachings and directives" of their pastors (CCC 87).

The Catechism recognizes more than one level of teaching. There are definitive teachings, and there are teachings in the ordinary Magisterium, where the faithful are asked to adhere with "religious assent," which the Catechism says is "distinct from the assent of faith" but "an extension of it" (CCC 892).

## Scripture

> "He that heareth you, heareth me; and he that despiseth you, despiseth me; and he that despiseth me, despiseth him that sent me."
*Luke 10:16 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And Jesus coming, spoke to them, saying: All power is given to me in heaven and in earth. Going therefore, teach ye all nations; baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost. Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and behold I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the world."
*Matthew 28:18-20 (Douay-Rheims)*

Protestants hold that Scripture alone is the final authority, and read it with the help of the Spirit and the church. Catholics hold that Christ also gave a teaching office. That is the real difference.

## Why this matters today

People often assume the Magisterium means the Church can change the faith whenever it likes. The Catechism says it can only teach what has been handed on (CCC 86).

Others assume everything a Catholic leader says carries the same weight. It does not. The Church distinguishes levels of authority, which is why this site labels each teaching.

## What's at stake

If no one has authority to say what Scripture means, every reader is on their own, and the faith splits. If someone has it without limits, the faith gets rewritten. The Church claims a middle path.

## How to live it

- **Learn the levels.** Dogma, definitive doctrine, authoritative teaching, discipline, and theological opinion are not the same thing.
- **Ask what a claim is based on** before accepting or rejecting it.
- **Ask questions in the open.** Learn what a teaching rests on before you reject it.
- **Read the actual document,** not the news story about it.
