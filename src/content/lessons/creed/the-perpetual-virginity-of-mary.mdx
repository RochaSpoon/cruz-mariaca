## The short version

The Church teaches that Mary was a virgin before, during and after Jesus' birth. The "brothers of Jesus" are understood as close relatives (CCC 499-500).

## What the Church teaches

Jesus was "conceived solely by the power of the Holy Spirit in the womb of the Virgin Mary" (CCC 496). The Gospels present this as "a divine work that surpasses all human understanding" (CCC 497).

The Church also confesses Mary's "real and perpetual virginity even in the act of giving birth." The liturgy calls her Aeiparthenos, "Ever-virgin" (CCC 499).

On the "brothers and sisters of Jesus," the Catechism says the Church "has always understood these passages as not referring to other children of the Virgin Mary." It says James and Joseph are sons of another Mary and "close relations of Jesus, according to an Old Testament expression" (CCC 500).

"Jesus is Mary's only son, but her spiritual motherhood extends to all men whom indeed he came to save" (CCC 501).

## Scripture

> "Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary, the brother of James, and Joseph, and Jude, and Simon? are not also his sisters here with us? And they were scandalized in regard of him."
*Mark 6:3 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "When Jesus therefore had seen his mother and the disciple standing whom he loved, he saith to his mother: Woman, behold thy son. After that, he saith to the disciple: Behold thy mother. And from that hour, the disciple took her to his own."
*John 19:26-27 (Douay-Rheims)*

Many Protestants read "brothers" in these verses as siblings. The Catechism says the Church "has always understood these passages" differently (CCC 500). The debate is old and both sides can quote the text.

## Why this matters today

The best objection is a plain one: the Gospels say "brothers." The Catechism's answer is that these are "close relations of Jesus, according to an Old Testament expression" (CCC 500). Catholics also point out that Jesus, dying, gave his mother to John (John 19:26-27), which would be an odd thing to do if she had other sons.

The Orthodox hold the same doctrine as Catholics, and Mary is called Ever-Virgin in the East as well.

## What's at stake

The Church sees in the virginal birth a sign that Jesus really is the Son of God. The virginity of Mary shows his birth as God's work, not human achievement (CCC 496, 502).

## How to live it

- **Read both sides of the "brothers" argument** in the Gospels themselves.
- **Notice who Jesus asks to look after his mother.** It tells you something.
- **Do not mock the objection.** It is a fair reading of the text.
- **Ask where each of the three claims comes from:** Scripture, Tradition, or both.
