## The short version

Catholics believe Christ made Peter the "rock," gave him the keys, and made him shepherd of the whole flock, and that the Bishop of Rome is his successor (CCC 881-882).

## What the Church teaches

Christ instituted the Twelve as a "college" and "placed Peter" at its head. In the same way, the Pope and the bishops, the successors of the apostles, form a college (CCC 880).

"The Lord made Simon alone, whom he named Peter, the 'rock' of his Church. He gave him the keys of his Church and instituted him shepherd of the whole flock" (CCC 881).

The Pope, the Bishop of Rome and Peter's successor, is "the perpetual and visible source and foundation of the unity both of the bishops and of the whole company of the faithful" (CCC 882).

The college of bishops "has no authority unless united with the Roman Pontiff," and the Pope "has full, supreme, and universal power over the whole Church" (CCC 882-883).

Each bishop is the visible source of unity in his own diocese (CCC 886). The Pope does not replace them.

This is one of the sharpest disagreements between Catholics and the Orthodox and Protestants. The Orthodox do not accept the Pope's universal jurisdiction, and most Protestants do not accept a papacy at all. Their readings of these verses are serious ones, and the lesson on the Magisterium explains how the Church answers.

## Why this matters today

Many people think the Pope can say anything he likes and Catholics must agree. The Catechism says the Magisterium "is not superior to the Word of God, but is its servant" (CCC 86).

Others think the Pope is just a famous bishop. The Church says his office belongs to the Church's "very foundation" (CCC 881).

## What's at stake

If Christ left no one to settle disputes, the Church ends up with thousands of voices and no way to say which one is his. The Church says he did leave someone, and that this is part of how the faith stays one.

## How to live it

- **Read Matthew 16:13-19 and John 21:15-17 yourself,** slowly, with no commentary.
- **Learn what the Pope has actually taught** before you judge a headline about him.
- **Know the difference** between a papal opinion and a definition. The next two lessons explain it.
- **Listen to the Orthodox objection.** It is old, and it is serious.
