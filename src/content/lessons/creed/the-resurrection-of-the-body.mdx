## The short version

Catholics believe that on the last day God will raise our actual bodies, transformed, and reunite them with our souls. It is not only the soul that survives (CCC 990, 997).

## What the Church teaches

"The resurrection of the flesh" means "not only that the immortal soul will live on after death, but that even our 'mortal body' will come to life again" (CCC 990).

At death "the human body decays and the soul goes to meet God, while awaiting its reunion with its glorified body." God "will definitively grant incorruptible life to our bodies by reuniting them with our souls" (CCC 997).

"All the dead will rise" (CCC 998). The risen will have "their own bodies which they now bear," which Christ will change "to be like his glorious body," a "spiritual body" (CCC 999).

The "how" "exceeds our imagination and understanding; it is accessible only to faith" (CCC 1000).

Our resurrection is tied to Christ's: "Just as Christ is risen and lives for ever, so all of us will rise at the last day" (CCC 1016). The Church already has a foretaste of it in Baptism and the Eucharist (CCC 1000, 1003).

## Scripture

> "So also is the resurrection of the dead. It is sown in corruption, it shall rise in incorruption. It is sown in dishonour, it shall rise in glory. It is sown in weakness, it shall rise in power. It is sown a natural body, it shall rise a spiritual body. If there be a natural body, there is also a spiritual body, as it is written:"
*1 Corinthians 15:42-44 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "Jesus said to her: I am the resurrection and the life: he that believeth in me, although he be dead, shall live: And every one that liveth, and believeth in me, shall not die for ever. Believest thou this?"
*John 11:25-26 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says the body is sown "a natural body" and raised "a spiritual body." The Church takes "spiritual" to mean transformed by the Spirit, not made of spirit.

## Why this matters today

Many people accept that something of us lives on after death, but find a bodily resurrection hard to believe. The Catechism quotes the saying, "On no point does the Christian faith encounter more opposition than on the resurrection of the body" (CCC 996).

The Church says hope in the resurrection "established itself as a consequence intrinsic to faith in God as creator of the whole man, soul and body" (CCC 992).

## What's at stake

If bodies do not matter, it hardly matters what we do with them. If they are destined for resurrection, then what you do with your body, and how you treat other people's, matters a great deal.

## How to live it

- **Treat your body, and everyone else's, with respect.** The Catechism says belonging to Christ gives the body a dignity that demands this (CCC 1004).
- **Bury and mourn the dead with respect.** "The bodies of the dead must be treated with respect and charity, in faith and hope of the Resurrection" (CCC 2300).
- **Read 1 Corinthians 15** in one sitting.
- **Notice the Eucharist.** The Church says it gives "a foretaste" (CCC 1000).
