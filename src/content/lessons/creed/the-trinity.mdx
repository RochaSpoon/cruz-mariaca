## The short version

One God in three Persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit. Not three gods, and not one God wearing three masks (CCC 253-254).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism calls the Trinity "the central mystery of Christian faith and life" (CCC 234). It is a mystery in the strict sense, something we could never have worked out and only know because God revealed it (CCC 237).

The Church says it this way: "We do not confess three Gods, but one God in three persons." Each person is "God whole and entire" (CCC 253).

The three Persons are really distinct. They are not just names for modes or roles. The Father generates, the Son is begotten, and the Holy Spirit proceeds (CCC 254).

The Church uses the word "substance" for what is one in God, and "person" for what is three. The distinction between the Persons lies in their relations to each other (CCC 252, 255).

Christians have always baptized "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (CCC 233). The Church says the teaching was lived and prayed before it was defined (CCC 249).

## Scripture

> "Going therefore, teach ye all nations; baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost."
*Matthew 28:19 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And I will ask the Father, and he shall give you another Paraclete, that he may abide with you for ever. The spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it seeth him not, nor knoweth him: but you shall know him; because he shall abide with you, and shall be in you."
*John 14:16-17 (Douay-Rheims)*

The word "Trinity" is not in the Bible. The Church says the word names what these verses and many others show.

## The Fathers and saints

The Catechism notes that the Church has described the Persons with the words "substance," "person" and "relation" (CCC 252). On the origin of the Holy Spirit, the Eastern tradition says first that he proceeds from the Father, and the Western tradition says first that he proceeds from the Father and the Son, the Filioque (CCC 248). That phrase was not in the Creed of 381, and it was admitted into the Latin liturgy gradually (CCC 247). This is a real disagreement between East and West, though both confess that the Spirit is God.

## Why this matters today

The usual attempts to explain the Trinity are heresies. Water in three forms makes the three Persons into modes, and the idea of one God playing three roles is an old error (CCC 254).

Some groups, such as Unitarians and Jehovah's Witnesses, reject the Trinity. The Church answers that the formula goes back to the apostolic writings, which already name the Father, the Son and the Spirit together (CCC 249).

## What's at stake

If God is not a Trinity, then love was not part of God before there was a world, and God needed creation to have something to love. The Church says God is "an eternal exchange of love" (CCC 221).

## How to live it

- **Pray the Sign of the Cross slowly.** It is a short creed.
- **Do not try to picture it.** The Church says it is a mystery, not a puzzle (CCC 237).
- **Learn the Creed's second paragraph.** It says what the Church believes about the Son.
- **Ask what "person" means to the person you are talking with.** Most arguments about the Trinity are arguments about words.
