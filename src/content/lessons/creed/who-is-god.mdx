## The short version

The Church's first claim about God is that there is only one, and that he is love (CCC 200, 221). Everything else in the Creed hangs on that.

## What the Church teaches

The Creed begins "I believe in God," and the Catechism calls this "the most fundamental" statement, the one the other articles depend on (CCC 199). Its first claim is that God is one: "there is only one God" (CCC 200).

When God gave Moses his name, "I AM WHO AM," the Church read it to mean that "God alone IS": he is the fullness of being and every perfection, without beginning or end (CCC 206, 213). Everything else receives what it is from him.

The second claim is about character. God revealed himself to Israel for one reason, "his sheer gratuitous love" (CCC 218). John goes further and says "God is love," which the Church takes to mean that God's very being is love (CCC 221).

That is why Christians say God is Father, Son and Holy Spirit. If God is love, then he is not a lonely power. The Catechism says God "is an eternal exchange of love" that he wants us to share in (CCC 221).

## Scripture

> "Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God is one Lord. Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with thy whole heart, and with thy whole soul, and with thy whole strength."
*Deuteronomy 6:4-5 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "Moses said to God: Lo, I shall go to the children of Israel, and say to them: The God of your fathers hath sent me to you. If they should say to me: What is his name? what shall I say to them? God said to Moses: I AM WHO AM. He said: Thus shalt thou say to the children of Israel: HE WHO IS, hath sent me to you."
*Exodus 3:13-14 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "He that loveth not, knoweth not God: for God is charity."
*1 John 4:8 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jews, Muslims and Christians all start from the first verse here. Christians add that the one God is also Father, Son and Holy Spirit.

## Why this matters today

Many people picture God as a force, a judge, or a distant designer. The Church says he is a person who knows you and loves you first.

Others say a good God would not allow suffering. That is a real objection, and the Church does not wave it away. It starts by saying that God is love and that evil is not his doing (CCC 311-312).

## What's at stake

If God is only power, then the world is about who wins. If God is love, then the world is about being loved and loving back. These lead to very different lives.

## How to fight it

- **When you feel he is distant,** read the Burning Bush passage (Exodus 3). God says "I have seen the affliction of my people" before he says anything else.
- **Stop treating God as a vending machine.** The Catechism says prayer is where God's thirst meets ours (CCC 2560).
- **Ask a hard question out loud.** You can tell God you are angry. The Psalms are full of it.
- **Talk to someone who believes differently.** A Jewish friend and a Muslim friend will push you to say what you mean by "one God."
