## The short version

Jesus is true God and true man, one Person with two natures (CCC 464, 480). He is not half and half, and not God pretending to be human.

## What the Church teaches

The Church confesses that "the Son of God assumed a human nature" in order to save us, and calls this the Incarnation (CCC 461).

"Jesus Christ is true God and true man." He is not "part God and part man," and not a "confused mixture" of the two (CCC 464).

The early heresies went wrong in both directions. Some denied his real humanity, and some denied he was Son of God by nature (CCC 465). The Council of Chalcedon, in 451, confessed "one and the same Son," "perfect in divinity and perfect in humanity" (CCC 467).

His human nature was "assumed, not absorbed" (CCC 470). He has a real human body, a human mind and a human will.

Because he is both, he is "the one and only mediator between God and men" (CCC 480).

## Scripture

> "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. The same was in the beginning with God. All things were made by him: and without him was made nothing that was made. In him was life, and the life was the light of men. And the light shineth in darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it."
*John 1:1-5 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we saw his glory, the glory as it were of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth."
*John 1:14 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "Who being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God: But emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, being made in the likeness of men, and in habit found as a man. He humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death, even to the death of the cross."
*Philippians 2:6-8 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Eastern Orthodox and most Protestant churches also accept the creeds of Nicaea and Chalcedon on who Jesus is. This is common ground.

## Why this matters today

Many people admire Jesus as a teacher and stop there. The Church says he gave us to understand that he himself is "the Lord" (CCC 202).

Others imagine him as a man who became divine later, or as God only dressed as a man. The Church rejects both (CCC 465).

## What's at stake

If Jesus is only a man, his death is a tragedy. If he is only God, he has not joined us. The Church says he is both so that God himself could take our place and reach us.

## How to live it

- **Read one Gospel straight through.** Mark is the shortest.
- **Notice both sides of him.** He gets tired and he forgives sins.
- **Learn Chalcedon's phrase,** "truly God and truly man" (CCC 467). It is easy to remember and covers most of the argument.
- **Ask who you think he is.** He asked his disciples the same thing (Matthew 16:15).
