## The short version

Catholics ask the saints to pray for them, the way you would ask a friend. They do not pray to saints as if they were God (CCC 956, 2683).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism says those in heaven "do not cease to intercede with the Father for us, as they proffer the merits which they acquired on earth through the one mediator between God and men, Christ Jesus" (CCC 956).

It also says we cherish the memory of the saints so that "the union of the whole Church in the Spirit may be strengthened," and that "our communion with the saints joins us to Christ" (CCC 957).

The saints "contemplate God, praise him and constantly care for those whom they have left on earth." Their intercession "is their most exalted service to God's plan" (CCC 2683).

The word "pray" here means "ask," as in the old English "I pray thee." Asking a saint to pray for you is the same kind of request as asking a friend, only addressed to a friend in heaven.

## Scripture

> "Confess therefore your sins one to another: and pray one for another, that you may be saved. For the continual prayer of a just man availeth much."
*James 5:16 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And when he had opened the book, the four living creatures, and the four and twenty ancients fell down before the Lamb, having every one of them harps, and golden vials full of odours, which are the prayers of saints:"
*Apocalypse 5:8 (Douay-Rheims)*

Revelation 5:8 shows the elders in heaven offering "the prayers of saints." Protestants read this differently and note that no verse tells Christians to ask the dead for prayer. Catholics say the New Testament shows the dead in Christ alive and caring for the Church, and that the practice follows from that.

## Why this matters today

The strongest objection is that Jesus is the one mediator (1 Timothy 2:5). The Catechism agrees and says the saints' prayers go "through the one mediator" (CCC 956).

Another worry is that asking a saint is a short step from worshiping one. The Church says veneration is not adoration (CCC 2132).

## What's at stake

If the dead are really dead, asking them is pointless. If they are alive in Christ, then not asking them is a strange way to treat the family you belong to.

## How to live it

- **Ask a friend to pray for you,** and then ask a saint. Note what is different.
- **Pick a saint who fits your situation.** Their stories are real.
- **Keep Christ at the center** of every prayer to anyone else.
- **Read the Protestant objection in its own words** before you answer it.
