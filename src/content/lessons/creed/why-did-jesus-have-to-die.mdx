## The short version

The Church teaches that Christ died "for our sins" as part of God's plan, out of love, and that his death atones for them (CCC 601, 616). It does not say God is angry at Jesus.

## What the Church teaches

Jesus' death "was not the result of chance." The Catechism says it is "part of the mystery of God's plan" (CCC 599), foretold in Scripture as the death of "the righteous one, my Servant" (CCC 601).

The Catechism says that "Jesus did not experience reprobation as if he himself had sinned." He took our place in solidarity with sinners (CCC 603).

What gives the sacrifice its value is love: "It is love 'to the end' that confers on Christ's sacrifice its value as redemption and reparation, as atonement and satisfaction" (CCC 616).

The Catechism uses several images for what the cross did: a ransom, a Paschal sacrifice, a sacrifice of the New Covenant, an obedience that undoes Adam's disobedience (CCC 613, 615). It says Christ died for all, and that his love "excludes no one" (CCC 605).

## Scripture

> "Surely he hath borne our infirmities and carried our sorrows: and we have thought him as it were a leper, and as one struck by God and afflicted. But he was wounded for our iniquities, he was bruised for our sins: the chastisement of our peace was upon him, and by his bruises we are healed. All we like sheep have gone astray, every one hath turned aside into his own way: and the Lord hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."
*Isaiah 53:4-6 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "But God commendeth his charity towards us; because when as yet we were sinners, according to the time,"
*Romans 5:8 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "For I delivered unto you first of all, which I also received: how that Christ died for our sins, according to the scriptures: And that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day, according to the scriptures:"
*1 Corinthians 15:3-4 (Douay-Rheims)*

Christians have explained the cross in different ways. The Orthodox stress Christ conquering death and healing human nature. Many Protestants stress him bearing the penalty for sin. The Catechism itself uses several images and does not reduce the cross to one.

## Why this matters today

A common objection is that this is cosmic child abuse: a Father who punishes his Son. The Catechism rules that picture out. The Father and the Son act together, and Jesus "freely" offers himself, in the love that always united him to the Father (CCC 603, 606).

Another objection is that forgiveness should not require a sacrifice. The Church replies that real love pays a real cost, and that this cost was paid by God himself, not demanded from someone else (CCC 616).

## What's at stake

If the cross is only an example, you are stuck with your guilt and your own strength. If it is more than that, you can be forgiven and started over.

## How to live it

- **Stand in front of a crucifix** and remember what the Catechism says: "He knew and loved us all when he offered his life" (CCC 616).
- **Go to Confession.** The Church teaches that those who approach the sacrament of Penance obtain pardon from God's mercy (CCC 1422).
- **Take up your own cross.** Jesus calls his disciples to do that (CCC 618).
- **Do not argue the theory at the foot of the cross.** Read Isaiah 53 and sit with it first.
