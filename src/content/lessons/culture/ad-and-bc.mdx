## The short version

A monk in Rome named Dionysius Exiguus introduced the system that counts years from the birth of Christ, about the year 527 (Catholic Encyclopedia, "General Chronology").

## What the Church teaches

The Catholic Encyclopedia says the Christian, Vulgar or Common Era "was introduced about the year 527 by Dionysius Exiguus, a Scythian monk resident at Rome" (Catholic Encyclopedia, General Chronology).

He fixed the birth of Christ in the year 753 from the foundation of Rome. He made this "the year 1 of his era" (Catholic Encyclopedia, General Chronology).

A.D. stands for Anno Domini, "in the year of the Lord." The year before is called B.C., Before Christ. There is no year 0 between them (Catholic Encyclopedia, General Chronology).

The same article notes that "many" suppose his calculation was off and that Christ was born a few years earlier. It says this does not matter for chronology.

## Scripture

> "But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent his Son, made of a woman, made under the law: That he might redeem them who were under the law: that we might receive the adoption of sons."
*Galatians 4:4-5 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says that when the fullness of time came, God sent His Son. The dating system counts time from that event.

## Why this matters today

Many schools and publications now write C.E. and B.C.E. The numbers are the same as A.D. and B.C.

Scholars generally place Jesus' birth a few years before the year 1, since Herod's reign ended earlier. The Catholic Encyclopedia says this as well.

## What's at stake

This is a history lesson, not a doctrine. It shows how deeply the Church's calendar shaped the way the West counts time.

## How to live it

- **Notice** the date on a document or coin you read.
- **Be** careful with claims that A.D. means "after death." It means "in the year of the Lord."
