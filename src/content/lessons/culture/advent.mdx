## The short version

Advent is the Church's season of waiting. It looks back to the first coming of Christ and ahead to the second (CCC 524).

## What the Church teaches

"When the Church celebrates the liturgy of Advent each year, she makes present this ancient expectancy of the Messiah." By sharing in the long preparation for his first coming, "the faithful renew their ardent desire for his second coming" (CCC 524).

The Church also remembers John the Baptist in Advent, and "unites herself to his desire: 'He must increase, but I must decrease'" (CCC 524).

The vestment color for Advent is violet, the same as Lent (GIRM 346). Rose may be used on Gaudete Sunday, the third Sunday of Advent (GIRM 346).

The Catechism says the cycle of feasts around the Incarnation "commemorate the beginning of our salvation" (CCC 1171).

## Scripture

> "He that hath the bride, is the bridegroom: but the friend of the bridegroom, who standeth and heareth him, rejoiceth with joy because of the bridegroom's voice. This my joy therefore is fulfilled. He must increase, but I must decrease."
*John 3:29-30 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "The people that walked in darkness, have seen a great light: to them that dwelt in the region of the shadow of death, light is risen."
*Isaiah 9:2 (Douay-Rheims)*

John the Baptist's line is the Advent theme: Christ grows and everything else gets smaller.

## Why this matters today

The shops start Christmas in October. Advent asks for the opposite: a few weeks of wanting something and not having it yet.

Some Protestant churches keep Advent too. It is one place where traditions overlap.

## What's at stake

If Christmas is only a day, you can finish it by lunch. If it is the answer to a long expectation, you have to feel the expectation first.

## How to live it

- **Pick one small thing to give up** until December 25, and keep it quiet.
- **Light a candle each Sunday** and read the Gospel for the day.
- **Delay the carols** a little. They mean more on Christmas Eve.
- **Go to Confession before Christmas.**
