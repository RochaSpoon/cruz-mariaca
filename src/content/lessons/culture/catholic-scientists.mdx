## The short version

Georges Lemaître, a Belgian Catholic priest, proposed in a 1931 Nature note that the universe began from a single "primeval atom." The Church teaches that faith and reason cannot contradict each other (CCC 159).

## What the Church teaches

"Though faith is above reason, there can never be any real discrepancy between faith and reason. Since the same God who reveals mysteries and infuses faith has bestowed the light of reason on the human mind, God cannot deny himself, nor can truth ever contradict truth" (CCC 159).

"Consequently, methodical research in all branches of knowledge, provided it is carried out in a truly scientific manner and does not override moral laws, can never conflict with the faith" (CCC 159).

Georges Lemaître, a Belgian cosmologist and priest, set out the idea of a single initial quantum in a short note published in Nature on May 9, 1931 (American Museum of Natural History; American Physical Society).

The term "Big Bang" came later. According to the sources found, it was coined by Fred Hoyle in a 1949 BBC radio talk, using it dismissively.

## Scripture

> "But all men are vain, in whom there is not the knowledge of God: and who by these good things that are seen, could not understand him that is, neither by attending to the works have acknowledged who was the workman: But have imagined either the fire, or the wind, or the swift air, or the circle of the stars, or the great water, or the sun and moon, to be the gods that rule the world. With whose beauty, if they, being delighted, took them to be gods: let them know how much the Lord of them is more beautiful than they: for the first author of beauty made all those things. Or if they admired their power and their effects, let them understand by them, that he that made them, is mightier than they: For by the greatness of the beauty, and of the creature, the creator of them may be seen, so as to be known thereby."
*Wisdom 13:1-5 (Douay-Rheims)*

The book says that from the greatness of the beauty of the creature, the Creator may be seen.

## Why this matters today

Lemaître himself saw faith and science as distinct fields. One source says he did not want his physics used to prove his religion.

Many other Catholics have been scientists. The lesson names one case that could be confirmed from sources read for this site.

## What's at stake

If faith and reason both come from God, the Church holds they cannot finally conflict. When they seem to, one has been misread.
