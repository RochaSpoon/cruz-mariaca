## The short version

Catholics and the Eastern Orthodox share the creeds, seven sacraments and apostolic succession. They differ most on the Pope's authority and on the Filioque (CCC 838, 882, 246-248).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism says Catholic communion with the Orthodox Churches "is so profound 'that it lacks little to attain the fullness that would permit a common celebration of the Lord's Eucharist'" (CCC 838).

The Orthodox "possess true sacraments, above all - by apostolic succession - the priesthood and the Eucharist" (CCC 1399). Vatican II notes that "for many centuries the Church of the East and that of the West each followed their separate ways though linked in a brotherly union of faith and sacramental life" (Unitatis Redintegratio 14).

**The papacy.** The Catholic Church teaches that the Pope has "full, supreme, and universal power over the whole Church" (CCC 882). The Orthodox do not accept this claim.

**The Filioque.** The West says the Spirit proceeds "from the Father and the Son"; the East says first that he proceeds from the Father (CCC 246-248).

**Married priests.** The Eastern tradition ordains married men as deacons and priests, while bishops come only from celibates. The Catechism says this practice "has long been considered legitimate" (CCC 1580).

Vatican II says differences in theological expression between East and West are "often... mutually complementary rather than conflicting" (Unitatis Redintegratio 17).

## Scripture

> "One body and one Spirit; as you are called in one hope of your calling. One Lord, one faith, one baptism. One God and Father of all, who is above all, and through all, and in us all."
*Ephesians 4:4-6 (Douay-Rheims)*

Ephesians 4 speaks of one body, one Spirit and one faith. The question between East and West is what visible unity requires.

## Why this matters today

Many Catholics know little about the Orthodox. They are a very old Church, with bishops and sacraments, and they take the same creed seriously.

The Orthodox objection to papal authority is old and serious. Honest conversation starts by stating it fairly.

## What's at stake

The division between East and West is one of the oldest in Christianity. Closing it would change the face of the Church.

## How to live it

- **Attend an Orthodox liturgy** and observe respectfully.
- **Learn how the Orthodox state the Filioque objection** in their own words.
- **Read Unitatis Redintegratio 14-18.**
- **Pray for reunion.**
