## The short version

Catholics and most Protestants share Christ, the Trinity and the Bible, and differ on authority, how we are saved, the sacraments and Mary. The Church calls Protestants "brothers" (CCC 818).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism says those "who believe in Christ and have been properly baptized are put in a certain, although imperfect, communion with the Catholic Church" (CCC 838). "Many elements of sanctification and of truth" are found outside her, "the written Word of God" among them (CCC 819).

**Authority.** Catholics hold that Scripture and Tradition together are the source of the faith, and that the teaching office interprets them (CCC 82, 85). Most Protestants hold that Scripture alone is the final authority.

**Salvation.** The Catechism says justification is from "the grace of God," not earned (CCC 1996). Protestants and Catholics both say we are saved by grace through Christ, and differ on how faith, works and the sacraments are related.

**Sacraments.** The Church counts seven (CCC 1113). The Westminster Confession says there are only two sacraments ordained by Christ, Baptism and the Lord's Supper (chapter 27).

**Mary and the saints.** The Church honors Mary and asks the saints to pray for her people (CCC 956, 971). Many Protestants reject praying to anyone but God.

## Scripture

> "For we account a man to be justified by faith, without the works of the law."
*Romans 3:28 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "Do you see that by works a man is justified; and not by faith only?"
*James 2:24 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says a man is justified by faith without the works of the law, and James says a man is justified by works and not by faith only. Both sides read these verses carefully and differ on how they fit together.

## Why this matters today

Catholics and Protestants often argue as if the other side believed less than it does. Most Protestant churches affirm Christ's divinity and the Trinity, as the Catholic Church does.

The disagreements are real. They are also narrower than the old arguments made them sound, and the Church says people born into Protestant communities are not to be blamed for the separation (CCC 818).

## What's at stake

If you cannot say what the other side believes in terms they would accept, you are arguing with a cartoon.

## How to live it

- **State a Protestant position** in its own best words before you answer it.
- **Read the Westminster or Augsburg Confession** alongside the Catechism.
- **Pray with Protestant friends** and share the Lord's Prayer.
- **Be honest about the differences.** Unity built on pretending does not last.
