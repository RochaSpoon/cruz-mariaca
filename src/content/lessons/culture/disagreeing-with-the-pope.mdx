## The short version

Catholics owe the Pope's teaching "religious submission of mind and will," and the Church also says lay people have "the right and even at times a duty" to voice their opinions on the good of the Church (LG 25; CCC 907).

## What the Church teaches

In matters of faith and morals, the faithful are "to accept" the bishops' teaching "and adhere to it with a religious assent" (Lumen Gentium 25).

The Council adds that this assent "must be shown in a special way to the authentic magisterium of the Roman Pontiff, even when he is not speaking ex cathedra." His mind and will may be known "from the character of the documents, from his frequent repetition of the same doctrine, or from his manner of speaking" (Lumen Gentium 25).

For teaching that is not an infallible definition, the Catechism says the faithful adhere with "religious assent," which is "distinct from the assent of faith" but "an extension of it" (CCC 892).

The Catechism also says lay people "have the right and even at times a duty to manifest to the sacred pastors their opinion on matters which pertain to the good of the Church," "with due regard to the integrity of faith and morals and reverence toward their pastors" (CCC 907).

The Church recognizes that people can have "personal difficulties" with a teaching, and distinguishes them from organized public opposition, which she calls "dissent" (Donum Veritatis 31-32).

## Scripture

> "But when Cephas was come to Antioch, I withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed."
*Galatians 2:11 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "He that heareth you, heareth me; and he that despiseth you, despiseth me; and he that despiseth me, despiseth him that sent me."
*Luke 10:16 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says he "withstood" Peter "to the face." Both verses are cited in this discussion. The Church distinguishes respectful criticism from public opposition to a teaching.

## Why this matters today

Many Catholics say they disagree with the Pope. The Church's question is what they mean: a prudential judgment on a policy, or a rejection of a doctrine.

Some Catholics treat every papal remark as binding. The Council says the weight depends on the kind of document and how it is taught (Lumen Gentium 25).

## What's at stake

If you cannot disagree with anyone, you are not free. If you can disagree with anything, you have no teacher. The Church sets a line between the two.

## How to live it

- **Ask what kind of statement it is.** An encyclical is not a press conference.
- **Read the document** before you take a side.
- **Voice concerns respectfully** to your pastor or bishop (CCC 907).
- **Keep studying** a teaching you find hard before you reject it.
