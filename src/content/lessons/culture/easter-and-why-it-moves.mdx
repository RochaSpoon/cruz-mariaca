## The short version

Easter moves because it is tied to the moon and the spring equinox. At Nicaea in 325 the Churches agreed to keep it on the same Sunday (CCC 1170).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism says: "At the Council of Nicaea in 325, all the Churches agreed that Easter, the Christian Passover, should be celebrated on the Sunday following the first full moon (14 Nisan) after the vernal equinox" (CCC 1170).

The Council's own letter to the Church of Alexandria mentions "the agreement concerning the holy Easter." It says the churches in the East that had followed another custom would now keep it "at the same time with the Romans" and the others (Synodal Letter of Nicaea).

The Catechism notes that the reform of the Western calendar, called "Gregorian" after Pope Gregory XIII (1582), caused a discrepancy between the Western and Eastern dates, and that the Churches are still seeking agreement (CCC 1170).

Easter is "not simply one feast among others" but "the Feast of feasts" (CCC 1169).

## Scripture

> "Purge out the old leaven, that you may be a new paste, as you are unleavened. For Christ our pasch is sacrificed. Therefore let us feast, not with the old leaven, nor with the leaven of malice and wickedness; but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth."
*1 Corinthians 5:7-8 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul calls Christ "our pasch." Pasch is the old word for Passover, and it is why Easter is tied to the Jewish feast in the first place.

## Why this matters today

People ask why Easter jumps around by weeks. The answer is that it follows a lunar rule, not a fixed date.

Eastern and Western Easter often fall on different days. The Catechism treats that as a lingering result of the calendar change (CCC 1170).

## What's at stake

The date matters because Easter is the center of the Church year. The agreement at Nicaea shows how seriously the early Church took keeping it together.

## How to live it

- **Look up this year's date** and count back to find the start of Lent.
- **Keep the full Triduum,** Holy Thursday through Easter.
- **Ask a friend from an Orthodox church** when they keep Easter, and why.
- **Read the Nicaea letter.** It is short.
