## The short version

The Church keeps feasts for Christ, for Mary and for the saints through the year. The Catechism says she proposes the saints "as examples who draw all men to the Father" (CCC 1173).

## What the Church teaches

From the time of the Mosaic law, "the People of God have observed fixed feasts, beginning with Passover," to remember what God has done, to thank him, and to teach new generations (CCC 1164).

The Church keeps Sunday every week as the memory of the Resurrection, and Easter once a year (CCC 1163, 1166).

The year also honors Mary "with a special love," because she is "inseparably linked with the saving work of her Son" (CCC 1172).

And it keeps the memorials of martyrs and other saints. The Church "proposes them to the faithful as examples who draw all men to the Father through Christ" (CCC 1173).

## Scripture

> "This is the day which the Lord hath made: let us be glad and rejoice therein."
*Psalms 117:24 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And therefore we also having so great a cloud of witnesses over our head, laying aside every weight and sin which surrounds us, let us run by patience to the fight proposed to us:"
*Hebrews 12:1 (Douay-Rheims)*

The first verse is Psalm 117 in the Douay-Rheims numbering. It is sung at Easter and on many feast days.

## Why this matters today

Our calendars are full of dates and most of them mean nothing. A feast day is a date that points at someone.

Many people know a saint only as a name on a church. The feast day is an invitation to read about the real person.

## What's at stake

A year with no feasts is all the same. The Church year makes some days bigger than others on purpose.

## How to live it

- **Look up the saint of today's date** and read one paragraph about them.
- **Learn your patron's feast day** and keep it.
- **Mark one feast a month** with a meal or a prayer.
- **Notice what the Church makes a feast of** and what that says about her priorities.
