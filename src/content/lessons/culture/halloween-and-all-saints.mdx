## The short version

Halloween is the eve of All Saints' Day, November 1. The Church's feast is about the saints in heaven and the whole communion of saints, not about fear (CCC 946-962).

## What the Church teaches

All Saints is a solemnity on November 1, and white vestments are used (GIRM 346).

The Catechism teaches that the "communion of saints is the Church" and includes "pilgrims on earth," those "being purified," and those "in glory" (CCC 946, 954).

The Church prays for the dead "from the beginning," above all in the Eucharist, "so that, thus purified, they may attain the beatific vision" (CCC 1032).

The saints are "examples who draw all men to the Father" (CCC 1173).

The Catechism also warns against occult practices, including spiritism, magic and sorcery (CCC 2116-2117).

## Scripture

> "After this I saw a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and tribes, and peoples, and tongues, standing before the throne, and in sight of the Lamb, clothed with white robes, and palms in their hands: And they cried with a loud voice, saying: Salvation to our God, who sitteth upon the throne, and to the Lamb."
*Apocalypse 7:9-10 (Douay-Rheims)*

John sees a great multitude "of all nations, and tribes, and peoples, and tongues" before the throne. The Church sees in this the saints of All Saints.

## Why this matters today

Some Christians avoid Halloween altogether, and some Catholics dress their kids as saints. Both are serious ways of answering the question.

The Church does not issue a ruling on costumes and candy. The Catechism does warn against dabbling in the occult (CCC 2116-2117), which is a separate matter.

## What's at stake

If the holiday is only about fear, it teaches you to be afraid. All Saints teaches you to be hopeful.

## How to live it

- **Dress as a saint** or read about one.
- **Go to Mass on November 1.**
- **Pray for a family member who has died** around the feast.
- **Skip the occult.** The Catechism warns against it (CCC 2116).
