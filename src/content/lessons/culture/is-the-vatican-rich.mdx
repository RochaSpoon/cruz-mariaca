## The short version

The Church teaches that love for the poor "is incompatible with immoderate love of riches" and that "the goods we possess are not ours, but theirs" (CCC 2445-2446). What the Vatican owns is a fair question to ask against that standard.

## What the Church teaches

"God blesses those who come to the aid of the poor and rebukes those who turn away from them" (CCC 2443). The Church's love for the poor "is a part of her constant tradition" (CCC 2444).

"Love for the poor is incompatible with immoderate love of riches or their selfish use" (CCC 2445).

The Catechism quotes John Chrysostom: "Not to enable the poor to share in our goods is to steal from them and deprive them of life. The goods we possess are not ours, but theirs" (CCC 2446).

The works of mercy include feeding the hungry, sheltering the homeless, clothing the naked and visiting the sick (CCC 2447).

## Scripture

> "For neither was there any one needy among them. For as many as were owners of lands or houses, sold them, and brought the price of the things they sold, And laid it down before the feet of the apostles. And distribution was made to every one, according as he had need."
*Acts 4:34-35 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me to drink; I was a stranger, and you took me in: Naked, and you covered me: sick, and you visited me: I was in prison, and you came to me."
*Matthew 25:35-36 (Douay-Rheims)*

Acts 4 shows the first Christians putting their goods in common for those in need. Matthew 25 is the passage on the works of mercy.

## Why this matters today

Critics point to art, buildings and the Vatican itself and ask why the Church does not sell it all. That is a fair challenge to put against the standard in the Catechism.

Defenders point out that the Church also runs schools, hospitals and charities. This lesson does not have figures for her accounts, and any real numbers should be checked in public financial reports.

## What's at stake

If the Church preaches love for the poor and hoards wealth, she is a hypocrite. If she is open about her accounts and spends on mercy, the criticism has less force. Either way, the standard is the same.

## How to live it

- **Use the Catechism's standard** when you judge any organization, including yours.
- **Give to the poor yourself** before you critique the Church's budget (CCC 2447).
- **Look up public financial reports** of your own diocese, which is where you can see where money goes.
- **Do not repeat numbers you cannot source.**
