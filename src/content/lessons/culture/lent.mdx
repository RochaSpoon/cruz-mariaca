## The short version

Lent is forty days of prayer, fasting and almsgiving before Easter. The Church says it joins Christ's own forty days in the desert (CCC 540, 1434, 1438).

## What the Church teaches

Jesus' temptation shows how he is the Messiah, "contrary to the way Satan proposes to him." By the "solemn forty days of Lent" the Church "unites herself" to Christ's time in the desert (CCC 540).

The Catechism says Scripture and the Fathers "insist above all on three forms, fasting, prayer, and almsgiving," which express conversion toward oneself, God and others (CCC 1434).

Lent and each Friday of the year are "seasons and days of penance," suitable for "voluntary self-denial such as fasting and almsgiving" (CCC 1438).

The Code of Canon Law says abstinence from meat is for Fridays, and that "abstinence and fasting are to be observed on Ash Wednesday and Good Friday" (can. 1251). Those obligations begin at age 14 for abstinence and at majority, until the sixtieth year, for fasting (can. 1252).

Violet is the color for the season (GIRM 346).

## Scripture

> "Then Jesus was led by the spirit into the desert, to be tempted by the devil. And when he had fasted forty days and forty nights, afterwards he was hungry. And the tempter coming said to him: If thou be the Son of God, command that these stones be made bread. Who answered and said: It is written, Not in bread alone doth man live, but in every word that proceedeth from the mouth of God."
*Matthew 4:1-4 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "Now therefore saith the Lord: Be converted to me with all your heart, in fasting, and in weeping, and in mourning. And rend your hearts, and not your garments, and turn to the Lord your God: for he is gracious and merciful, patient and rich in mercy, and ready to repent of the evil."
*Joel 2:12-13 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And when you fast, be not as the hypocrites, sad. For they disfigure their faces, that they may appear unto men to fast. Amen I say to you, they have received their reward. But thou, when thou fastest anoint thy head, and wash thy face; That thou appear not to men to fast, but to thy Father who is in secret: and thy Father who seeth in secret, will repay thee."
*Matthew 6:16-18 (Douay-Rheims)*

Joel says to return to God with fasting, weeping and mourning, and to "rend your hearts and not your garments." Jesus in Matthew 6 says to fast without making a show of it.

## Why this matters today

People give up chocolate and social media and call it Lent. The Church says the point is conversion, not a diet (CCC 1434).

Some Christians say fasting is legalism. The Catholic reply is that Jesus says "when you fast," not "if" (Matthew 6:16), and that discipline serves love.

## What's at stake

If Lent is only a habit, it changes nothing. If it is a real turn toward God, it can break habits that nothing else does.

## How to live it

- **Pick three things:** one for prayer, one for fasting, one for the poor.
- **Keep your fasting hidden.** Matthew 6 says to.
- **Go to Confession** at least once before Easter.
- **Do not turn it into a contest** with friends.
