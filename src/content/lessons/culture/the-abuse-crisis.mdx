## The short version

The Catechism calls sexual abuse of minors a scandalous harm that leaves the young "scarred by it all their lives" (CCC 2389). Vos Estis Lux Mundi (2019) requires clerics to report abuse.

## What the Church teaches

"Sexual abuse perpetrated by adults on children or adolescents entrusted to their care" compounds the offense "by the scandalous harm done to the physical and moral integrity of the young, who will remain scarred by it all their lives" (CCC 2389).

Pope Francis's 2019 motu proprio says that "whenever a cleric or a member of an Institute of Consecrated Life or of a Society of Apostolic Life has notice of, or well-founded motives to believe that" abuse has been committed, "that person is obliged to report promptly the fact to the local Ordinary" (Vos Estis Lux Mundi, Art. 3 §1).

It also requires dioceses to set up "public, stable and easily accessible systems for submission of reports" (Vos Estis Lux Mundi, Art. 2 §1).

## Scripture

> "But he that shall scandalize one of these little ones that believe in me, it were better for him that a millstone should be hanged about his neck, and that he should be drowned in the depth of the sea."
*Matthew 18:6 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus warns against causing one of the little ones to sin and says it would be better for such a one to have a millstone hung around his neck.

## Why this matters today

The abuse crisis is documented by civil investigations and Church reports in many countries. This lesson does not give numbers because I did not retrieve a verified source.

If you or someone you know was abused, contact the police or a child protection service. Reporting to the Church is also possible. You do not have to handle this alone.

## What's at stake

The scandal damaged victims and trust in the Church. Honest reporting and accountability are part of the Church's own stated response.

## How to live it

- **Believe** survivors.
- **Report** abuse to the authorities.
- **Support** safe environment programs.
