## The short version

Universities arose in the Middle Ages. The Catholic Encyclopedia counts 81 founded before the Reformation, and 53 of them had a papal charter, alone or with a royal or imperial one.

## What the Church teaches

The Catholic Encyclopedia says the name was sometimes given to the schools of Athens and Alexandria, but "it is generally held that the universities first arose in the Middle Ages."

"Previous to the Reformation 81 universities were established. Of these 13 had no charter; they developed spontaneously ex consuetudine; 33 had only the papal charter; 15 were founded by imperial or royal authority; 20 by both papal and imperial (or royal) charters" (Catholic Encyclopedia, "Universities").

Paris and Bologna were the earliest models. At Bologna, non-Bolognese students formed voluntary associations "for purposes of mutual support and protection" (Catholic Encyclopedia, "Universities").

The same article notes that medieval "universitas" was a legal term for a corporate body of persons, and did not at first mean a school of all subjects.

The article says there was disagreement over who could found one: some held that only the pope could, and others that the emperor could too.

## Why this matters today

The Church was not the only force behind universities. Kings, emperors, towns and students themselves shaped them.

The Church's role was real and large: popes issued charters and many teachers were clergy.

## What's at stake

A common claim is that the Church opposed learning. The record of its role in the early universities is a significant counterexample.

## How to live it

- **Check** the founding story of any school you attend.
- **Avoid** claims that the Church founded every university or none.
