## The short version

The Catholic Encyclopedia says Christians in the East had founded hospitals before 361, and that Charlemagne ordered a hospital attached to each cathedral and monastery.

## What the Church teaches

The Catholic Encyclopedia says the first hospital's origin is disputed. It adds that "the Christians in the East had founded hospitals before Julian the Apostate came to the throne (361)" (Catholic Encyclopedia, "Hospitals").

The emperor Julian himself wrote to a pagan high priest telling him to establish a xenodochium, a hospice, in each city (Catholic Encyclopedia, "Hospitals").

Charlemagne decreed that decayed hospitals should be restored, and "further ordered that a hospital should be attached to each cathedral and monastery" (Catholic Encyclopedia, "Hospitals").

The Crusades "gave rise to various orders of chivalry which combined with military service the care of the sick." The earliest was the Order of St. John (Catholic Encyclopedia, "Hospitals").

On the Paris Hôtel-Dieu, the article quotes a historian: "soldiers and citizens, religious and laymen, Jews and Mohammedans, repaired in case of need to the Hôtel-Dieu, and all were admitted" (Catholic Encyclopedia, "Hospitals").

## Scripture

> "Then shall the king say to them that shall be on his right hand: Come, ye blessed of my Father, possess you the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world. For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me to drink; I was a stranger, and you took me in: Naked, and you covered me: sick, and you visited me: I was in prison, and you came to me. Then shall the just answer him, saying: Lord, when did we see thee hungry, and fed thee; thirsty, and gave thee drink? And when did we see thee a stranger, and took thee in? or naked, and covered thee? Or when did we see thee sick or in prison, and came to thee? And the king answering, shall say to them: Amen I say to you, as long as you did it to one of these my least brethren, you did it to me."
*Matthew 25:34-40 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus says that what you did for the least of His brethren, you did for Him. Christians cited this for care of the sick.

## Why this matters today

Medieval hospitals were not modern hospitals. They offered shelter and basic care and often reflected the best medicine of the time.

Many hospitals today still carry the names of saints or religious orders.

## What's at stake

The Church's teaching on caring for the sick has a long record in practice, as well as in words.

## How to live it

- **Visit** someone sick this week.
- **Support** a Catholic hospital or charity.
