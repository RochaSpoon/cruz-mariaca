## The short version

Pope Urban II preached the First Crusade at Clermont on November 27, 1095. The Church today separates historical fact from judgment and has asked pardon for the sins of its members (ITC, Memory and Reconciliation, 2000).

## What the Church teaches

The Catholic Encyclopedia says Urban II convened a council at Clermont-Ferrand, attended by "fourteen archbishops, 250 bishops, and 400 abbots." On 27 November 1095 he addressed the crowds, "exhorting them to go forth and rescue the Holy Sepulchre" (Catholic Encyclopedia, "Crusades").

The same article, written in 1908, calls the Crusades "essentially a papal enterprise" (Catholic Encyclopedia, "Crusades").

The International Theological Commission asks whether today's conscience can be assigned guilt for "isolated historical phenomena like the Crusades or the Inquisition." It warns against judging the past only by today's conscience (Memory and Reconciliation, 2000).

It says historical judgment must be made "without falsehoods on one side or the other." Both "an apologetics that seeks to justify everything and an unwarranted laying of blame" must be avoided (Memory and Reconciliation, 2000).

## Scripture

> "Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called children of God."
*Matthew 5:9 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus calls the peacemakers blessed. Christians have differed on how this bears on war.

## Why this matters today

Popular accounts often claim the Crusades were pure aggression or pure defense. Historians continue to argue about motives and consequences.

Violence against civilians happened in the Crusades. The Church does not defend it. See the lesson on the Church's requests for forgiveness.

## What's at stake

The task is to tell the truth about the past without excusing wrongs or inventing them.

## How to live it

- **Read** more than one historian.
- **Resist** using the Crusades as a slogan.
