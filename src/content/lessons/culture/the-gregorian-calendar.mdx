## The short version

Pope Gregory XIII reformed the calendar in 1582. Ten days were dropped: October 4 was followed by October 15 (Catholic Encyclopedia, "General Chronology").

## What the Church teaches

The Julian calendar "made the year slightly too long, with the result that by the sixteenth century it had fallen ten days in arrear" (Catholic Encyclopedia, General Chronology).

So the date of the spring equinox, which should have been March 21, "was called 11 March" (Catholic Encyclopedia, General Chronology).

Pope Gregory XIII "ordained that ten days in October, 1582, should not be counted, the fourth of that month being immediately followed by the fifteenth" (Catholic Encyclopedia, General Chronology).

He ruled that three leap years should be omitted in every four centuries, the centennial years not divisible by four, such as 1700, 1800, 1900 and 2100 (Catholic Encyclopedia, General Chronology).

"The New Style (N.S.) was speedily adopted by Catholic States, but for a long time the Protestant States retained the Old (O.S.)" (Catholic Encyclopedia, General Chronology).

## Scripture

> "All things have their season, and in their times all things pass under heaven."
*Ecclesiastes 3:1 (Douay-Rheims)*

The verse says there is a time and a season for everything under heaven.

## Why this matters today

Almost the whole world now uses the Gregorian calendar for civil purposes. The reason for the reform was practical, the date of Easter being tied to the spring equinox.

Some Orthodox Churches still use the Julian calendar for their feasts. That difference remains.

## What's at stake

This lesson is about history, not faith. It shows that the Church has long cared about astronomy and accurate time.

## How to live it

- **Remember** that today's calendar came from a papal reform.
- **Be** careful with old documents that use Old Style dates.
