## The short version

Vatican II says the Church was divided in the West by events "commonly referred to as the Reformation," and that those born into these communities "cannot be accused of the sin involved in the separation" (Unitatis Redintegratio 3).

## What the Church teaches

"In subsequent centuries much more serious dissensions made their appearance and quite large communities came to be separated from full communion with the Catholic Church - for which, often enough, men of both sides were to blame" (Unitatis Redintegratio 3).

"The children who are born into these Communities and who grow up believing in Christ cannot be accused of the sin involved in the separation, and the Catholic Church embraces upon them as brothers, with respect and affection" (Unitatis Redintegratio 3).

Christ "summons the Church, as she goes her pilgrim way, to that continual reform of which she always has need, insofar as she is a human institution here on earth" (Unitatis Redintegratio 6, quoted in Memory and Reconciliation).

The ITC calls the Reformation one of the principal divisions of the past millennium and notes that the divisions "differ greatly from one another" in their nature and gravity (Memory and Reconciliation).

## Scripture

> "And not for them only do I pray, but for them also who through their word shall believe in me; That they all may be one, as thou, Father, in me, and I in thee; that they also may be one in us; that the world may believe that thou hast sent me. And the glory which thou hast given me, I have given to them; that they may be one, as we also are one: I in them, and thou in me; that they may be made perfect in one: and the world may know that thou hast sent me, and hast loved them, as thou hast also loved me."
*John 17:20-23 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus prays that they all may be one. The division of Christians is understood by the Church as a wound to this prayer.

## Why this matters today

Many Protestants and Catholics now cooperate on moral and social issues. Real doctrinal differences remain on justification, the sacraments, authority and Mary.

Protestants ground their view in Scripture and in a different understanding of the Church. A fair account has to say this.

## What's at stake

A Catholic is asked to hold the Church's teaching and to treat other Christians as brothers and sisters.

## How to live it

- **Learn** what your Protestant friends believe, from them.
- **Pray** for Christian unity.
