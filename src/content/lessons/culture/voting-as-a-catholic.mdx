## The short version

The Catechism says it is "morally obligatory" to vote, and that every citizen must form their conscience (CCC 2240, 1783). The Church does not tell Catholics which party to join.

## What the Church teaches

"Submission to authority and co-responsibility for the common good make it morally obligatory to pay taxes, to exercise the right to vote, and to defend one's country" (CCC 2240).

"Conscience must be informed and moral judgment enlightened. A well-formed conscience is upright and truthful" (CCC 1783). Forming it is "a lifelong task" (CCC 1784).

In forming it, "the Word of God is the light for our path," and we are "aided by the witness or advice of others and guided by the authoritative teaching of the Church" (CCC 1785).

"The citizen is obliged in conscience not to follow the directives of civil authorities when they are contrary to the demands of the moral order, to the fundamental rights of persons or the teachings of the Gospel" (CCC 2242).

Vatican II says conscience is "the most secret core and sanctuary of a man," where he "is alone with God" (Gaudium et Spes 16).

## Scripture

> "They say to him: Caesar's. Then he saith to them: Render therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar's; and to God, the things that are God's."
*Matthew 22:21 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "But Peter and the apostles answering, said: We ought to obey God, rather than men."
*Acts 5:29 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus says to give to Caesar what is Caesar's and to God what is God's. Peter and the apostles say, "We ought to obey God, rather than men."

## Why this matters today

Many Catholics feel that neither party fits. That is common, and the Church does not endorse a party.

Some people say faith should stay out of politics. The Catechism says Christians owe the political community their participation (CCC 2239-2240), and that conscience must be formed by more than slogans.

## What's at stake

If you vote on slogans, someone else is forming your conscience. If you do not vote, you give your say to others.

## How to live it

- **Read the actual Church teaching** on the issues, not summaries from either side.
- **Weigh what is at stake** in each choice, and be honest about what you cannot support.
- **Pray over the decision** (CCC 1785).
- **Do not use the Church's name for your party.** Say "I think" and give your reasons.
