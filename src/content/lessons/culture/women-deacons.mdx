## The short version

The Church has not ordained women as deacons. In 2002 the International Theological Commission said early deaconesses "were not purely and simply equivalent to the deacons," and left the question to the Church's authority to decide.

## What the Church teaches

The ITC concluded: "The deaconesses mentioned in the tradition of the ancient Church - as evidenced by the rite of institution and the functions they exercised - were not purely and simply equivalent to the deacons" (From the Diakonia of Christ to the Diakonia of the Apostles, 2002).

It also stressed "the unity of the sacrament of Holy Orders, in the clear distinction between the ministries of the bishop and the priests on the one hand and the diaconal ministry on the other."

It said the question "pertains to the ministry of discernment which the Lord established in his Church to pronounce authoritatively."

Pope Francis set up a study commission in 2016 and another in 2020. Its report was sent to Pope Leo XIV in September 2025, and a synthesis was published on December 4, 2025 (Catholic News Agency; USCCB).

The published synthesis voted against admitting women to the diaconate as a degree of Holy Orders. It said this was not a definitive judgment. It called for expanding ministries open to women and further study (USCCB; Catholic News Agency).

## Scripture

> "And I commend to you Phebe, our sister, who is in the ministry of the church, that is in Cenchrae: That you receive her in the Lord as becometh saints; and that you assist her in whatsoever business she shall have need of you. For she also hath assisted many, and myself also."
*Romans 16:1-2 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul commends Phoebe, a servant (diakonos) of the church at Cenchrae. Scholars debate what office this describes.

## Why this matters today

Supporters of ordaining women deacons point to early deaconesses and to Phoebe. The Church's study commissions read the evidence as showing a different office.

Women already hold many roles in parishes, schools, hospitals and Vatican offices. The question here is sacramental ordination.

## What's at stake

The question has not been settled by a definitive act. The Church asks for patience and honesty about what the evidence shows.

## How to live it

- **Read** the ITC document's conclusion.
- **Avoid** claims that the Church has "always" or "never" had deaconesses without sources.
