## The short version

Gluttony is an inordinate desire for food or drink. It's not enjoying a good meal. It's when your stomach is in charge instead of you. St. Paul says of some people that "their God is their belly."

## What the Church teaches

Gluttony is a capital sin (CCC 1866). The virtue that fights it is temperance, which "ensures the will's mastery over instincts and keeps desires within the limits of what is honorable" (CCC 1809). The virtue of temperance disposes us to avoid every kind of excess: food, alcohol, tobacco, or medicine (CCC 2290).

## Scripture

> "Whose end is destruction; whose God is their belly; and whose glory is in their shame; who mind earthly things."
*Philippians 3:19 (Douay-Rheims)*

St. Paul compares the Christian to an athlete: "every one that striveth for the mastery, refraineth himself from all things" (1 Corinthians 9:25).

## The Fathers and saints

St. Gregory the Great listed five ways to commit gluttony, and Aquinas adopted them (*ST* II-II, q. 148, a. 4). In Latin: *praepropere, laute, nimis, ardenter, studiose*. That means eating:

1. **Too soon** (before the proper time, constant snacking)
2. **Too expensively** (luxury for its own sake)
3. **Too much**
4. **Too eagerly** (wolfing it down, can't wait)
5. **Too daintily** (being fussy and demanding about food)

Note number 5. Being obsessive and picky about food is a form of gluttony too, not just overeating.

## Why this hits hard today

Ultra-processed food is engineered to override your fullness signals. Food delivery puts anything in your hands at any hour. And there's an opposite trap: making food and the body an idol through extreme diet culture. Both make the body the boss.

Note: Gluttony is a moral question about self-mastery. It is not a judgment on anyone's weight or body type, and eating disorders are medical issues that need real care, not shame.

## What's at stake

Gluttony is usually a venial sin, but it's a capital sin because it opens the door to others. A person who can't say no to his stomach will struggle to say no to lust, drunkenness, and laziness. Self-mastery in small things is training for big ones.

## How to fight it

- **Keep the Church's fasts.** Ash Wednesday and Good Friday fasting, Friday abstinence, the one-hour fast before Communion.
- **Pray before meals.** It puts food back in its place: a gift.
- **Pick a small daily "no."** Skip seconds, skip a snack, drink water instead of soda.
- **Eat at the table with people**, not alone in front of a screen.
