## The short version

The Catechism says hatred of God "comes from pride." It is "contrary to love of God, whose goodness it denies" (CCC 2094). Aquinas asks whether it is the greatest of sins (ST II-II q.34 a.2).

## What the Church teaches

Among the sins against God's love the Catechism lists "hatred of God," which "comes from pride. It is contrary to love of God, whose goodness it denies, and whom it presumes to curse as the one who forbids sins and inflicts punishments" (CCC 2094).

It also lists indifference, ingratitude, lukewarmness and acedia (CCC 2094).

Aquinas asks in the Summa whether it is possible to hate God (ST II-II, q.34, a.1) and whether hatred of God is the greatest of sins (a.2).

The opposite of hatred is the first commandment's love: "you shall love the Lord your God with all your heart" (CCC 2093).

## Scripture

> "Jesus said to him: Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with thy whole heart, and with thy whole soul, and with thy whole mind. This is the greatest and the first commandment."
*Matthew 22:37-38 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus calls loving God with all your heart the greatest and first commandment.

## Why this matters today

Few people say they hate God. Many are angry at God for suffering or for what they think he asked of them. The Church distinguishes anger from hatred, and the lesson on resentment toward God covers that.

Contempt for God can grow from small habits: refusing to pray, mocking, resenting being told what to do.

## What's at stake

Hatred of God starts small and grows. The Church treats it as the root of a great deal else, and traces it to pride.

## How to fight it

- **Tell God** what you are angry about.
- **Do not mock** what is holy.
- **Pray** for the grace to love him.
- **Go to Confession.**
