## The short version

The cure for envy is "good will," rejoicing in others' good and doing good to them. The Catechism says the baptized "should struggle against it by exercising good will" (CCC 2540).

## What the Church teaches

"Envy represents a form of sadness and therefore a refusal of charity; the baptized person should struggle against it by exercising good will" (CCC 2540).

Paul says that charity is patient and kind, and does not envy (1 Corinthians 13:4).

He also says to rejoice with those who rejoice and weep with those who weep (Romans 12:15).

The fruits of the Spirit include "kindness, goodness, generosity" (CCC 1832).

The Catechism also lists charity's fruits: "joy, peace, and mercy" (CCC 1829).

## Scripture

> "Rejoice with them that rejoice; weep with them that weep. Being of one mind one towards another. Not minding high things, but consenting to the humble. Be not wise in your own conceits."
*Romans 12:15-16 (Douay-Rheims)*

Rejoicing with others is the direct remedy for envy.

## Why this matters today

Social media makes other people's good news constant. The practice is to answer it with real congratulation.

Kindness is not softness. It is a habit of wishing and doing good.

## What's at stake

Envy makes you smaller. Kindness makes you larger.

## How to live it

- **Congratulate** someone on what you wanted.
- **Do** one quiet kindness a day.
- **Pray** for the person you envy.
- **Notice** what you have.
