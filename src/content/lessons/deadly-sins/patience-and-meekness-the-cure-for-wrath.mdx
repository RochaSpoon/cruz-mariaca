## The short version

Meekness is strength under control. Jesus says "blessed are the meek" (Matthew 5:5), and patience is listed among the fruits of the Spirit (CCC 1832). Aquinas treats clemency and meekness together (ST II-II q.157).

## What the Church teaches

Jesus says that the meek are blessed, for they shall possess the land (Matthew 5:4 in the Douay-Rheims numbering).

The Catechism lists "patience" and "gentleness" among the fruits of the Spirit (CCC 1832).

Aquinas discusses clemency and meekness in II-II q.157, asking whether they are virtues (a.2) and whether they are parts of temperance (a.3).

The Catechism says anger "is a desire for revenge," and that deliberate desire to kill or seriously wound is mortal sin (CCC 2302).

It does not condemn all anger. It condemns the desire to do evil to someone (CCC 2302).

## Scripture

> "Blessed are the meek: for they shall possess the land. Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted."
*Matthew 5:4-5 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Douay-Rheims puts the beatitude on the meek at verse 4. Many other Bibles number it verse 5.

## Why this matters today

Many people think meekness means weakness. In the Church's use it is strength that is under control and used for good.

Anger can be a signal that something is wrong. The question is what you do with it.

## What's at stake

If anger runs you, you will say and do what you regret.

## How to live it

- **Count** to ten.
- **Walk away** when you must.
- **Answer** gently.
- **Pray** for the person.
