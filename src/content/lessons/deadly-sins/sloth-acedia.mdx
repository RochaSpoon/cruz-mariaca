## The short version

The old word for sloth is *acedia*. It's not mainly about being physically lazy. It's a spiritual sadness that finds God boring, prayer pointless, and holiness too much effort. It looks like "I don't really care." It is one of the most common sins of our time, and one of the least confessed.

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism describes acedia, or spiritual sloth, as going so far as to refuse the joy that comes from God and to be repelled by divine goodness (CCC 2094). It shows up in prayer as discouragement and dryness (CCC 2733). Aquinas defines it as sorrow about spiritual good, and calls it a mortal sin in its full form, when a person deliberately turns away from the good of God because it seems burdensome (*ST* II-II, q. 35, a. 1-3).

## Scripture

> "Of the arrow that flieth in the day, of the business that walketh about in the dark: of invasion, or of the noonday devil."
*Psalm 90:6 (Douay-Rheims; Psalm 91:6)*

The desert fathers took "noonday devil" to mean acedia, the temptation that hits in the long middle of the day when the work feels endless.

> "I know thy works, that thou art neither cold, nor hot. I would thou wert cold, or hot. But because thou art lukewarm, and neither cold, nor hot, I will begin to vomit thee out of my mouth."
*Revelation 3:15-16 (Douay-Rheims)*

## The Fathers and saints

The fourth-century monk Evagrius Ponticus described the demon of acedia as the most oppressive of all demons. He said it makes the sun seem to stand still, the day feel fifty hours long, and makes the monk keep looking out the window, hate his work, and want to be anywhere else (*Praktikos* 12). That is a perfect description of doomscrolling, 1,600 years early.

## Why this hits hard today

Acedia is the background hum of modern life: endless distraction, nothing ever satisfying, skipping Mass because you "don't feel it," scrolling until 2 a.m. and waking up empty. It hides behind busyness too. You can be very productive and still be spiritually asleep.

## What's at stake

Lukewarmness is what Jesus says he will "vomit" out. Acedia leads to missing Mass, abandoning prayer, and eventually losing faith, not by dramatic rejection but by slow neglect. Many people don't walk away from God. They drift.

## How to fight it

- **Do the small thing anyway.** Pray five minutes when you don't feel like it. The feeling follows the act, not the other way around.
- **Keep a fixed time for prayer.** Same time, same place.
- **Do your duties well.** Homework, chores, your job. Faithful work fights acedia (Proverbs 6:6).
- **Get physical.** Walk, work out, work with your hands.
- **Limit your screen.** Acedia feeds on endless low-grade stimulation.
- **Remember the end.** Monks fought acedia by remembering death and judgment.
