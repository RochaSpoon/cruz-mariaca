## The short version

Temperance is mastery over the appetites, as freedom and not deprivation. The Catechism says it "moderates the attraction of pleasures and provides balance in the use of created goods" (CCC 1809).

## What the Church teaches

"Temperance is the moral virtue that moderates the attraction of pleasures and provides balance in the use of created goods. It ensures the will's mastery over instincts and keeps desires within the limits of what is honorable" (CCC 1809).

The virtue "disposes us to avoid every kind of excess: the abuse of food, alcohol, tobacco, or medicine" (CCC 2290).

Paul tells Titus that the grace of God has appeared, teaching us to renounce ungodliness and worldly desires and to live soberly, justly and godly in this world (Titus 2:11-12).

The Catechism treats fasting as one of the three forms of penance (CCC 1434).

"Gluttony" is on the list of capital sins (CCC 1866).

## Scripture

> "For the grace of God our Saviour hath appeared to all men; Instructing us, that, denying ungodliness and worldly desires, we should live soberly, and justly, and godly in this world,"
*Titus 2:11-12 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Douay-Rheims says "soberly, and justly, and godly." That is the same three-part picture as temperance, justice and religion.

## Why this matters today

Temperance is not dieting. It is freedom from being ruled by appetite.

The Church honors food and drink as gifts. The sin is excess and slavery.

## What's at stake

If appetite rules you, you will find it hard to love or pray.

## How to live it

- **Say a blessing** before meals.
- **Leave the table** a little before you are full.
- **Fast** on Fridays.
- **Enjoy** good food as a gift.
