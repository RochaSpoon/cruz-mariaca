## The short version

Anger is a passion. It isn't automatically sinful. Jesus drove the money changers out of the Temple (John 2:15). But anger becomes the deadly sin of wrath when it wants revenge, wants someone harmed, hangs on as hatred, or makes you lose control.

## What the Church teaches

Anger is "a desire for revenge" (CCC 2302). To desire vengeance in order to do evil to someone is illicit. "If anger reaches the point of a deliberate desire to kill or seriously wound a neighbor, it is gravely against charity; it is a mortal sin" (CCC 2302). Deliberate hatred of a neighbor is a sin, and gravely so when you wish him grave harm (CCC 2303).

St. Thomas Aquinas teaches that anger can be good when it follows reason: angry at the right thing, in the right amount, for justice (*ST* II-II, q. 158, a. 1-2). It is sinful when it seeks unjust punishment, or when it is excessive or out of control.

## The Fathers and saints

The desert monk John Cassian spent a whole book of his *Institutes* (Book VIII) on anger. His point: anger blinds the mind, so a man consumed by it can't pray, can't see truth, and can't hear God. He says not to excuse anger by blaming whoever provoked you. The provocation only revealed what was already in you.

St. Francis de Sales advises in *Introduction to the Devout Life* (Part III, ch. 8) that it is better to refuse entrance to even the least semblance of anger, because once it enters it is hard to get rid of. He cites Augustine for this.

## Why this hits hard today

Outrage is a business model. Algorithms push whatever makes you angriest, because anger keeps you scrolling. Politics, comment sections, sports, family group chats. People who would never punch someone will write things online that are hateful. Road rage, gaming rage, rage at parents. A lot of it feels "justified." Most of it isn't.

## What's at stake

Jesus ties anger directly to the Fifth Commandment and to hell fire. Murder starts in the heart. Unchecked wrath ruins marriages, destroys friendships, and leads to violence. And if you refuse to forgive, Jesus says the Father will not forgive you (Matthew 6:15).

## How to fight it

- **Slow down.** Before you respond, wait. Ten breaths, ten minutes, or until tomorrow.
- **Don't type angry.** Leave the comment unwritten.
- **Ask: angry at what?** Real injustice or my pride getting hit?
- **Settle it before sundown.** Apologize, or at least give it to God.
- **Pray for the person** who made you angry (Matthew 5:44).
- **Watch HALT:** hungry, angry, lonely, tired. Anger spikes when you're depleted.
- **Fight for justice with justice.** Righteous anger acts calmly and fairly.
