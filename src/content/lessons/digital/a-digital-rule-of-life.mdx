## The short version

A rule of life for screens means deciding in advance when and how you will use them. Paul says to make the most of the time (Ephesians 5:15-16), and the Catechism asks for "moderation and discipline" (CCC 2496).

## What the Church teaches

Paul tells the Ephesians to look carefully how they walk, not as unwise but as wise, making good use of the time, because the days are evil (Ephesians 5:15-16).

"Users should practice moderation and discipline in their approach to the mass media. They will want to form enlightened and correct consciences the more easily to resist unwholesome influences" (CCC 2496).

Pope Leo XIV says a healthy attitude needs "rhythms that incorporate silence, in-depth study, reading and judicious analysis" (Magnifica Humanitas 146).

The Catechism treats human life as having "a rhythm of work and rest" (CCC 2184).

A rule is a short list you actually keep: times for prayer, times for screens, times for people, and a way of checking in.

## Scripture

> "See therefore, brethren, how you walk circumspectly: not as unwise, But as wise: redeeming the time, because the days are evil. Wherefore become not unwise, but understanding what is the will of God."
*Ephesians 5:15-17 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Douay-Rheims says "redeeming the time." Other translations say it in different words, but the meaning is the same: use the time well.

## Why this matters today

Most people have no plan for screens. The defaults are set by someone else.

A rule need not be strict. It needs to be yours and to be kept.

## What's at stake

If you do not set limits, the apps will.

## How to live it

- **Write down three rules:** one for morning, one for meals, one for night.
- **Tell a friend** and check in weekly.
- **Replace** one hour of scrolling with prayer, reading or people.
- **Adjust** after a month.
