## The short version

You were made for God, and nothing else fills you. Doomscrolling is the attempt to fill that hunger with an endless supply of tiny distractions. It never works, and it leaves you tired, anxious, and further from prayer. The old word for this restless boredom with God is *acedia*.

## What the Church teaches

Acedia is spiritual sloth: it goes so far as to refuse the joy that comes from God (CCC 2094). The Catechism also teaches that the media should be used with moderation and discipline so they don't make us passive consumers (CCC 2496).

Pope Leo XIV's 2026 encyclical *Magnifica Humanitas*, on the human person in the time of artificial intelligence, warns that "platforms and services are often designed to capture users' time and attention, exploiting their vulnerabilities and weakening their inner freedom" (no. 170). It calls for "rhythms that incorporate silence, in-depth study, reading and judicious analysis, for without these elements inner freedom may be compromised" (no. 146).

## Why this hits hard today

Apps are designed to keep you scrolling. Infinite feeds, autoplay, and notifications are built on the same principles as slot machines. Social psychologist Jonathan Haidt argues in *The Anxious Generation* (2024) that the shift to phone-based childhood in the early 2010s lines up with sharp rises in teen anxiety and depression. Teens now average several hours a day on screens, outside of school.

## What's at stake

Time is the stuff your life is made of. Every hour you scroll is an hour you won't get back, and you will give an account of how you used it. Doomscrolling also opens the door to other sins: envy, lust, anger, and skipping prayer because you're "too tired."

## How to fight it

- **First and last thing: God, not the phone.** Pray before you look at a screen in the morning, and after you put it down at night.
- **Phone out of the bedroom.**
- **Turn on grayscale.** It makes the phone less appealing.
- **Delete the worst app** for one week. See what happens.
- **Replace it:** a book, a walk, a call to a friend, a Rosary.
- **Keep a real Sabbath.** Sunday as a screen-light day.
- **Use this site's Daily Bread,** which ends on purpose.
