## The short version

Gambling mechanics hidden in games and apps raise the same moral issues as other gambling. The Catechism says "the passion for gambling risks becoming an enslavement" (CCC 2413).

## What the Church teaches

"Games of chance (card games, etc.) or wagers are not in themselves contrary to justice. They become morally unacceptable when they deprive someone of what is necessary to provide for his needs and those of others. The passion for gambling risks becoming an enslavement" (CCC 2413).

A loot box charges real money for a chance at a prize. That is a wager in structure, even if it is dressed as a game.

The Catechism says "unfair wagers and cheating at games constitute grave matter" (CCC 2413).

Magnifica Humanitas says platforms that profit from weakness make the person "a means rather than... an end" and that "those who design or finance such systems bear a moral responsibility that cannot be ignored" (Magnifica Humanitas 170).

## Scripture

> "For they that will become rich, fall into temptation, and into the snare of the devil, and into many unprofitable and hurtful desires, which drown men into destruction and perdition. For the desire of money is the root of all evils; which some coveting have erred from the faith, and have entangled themselves in many sorrows."
*1 Timothy 6:9-10 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says the desire to be rich is a snare.

## Why this matters today

Many apps use random rewards, near-misses and countdowns to make you spend. Children are especially exposed.

If you have spent money you could not afford, stop and tell someone. Many places have helplines for gambling problems.

## What's at stake

These systems are built by people who study how to keep you spending. You do not have to take part.

## How to live it

- **Do not spend** on random rewards.
- **Turn off** in-app purchases and set a limit for children.
- **Delete** an app that gets a hold on you.
- **Tell someone** if you have lost control.
