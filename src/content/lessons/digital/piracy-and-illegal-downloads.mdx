## The short version

Taking music, films, software or books without paying breaks the seventh commandment. The Catechism says "any form of unjustly taking and keeping the property of others is against the seventh commandment" (CCC 2408-2409).

## What the Church teaches

"The seventh commandment forbids theft, that is, usurping another's property against the reasonable will of the owner" (CCC 2408).

"Any form of unjustly taking and keeping the property of others is against the seventh commandment" (CCC 2409).

The Catechism's list of examples is wide: "deliberate retention of goods lent or of objects lost; business fraud; paying unjust wages" (CCC 2409). The principle covers creative work, which belongs to those who made it.

The Catechism says "the right to private property, acquired by work... does not do away with the original gift of the earth to the whole of mankind" (CCC 2403). Where a work is freely offered, you may take it. Where it is for sale, you pay.

If you have taken what was not yours, make restitution (CCC 2412).

## Scripture

> "Thou shalt not steal."
*Exodus 20:15 (Douay-Rheims)*

"You shall not steal" is the seventh commandment in the Catholic numbering.

## Why this matters today

People say it is not stealing because the creator still has the work. The Catechism asks about the owner's reasonable will, not about whether the item is gone (CCC 2408).

Creators and workers in the industry depend on payment. The issue is justice to them.

## What's at stake

If you take what you like for free, you are teaching yourself that other people's work is yours.

## How to live it

- **Pay for** what you use.
- **Use legal free sources** and libraries.
- **Delete** pirated copies and make it right where you can.
- **Support** the artists you like.
