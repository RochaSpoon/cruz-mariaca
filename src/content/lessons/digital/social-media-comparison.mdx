## The short version

Comparison feeds envy. The Catechism calls envy "sadness at the sight of another's goods," and Paul says each should test his own work and not compare himself to another (CCC 2539; Galatians 6:4).

## What the Church teaches

"Envy is a capital sin. It refers to the sadness at the sight of another's goods and the immoderate desire to acquire them for oneself, even unjustly" (CCC 2539).

"Envy represents a form of sadness and therefore a refusal of charity; the baptized person should struggle against it by exercising good will. Envy often comes from pride" (CCC 2540).

Paul says each person should prove his own work, and then he will have glory in himself alone and not in another (Galatians 6:4).

The tenth commandment "requires that envy be banished from the human heart" (CCC 2538).

## Scripture

> "But let every one prove his own work, and so he shall have glory in himself only, and not in another. For every one shall bear his own burden."
*Galatians 6:4-5 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says each one shall bear his own burden. The Church takes this as freeing us from measuring ourselves against others.

## Why this matters today

You see other people's best moments all day. That is not a fair comparison.

Most posts are edited. The Church's remedy is to practice good will toward the person (CCC 2540).

## What's at stake

If you measure yourself against a feed, you will lose every time.

## How to live it

- **Notice** when a post makes you feel smaller.
- **Wish them well** in one sentence.
- **Unfollow** if it keeps happening.
- **Thank God** for one thing you have.
