## The short version

Games are not a sin. The Church asks whether they have become an idol, and whether they crowd out prayer, rest, people and duty (CCC 2113, 2184-2185; 1 Corinthians 10:31).

## What the Church teaches

Paul says: "Whether you eat or drink, or whatsoever else you do, do all to the glory of God" (1 Corinthians 10:31).

The Catechism says idolatry "consists in divinizing what is not God" and includes honoring "power, pleasure... money" in place of him (CCC 2113).

It says human life "has a rhythm of work and rest" and asks for "appropriate relaxation of mind and body" (CCC 2184-2185). Play is part of that.

The Catechism says temperance "provides balance in the use of created goods" (CCC 1809).

Gambling-style mechanics are a separate problem. See the lesson on online gambling.

## Scripture

> "Therefore, whether you eat or drink, or whatsoever else you do, do all to the glory of God."
*1 Corinthians 10:31 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says to do everything for the glory of God. That includes play.

## Why this matters today

Many games are made to keep you playing, with rewards, streaks and social pressure. The Church's concern is freedom and balance.

If gaming is crowding out sleep, school, work or people, or if you feel you cannot stop, treat it as a real problem and ask for help. This lesson makes no medical claims.

## What's at stake

Time is the one thing you cannot get back.

## How to live it

- **Set a stopping time** before you start.
- **Play with real friends** where you can.
- **Keep prayer and sleep first.**
- **Ask someone** to tell you honestly if it is too much.
