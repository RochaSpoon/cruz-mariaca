## The short version

The Church has no ruling on specific films or games. It asks you to choose what you watch with a formed conscience, and Paul tells Christians to think on what is true, honorable and pure (Philippians 4:8; CCC 2496).

## What the Church teaches

Paul says to think about whatever is true, honorable, just, pure, lovable and of good repute (Philippians 4:8).

The Catechism says users of media "will want to form enlightened and correct consciences the more easily to resist unwholesome influences" (CCC 2496).

Vatican II said "the narration, description or portrayal of moral evil, even through the media of social communication, can indeed serve to bring about a deeper knowledge and study of humanity" and can be done well, but that it must respect moral norms (Inter Mirifica 7).

The Council also asked viewers to "fully favor those presentations that are outstanding for their moral goodness, knowledge and artistry" (Inter Mirifica 9).

The Catechism condemns cruelty itself: torture, terrorism and violence against the person are "morally wrong" (CCC 2297).

## Scripture

> "For the rest, brethren, whatsoever things are true, whatsoever modest, whatsoever just, whatsoever holy, whatsoever lovely, whatsoever of good fame, if there be any virtue, if any praise of discipline, think on these things."
*Philippians 4:8 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul's list is a test for what you let into your mind.

## Why this matters today

Violent stories are as old as the Bible, and war and tragedy appear in Scripture. The question is how they are told and what they do to you.

If a game or film leaves you numb, cruel or anxious, it is worth asking whether it is good for you. This lesson makes no medical claims.

## What's at stake

What you look at shapes what you think is normal.

## How to live it

- **Ask** what a film does to you afterward.
- **Choose** stories that treat suffering seriously.
- **Check** age ratings for children.
- **Talk** about what you watch with someone.
