## The short version

The Church holds that God can be known by reason from creation, and that atheism takes many forms. She also says believers can bear some blame for it (CCC 31-36, 2123-2128).

## What the Church teaches

"The Church... holds and teaches that God, the first principle and last end of all things, can be known with certainty from the created world by the natural light of human reason" (CCC 36).

The "proofs" for God's existence are "converging and convincing arguments," not proofs in the sense of the natural sciences (CCC 31).

The Catechism says "the name 'atheism' covers many very different phenomena," including practical materialism and "atheistic humanism" (CCC 2124).

It says atheism is "a sin against the virtue of religion," but that the blame "can be significantly diminished in virtue of the intentions and the circumstances" (CCC 2125).

And it admits: "Believers can have more than a little to do with the rise of atheism. To the extent that they are careless about their instruction in the faith, or present its teaching falsely, or even fail in their religious, moral, or social life, they must be said to conceal rather than to reveal the true nature of God and of religion" (CCC 2125).

On agnosticism: it "can sometimes include a certain search for God," but it can also express "indifferentism, a flight from the ultimate question of existence" (CCC 2128).

## Scripture

> "But all men are vain, in whom there is not the knowledge of God: and who by these good things that are seen, could not understand him that is, neither by attending to the works have acknowledged who was the workman: But have imagined either the fire, or the wind, or the swift air, or the circle of the stars, or the great water, or the sun and moon, to be the gods that rule the world. With whose beauty, if they, being delighted, took them to be gods: let them know how much the Lord of them is more beautiful than they: for the first author of beauty made all those things. Or if they admired their power and their effects, let them understand by them, that he that made them, is mightier than they: For by the greatness of the beauty, and of the creature, the creator of them may be seen, so as to be known thereby."
*Wisdom 13:1-5 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "Because that which is known of God is manifest in them. For God hath manifested it unto them. For the invisible things of him, from the creation of the world, are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made; his eternal power also, and divinity: so that they are inexcusable."
*Romans 1:19-20 (Douay-Rheims)*

Both passages say creation points to a maker. The Church takes them as the Bible's own basis for natural knowledge of God.

## Why this matters today

Many atheists have honest reasons for their view. The Catechism says believers can be part of the problem (CCC 2125).

Many agnostics are seekers. The Church says agnosticism can include a search for God (CCC 2128).

## What's at stake

If you argue to win, you may lose the person. The Church says to take the objections seriously and to live the faith honestly.

## How to live it

- **Ask what they actually do not believe,** and why.
- **Read the best atheist objections** and the best replies.
- **Admit where Christians have done harm.** The Catechism does (CCC 2125).
- **Be a decent friend** first.
