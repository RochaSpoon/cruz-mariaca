## The short version

The Church has forbidden Catholics to join Masonic associations. A 1983 declaration says their principles have always been considered irreconcilable with the doctrine of the Church, and membership remains forbidden.

## What the Church teaches

In a declaration of 26 November 1983, the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith said that "the Church's negative judgment in regard to Masonic association remains unchanged since their principles have always been considered irreconcilable with the doctrine of the Church and therefore membership in them remains forbidden."

It adds: "The faithful who enrol in Masonic associations are in a state of grave sin and may not receive Holy Communion."

It also says that "it is not within the competence of local ecclesiastical authorities to give a judgment on the nature of Masonic associations" that would depart from this.

The declaration was issued because the 1983 Code of Canon Law did not mention Masonic groups by name. It explains that this was "an editorial criterion" and not a change of position.

## Why this matters today

Members often say their lodge is charitable and is not a religion. The declaration's concern is the principles of the association, not the character of individual members.

Many Catholics are surprised to learn of the ban, because the lodge is often a family tradition. A priest can help sort that out.

## What's at stake

This is about where your deepest loyalties lie. The Church says Masonic principles conflict with the faith.

## How to live it

- **Read the declaration,** which is short.
- **Talk to a priest** if you or a relative is in a lodge.
- **Be kind to members.** The question is about the association, not the people.
- **Look for charitable work** in the Church.
