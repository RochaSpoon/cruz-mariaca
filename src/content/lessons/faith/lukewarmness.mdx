## The short version

The Church calls lukewarmness "hesitation or negligence in responding to divine love" (CCC 2094). Revelation says God would rather you be cold or hot than lukewarm.

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism lists sins against God's love: indifference, ingratitude, and "lukewarmness," which it defines as "hesitation or negligence in responding to divine love; it can imply refusal to give oneself over to the prompting of charity" (CCC 2094).

Revelation addresses the church at Laodicea: "Because thou art lukewarm, and neither cold, nor hot, I will begin to vomit thee out of my mouth" (Apocalypse 3:16, Douay-Rheims).

The Catechism also names the related sin of acedia, or spiritual sloth, which "goes so far as to refuse the joy that comes from God and to be repelled by divine goodness" (CCC 2094).

Dryness in prayer is not lukewarmness. The Catechism describes dryness as a moment of "sheer faith clinging faithfully to Jesus" (CCC 2731).

## Scripture

> "I know thy works, that thou art neither cold, nor hot. I would thou wert cold, or hot. But because thou art lukewarm, and neither cold, nor hot, I will begin to vomit thee out of my mouth."
*Apocalypse 3:15-16 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Laodiceans thought they were doing well. The letter says they did not see their own need.

## Why this matters today

Lukewarmness rarely feels like a sin. It feels like being busy, or being reasonable, while prayer and Mass slowly shrink.

Many people drift this way without ever deciding to leave.

## What's at stake

The danger is not a dramatic rejection. It is a slow loss of attention to the one you said you love.

## How to live it

- **Pick one habit** (daily prayer, Sunday Mass) and keep it for a month.
- **Tell a friend** what you are trying.
- **Go to Confession.** It is one of the Church's ways of starting again.
- **Ask what you would change** if you took this seriously.
