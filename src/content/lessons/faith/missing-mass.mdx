## The short version

Catholics are required to attend Mass every Sunday and every Holy Day of Obligation. Skipping on purpose without a serious reason is a grave sin. This isn't the Church being controlling. It's the bare minimum of keeping the Third Commandment, and it's how Christ chose to feed you.

## What the Church teaches

"On Sundays and other holy days of obligation the faithful are bound to participate in the Mass" (can. 1247; CCC 2180). "Those who deliberately fail in this obligation commit a grave sin" (CCC 2181). Saturday evening vigil Masses count (can. 1248).

What counts as a serious reason? Illness, caring for a sick person or a baby, or no reasonable way to get there. Being tired, having homework, or a sports tournament is not, unless there was truly no other Mass you could go to (CCC 2181).

## Scripture and history

> "Remember that thou keep holy the sabbath day."
*Exodus 20:8 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "Not forsaking our assembly, as some are accustomed."
*Hebrews 10:25 (Douay-Rheims)*

Around the year 155, St. Justin Martyr wrote to the Roman emperor describing Christian worship: on "the day called Sunday," all Christians gathered in one place, the writings of the apostles and prophets were read, the presider preached, prayers were offered, and bread and wine were brought and given thanks over (*First Apology* 67). It's the Mass, in the second century.

In 304, during Diocletian's persecution, 49 Christians in Abitinae (in North Africa) were arrested for celebrating Sunday Mass, which was illegal. According to the Acts of the Martyrs of Abitinae, when they were asked why they had disobeyed, they answered: "*Sine dominico non possumus*." The phrase is usually translated "We cannot do without the Lord's Day," and some read "dominico" as the Eucharist itself. They are honored as martyrs, and their feast is February 12.

## Why this hits hard today

Most Catholics in the U.S. don't go to Mass weekly. Sunday has become a day for sports, brunch, homework, and sleeping in. Many Catholics have never been told it's a serious obligation.

## What's at stake

Deliberately missing Mass is grave matter. If you do it knowingly and freely, you need to go to Confession before receiving Communion again. Beyond the rule: you're skipping the one hour a week when Christ gives you his own Body and Blood. People who stop going to Mass usually stop believing. It doesn't happen the other way around as often.

## How to keep it

- **Plan it first.** Put Mass on the calendar before anything else that weekend.
- **Traveling?** Search masstimes.org.
- **Go Saturday night** if Sunday's packed.
- **Bring a friend.**
- **Learn the Mass,** so you know what's happening (see "The Mass, Explained" on this site).
