## The short version

The Catechism rejects "conjuring up the dead" and recourse to mediums. A Ouija board is used for exactly that (CCC 2116).

## What the Church teaches

"All forms of divination are to be rejected: recourse to Satan or demons, conjuring up the dead or other practices falsely supposed to 'unveil' the future" (CCC 2116).

The Catechism also lists "recourse to mediums" (CCC 2116). A Ouija board is a device for contacting spirits, so it falls under these.

It condemns "all practices of magic or sorcery, by which one attempts to tame occult powers" (CCC 2117).

The Old Testament forbids consulting the dead too, as the passage below shows (Deuteronomy 18:10-12).

## Scripture

> "Neither let there be found among you any one that shall expiate his son or daughter, making them to pass through the fire: or that consulteth soothsayers, or observeth dreams and omens, neither let there be any wizard, Nor charmer, nor any one that consulteth pythonic spirits, or fortune tellers, or that seeketh the truth from the dead. For the Lord abhorreth all these things, and for these abominations he will destroy them at thy coming."
*Deuteronomy 18:10-12 (Douay-Rheims)*

In Deuteronomy, consulting the dead is listed with child sacrifice and sorcery as practices God hates.

## Why this matters today

Most people treat it as a party game. The Church treats it as an invitation to spirits whose identity you cannot check.

Many people who have used one report that nothing happened. The Catechism's warning does not depend on results. It is about what you are asking for.

## What's at stake

If you open a door to something you do not understand, you do not control what comes through. The Church says do not open it.

## How to live it

- **Do not play.** Say no kindly.
- **If you have used one,** go to Confession and talk to a priest.
- **Throw it out.**
- **Replace it with a game** that is only a game.
