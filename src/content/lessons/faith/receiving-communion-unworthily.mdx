## The short version

The Eucharist is Jesus himself: Body, Blood, Soul, and Divinity. If you're aware of mortal sin you haven't confessed, you must not receive Communion. Go to Confession first. Receiving anyway is the sin of sacrilege.

## What the Church teaches

"Anyone conscious of a grave sin must receive the sacrament of Reconciliation before coming to communion" (CCC 1385; can. 916). The only exception is a grave reason with no chance to confess, and even then you must make an act of perfect contrition and intend to confess as soon as possible (can. 916).

You should also fast from food and drink (except water and medicine) for one hour before receiving (can. 919). Non-Catholics normally don't receive, because Communion is a sign of unity in one faith. Orthodox Christians are a special case under can. 844.

## Scripture

> "Therefore whosoever shall eat this bread, or drink the chalice of the Lord unworthily, shall be guilty of the body and of the blood of the Lord. But let a man prove himself: and so let him eat of that bread, and drink of the chalice. For he that eateth and drinketh unworthily, eateth and drinketh judgment to himself, not discerning the body of the Lord."
*1 Corinthians 11:27-29 (Douay-Rheims)*

St. Paul adds in the next verse that this is why many in Corinth were sick and some had died (11:30).

## The Fathers

> "Let no one eat or drink of your Thanksgiving (Eucharist), but they who have been baptized into the name of the Lord."
*Didache 9.5 (Riddle translation)*

St. Justin Martyr wrote around 155 AD that no one may share in the Eucharist except the one who believes what Christians teach, has been baptized, and lives as Christ commanded. He says this food is not common bread but the flesh and blood of the incarnate Jesus (*First Apology* 66).

## Why this hits hard today

In many parishes, everyone goes up to Communion, and lines for Confession are short. Social pressure makes it awkward to stay in the pew. A 2019 Pew survey found that about two-thirds of U.S. Catholics believe the bread and wine are only symbols, and many of them didn't know the Church teaches otherwise.

## What's at stake

Receiving Christ while knowingly in mortal sin is a sacrilege, a grave sin against the holiest thing on earth. You're bringing Christ into a soul that has, by its own choice, shut him out.

## What to do

- **Staying in the pew is normal and good.** Nobody's watching as much as you think. Make a spiritual communion instead: "My Jesus, I believe you are present in the Most Holy Sacrament. I love you above all things, and I desire to receive you into my soul. Since I cannot now receive you sacramentally, come at least spiritually into my heart."
- **Many parishes have Confession** before weekend Masses. Go early.
- **If you received unworthily,** confess that too.
