## The short version

Simony is buying or selling spiritual things. The name comes from Simon the magician, whom Peter rebuked (CCC 2121; Acts 8:18-20).

## What the Church teaches

"Simony is defined as the buying or selling of spiritual things" (CCC 2121).

Simon the magician wanted to buy spiritual power. Peter answered, "Your silver perish with you, because you thought you could obtain God's gift with money!" (CCC 2121).

Peter was holding to Jesus' words: "You received without pay, give without pay" (CCC 2121).

Canon law adds that "it is absolutely forbidden to sell sacred relics" (can. 1190 §1).

The Catechism lists simony with tempting God and sacrilege among the sins of irreligion (CCC 2118).

## Scripture

> "And when Simon saw, that by the imposition of the hands of the apostles, the Holy Ghost was given, he offered them money, Saying: Give me also this power, that on whomsoever I shall lay my hands, he may receive the Holy Ghost. But Peter said to him: Keep thy money to thyself, to perish with thee, because thou hast thought that the gift of God may be purchased with money."
*Acts 8:18-20 (Douay-Rheims)*

Simon offers the apostles money for the power to give the Holy Spirit. The word "simony" comes from him.

## Why this matters today

Peter's rule is "give without pay." A free gift to the Church is a different thing from putting a price on grace.

The rule protects the idea that grace is a gift.

## What's at stake

If grace could be bought, the rich would have more of it. The Church says it is a gift.

## How to live it

- **Never pay for a sacrament.** Offerings are free-will.
- **Do not buy or sell relics.**
- **Be wary of anyone** who sells blessings or healings.
- **Give freely** to the Church, as you are able.
