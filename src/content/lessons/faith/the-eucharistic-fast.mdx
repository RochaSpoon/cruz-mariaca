## The short version

Catholics fast from food and drink for at least one hour before communion, with water and medicine allowed (can. 919). The Catechism says the faithful should observe the fast their Church requires (CCC 1387).

## What the Church teaches

Canon 919 says: "A person who is to receive the Most Holy Eucharist is to abstain for at least one hour before holy communion from any food and drink, except for only water and medicine" (can. 919 §1).

The Catechism says: "To prepare for worthy reception of this sacrament, the faithful should observe the fast required in their Church" (CCC 1387).

It adds that "bodily demeanor (gestures, clothing) ought to convey the respect, solemnity, and joy of this moment when Christ becomes our guest" (CCC 1387).

A priest who celebrates Mass two or three times in a day may take something between Masses (can. 919 §2).

The fast is separate from the requirement to be in a state of grace (CCC 1415).

## Scripture

> "But let a man prove himself: and so let him eat of that bread, and drink of the chalice."
*1 Corinthians 11:28 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says a man should prove himself before he eats the bread and drinks the cup.

## Why this matters today

The rule in canon law is one hour, with water and medicine allowed (can. 919).

The point is preparation, not punishment.

## What's at stake

The fast is small on purpose. It is a way to make room for something important.

## How to live it

- **Check the time** before Mass.
- **Take medicine and water** as needed.
- **Use the hour** to pray.
- **If you are ill,** do not worry about it. Talk to your priest.
