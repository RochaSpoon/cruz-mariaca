## The short version

The five precepts of the Church are the minimum duties of every Catholic: Mass on Sundays and holy days, confession once a year, communion at Easter, keep holy days, and fast and abstain on the prescribed days. A fifth duty is to provide for the Church's needs (CCC 2041-2043).

## What the Church teaches

The precepts are "meant to guarantee to the faithful the indispensable minimum in the spirit of prayer and moral effort, in the growth in love of God and neighbor" (CCC 2041).

**First:** "You shall attend Mass on Sundays and holy days of obligation" (CCC 2042).

**Second:** "You shall confess your sins at least once a year." **Third:** "You shall humbly receive your Creator in Holy Communion at least during the Easter season" (CCC 2042).

**Fourth:** "You shall keep holy the holy days of obligation." **Fifth:** "You shall observe the prescribed days of fasting and abstinence" (CCC 2043).

"The faithful also have the duty of providing for the material needs of the Church, each according to his abilities" (CCC 2043).

## Scripture

> "And I will give to thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven. And whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth, it shall be bound also in heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt loose upon earth, it shall be loosed also in heaven."
*Matthew 16:19 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus gave Peter and the apostles the power of binding and loosing (CCC 881). Canon law says the supreme authority of the Church establishes feast days and days of penance common to the whole Church (can. 1244).

## Why this matters today

The precepts are a floor, not a ceiling. The Church calls them "the indispensable minimum" (CCC 2041).

Some Catholics treat them as a checklist and others ignore them. The Church sees them as a way to keep the rhythm of the faith.

## What's at stake

If you only do the minimum, the minimum may not hold. If you ignore it, the structure that supports you is gone.

## How to live it

- **Learn the five.**
- **Go to Confession** this season.
- **Receive communion at Easter** at the least, and more often.
- **Give regularly** to your parish.
