## The short version

The Vatican's 1989 letter on Christian meditation warns against fusing Christian prayer with methods from other religions, and says God's love "cannot be mastered by any method or technique" (Orationis Formas 12, 31).

## What the Church teaches

The letter, issued by the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith in 1989, says that with "the present diffusion of eastern methods of meditation in the Christian world," there is "an attempt, which is not free from dangers and errors, to fuse Christian meditation with that which is non-Christian" (Orationis Formas 12).

It says that "the love of God, the sole object of Christian contemplation, is a reality which cannot be 'mastered' by any method or technique" (Orationis Formas 31).

A footnote defines "eastern methods" as "methods which are inspired by Hinduism and Buddhism, such as 'Zen,' 'Transcendental Meditation' or 'Yoga'" (Orationis Formas, note 1).

The letter does not condemn all physical or bodily practices. It says Eastern Christian meditation "has valued psychophysical symbolism," and that the Jesus Prayer, for example, can use rhythms of the body (Orationis Formas 27).

Prayer itself is a gift. The Catechism says contemplative prayer "is a gift, a grace; it can be accepted only in humility and poverty" (CCC 2713).

## Scripture

> "But thou when thou shalt pray, enter into thy chamber, and having shut the door, pray to thy Father in secret: and thy Father who seeth in secret will repay thee."
*Matthew 6:6 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus tells his disciples to enter their room and pray to the Father in secret. The Church's emphasis is on a relationship, not a technique.

## Why this matters today

Many Catholics do yoga for exercise and think nothing of it. The letter is about prayer methods, and says to look at whether a practice is being used as a way to God or as a way to the self.

The letter asks the Christian to be careful about where a method leads.

## What's at stake

If prayer is a technique, you are at the center. If it is a relationship, God is.

## How to live it

- **Ask what a class is actually teaching,** and whether it includes spiritual claims.
- **Keep your prayer in Christ** whatever else you do.
- **Read Orationis Formas.** It is short and calm.
- **Ask a priest** if you are unsure.
