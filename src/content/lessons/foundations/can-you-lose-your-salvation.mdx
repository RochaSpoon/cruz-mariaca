## The short version

Catholics teach that mortal sin can cause a person to lose the state of grace, and that no one can rely on feelings to know they are saved (CCC 1861, 2005). Many Protestants teach differently.

## What the Church teaches

"Mortal sin is a radical possibility of human freedom, as is love itself. It results in the loss of charity and the privation of sanctifying grace, that is, of the state of grace" (CCC 1861).

"If it is not redeemed by repentance and God's forgiveness, it causes exclusion from Christ's kingdom and the eternal death of hell" (CCC 1861).

For mortal sin, three conditions must be met: grave matter, full knowledge and deliberate consent (CCC 1857).

On assurance, the Catechism says grace "escapes our experience and cannot be known except by faith. We cannot therefore rely on our feelings or our works to conclude that we are justified and saved" (CCC 2005).

The same Catechism teaches that we can hope. We can "hope, with the grace of God, to persevere 'to the end'" (CCC 1821).

## Scripture

> "Wherefore, my dearly beloved, (as you have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but much more now in my absence,) with fear and trembling work out your salvation. For it is God who worketh in you, both to will and to accomplish, according to his good will."
*Philippians 2:12-13 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "But I chastise my body, and bring it into subjection: lest perhaps, when I have preached to others, I myself should become a castaway."
*1 Corinthians 9:27 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says to work out your salvation "with fear and trembling," and that he disciplines his body lest he himself be a castaway. Many Protestants read these verses differently, and also point to verses like John 10:28 on the security of believers.

## Why this matters today

Some Protestant traditions teach that a believer is always saved, meaning that a genuine believer cannot finally fall away. Others do not. That difference is real, and the teaching you meet depends on the church.

Catholics can easily go the other way and live in dread. The Church does not ask for that. She asks for vigilance and confidence in God's mercy (CCC 1821).

## What's at stake

If you cannot lose salvation, there is no reason to fear sin. If it is easy to lose, there is no reason to hope. The Church teaches that it is possible to lose, and that God's mercy is greater than our failures.

## How to live it

- **Stay close to the sacraments,** especially Confession.
- **Do not assess your standing by your feelings** (CCC 2005).
- **Hope.** The Church tells you to hope to persevere (CCC 1821).
- **Read the verses on both sides** of this question.
