## The short version

Conscience is a judgment of reason about the moral quality of an act. The Catechism says it must be informed and formed, because a badly formed conscience can mislead you (CCC 1778, 1783).

## What the Church teaches

"Conscience is a judgment of reason whereby the human person recognizes the moral quality of a concrete act that he is going to perform, is in the process of performing, or has already completed" (CCC 1778).

"A human being must always obey the certain judgment of his conscience. If he were deliberately to act against it, he would condemn himself" (CCC 1790).

But conscience can be wrong. "Conscience must be informed and moral judgment enlightened. A well-formed conscience is upright and truthful" (CCC 1783).

Ignorance can be blameworthy: when "a man takes little trouble to find out what is true and good, or when conscience is by degrees almost blinded through the habit of committing sin" (CCC 1791).

The sources of error include "bad example given by others, enslavement to one's passions, assertion of a mistaken notion of autonomy of conscience, rejection of the Church's authority and her teaching, lack of conversion and of charity" (CCC 1792).

In forming it, "the Word of God is the light for our path." We are "assisted by the gifts of the Holy Spirit, aided by the witness or advice of others and guided by the authoritative teaching of the Church" (CCC 1785).

"Man has the right to act in conscience and in freedom." He "must not be forced to act contrary to his conscience" (CCC 1782).

## Scripture

> "For when the Gentiles, who have not the law, do by nature those things that are of the law; these having not the law are a law to themselves: Who shew the work of the law written in their hearts, their conscience bearing witness to them, and their thoughts between themselves accusing, or also defending one another, In the day when God shall judge the secrets of men by Jesus Christ, according to my gospel."
*Romans 2:14-16 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says the Gentiles show "the work of the law written in their hearts," their conscience bearing witness.

## Why this matters today

Follow your conscience is a common phrase. The Church agrees, and adds that conscience must be formed (CCC 1783).

Some people use "my conscience" to end an argument. The Catechism says the right to follow conscience does not mean every conscience is correct (CCC 1790-1793).

## What's at stake

If conscience is only feeling, it will follow your convenience. If it is a judgment of reason that can be educated, it can grow.

## How to live it

- **Read the Gospels** regularly. The Catechism says the Word of God is the light (CCC 1785).
- **Ask someone wiser** before a big moral decision.
- **Learn what the Church actually teaches** on the question.
- **Examine your conscience nightly.** See the next lessons.
