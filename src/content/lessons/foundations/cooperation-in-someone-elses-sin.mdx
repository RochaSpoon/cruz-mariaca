## The short version

Sin is a personal act, but you can share responsibility for another's sin by ordering, advising, praising or approving it, by not hindering it when you should, or by protecting evildoers (CCC 1868).

## What the Church teaches

"Sin is a personal act. Moreover, we have a responsibility for the sins committed by others when we cooperate in them" (CCC 1868).

The ways listed are: "by participating directly and voluntarily in them; by ordering, advising, praising, or approving them; by not disclosing or not hindering them when we have an obligation to do so; by protecting evil-doers" (CCC 1868).

"Sin makes men accomplices of one another and causes concupiscence, violence, and injustice to reign among them" (CCC 1869).

"Structures of sin" are "the expression and effect of personal sins" (CCC 1869).

## Scripture

> "Who, having known the justice of God, did not understand that they who do such things, are worthy of death; and not only they that do them, but they also that consent to them that do them."
*Romans 1:32 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says that not only those who do such things deserve death, but also those who consent to them.

## Why this matters today

People say "it was not me" when something wrong happens around them. The Catechism says you can share responsibility if you praise it, hide it or fail to hinder it when you had a duty to.

The same applies in an organization, where silence can protect an abuser.

## What's at stake

If sin is only private, nobody has to speak. The Church says that silence can be part of the sin.

## How to live it

- **Do not laugh** at what is wrong.
- **Speak up** where you have the duty and the standing.
- **Report abuse.** Do not protect the guilty.
- **Keep the limit in mind:** the duty applies when you "have an obligation to do so" (CCC 1868).
