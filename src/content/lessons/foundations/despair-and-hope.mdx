## The short version

Despair is when you decide God cannot or will not forgive you. The Catechism calls it a sin against hope (CCC 2091). It is the devil's last trick: first he says your sin is no big deal, then once you've done it he says it is too big to forgive.

## What the Church teaches

Despair is when someone "ceases to hope for his personal salvation from God, for help in attaining it or for the forgiveness of his sins. Despair is contrary to God's goodness, to his justice... and to his mercy" (CCC 2091). Its mirror image is presumption: assuming you'll be saved without repentance (CCC 2092). Both are sins.

## Scripture

Peter denied Jesus three times. Judas betrayed him. Both were apostles. Peter wept and came back, and Jesus restored him three times on the beach (John 21). Judas, full of remorse, did not come back (Matthew 27:3-5). The difference was not the size of the sin. It was whether they trusted mercy.

> "A sacrifice to God is an afflicted spirit: a contrite and humbled heart, O God, thou wilt not despise."
*Psalm 50:19 (Douay-Rheims)*

## If you're struggling right now

If you're having thoughts of ending your life, please stop reading and reach out now. In the U.S., call or text **988** (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline), or call 911. Tell a parent, a priest, a teacher, or a friend tonight.

The Church teaches that grave psychological disturbance, anguish, or fear can lessen a person's responsibility (CCC 2282). It also says: "We should not despair of the eternal salvation of persons who have taken their own lives. By ways known to him alone, God can provide the opportunity for salutary repentance. The Church prays for persons who have taken their own lives" (CCC 2283).

Your life is not yours to throw away, because it is a gift, and you are loved more than you know. Stay.

## How to fight despair

- Go to Confession, even if you've confessed the same sin a hundred times. Christ told Peter to forgive "seventy times seven."
- Say it out loud: "Jesus, I trust in you."
- Tell someone what's going on. Despair grows in isolation.
- Read the end of John's Gospel (John 21) slowly.
