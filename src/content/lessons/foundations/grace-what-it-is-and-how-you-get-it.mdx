## The short version

Grace is "favor, the free and undeserved help that God gives us" to become his children (CCC 1996). You cannot earn the first grace of forgiveness (CCC 2010).

## What the Church teaches

"Our justification comes from the grace of God. Grace is favor, the free and undeserved help that God gives us to respond to his call to become children of God, adoptive sons, partakers of the divine nature and of eternal life" (CCC 1996).

"Grace is a participation in the life of God. It introduces us into the intimacy of Trinitarian life" (CCC 1997).

Sanctifying grace is "an habitual gift, a stable and supernatural disposition that perfects the soul itself." It is distinguished from "actual graces," which are God's interventions at the start of a conversion or in the course of sanctification (CCC 2000).

Even our readiness is a gift: "The preparation of man for the reception of grace is already a work of grace" (CCC 2001).

"No one can merit the initial grace of forgiveness and justification" (CCC 2010).

Grace "escapes our experience and cannot be known except by faith" (CCC 2005). It comes through the sacraments, above all Baptism (CCC 1999).

## Scripture

> "For by grace you are saved through faith, and that not of yourselves, for it is the gift of God; Not of works, that no man may glory. For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus in good works, which God hath prepared that we should walk in them."
*Ephesians 2:8-10 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "I am the vine: you the branches: he that abideth in me, and I in him, the same beareth much fruit: for without me you can do nothing."
*John 15:5 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says "by grace you are saved through faith, and that not of yourselves, for it is the gift of God." Jesus says, "without me you can do nothing."

## Why this matters today

Many people think of religion as self-improvement. The Church says you cannot make yourself good by effort alone, and that grace comes first (CCC 1998).

Many Protestants and Catholics agree on this point, that salvation is by grace. Where they differ is how grace and human cooperation relate (CCC 1993).

## What's at stake

If it is all your effort, you will fail or you will be proud. If it is all God and you do nothing, you do not grow. The Church's picture is that grace comes first and asks for a response.

## How to live it

- **Ask for grace** in every prayer, and thank him afterward.
- **Receive the sacraments.** The Catechism says grace comes through them (CCC 1999).
- **Do not trust feelings** as a measure (CCC 2005).
- **Notice what you have been given.**
