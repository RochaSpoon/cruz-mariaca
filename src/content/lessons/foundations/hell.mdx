## The short version

Hell exists. It lasts forever. The people there chose it by refusing God's love until the end. Its worst pain is the permanent loss of God, the only thing you were made for. This is defined Catholic teaching, not a medieval scare tactic.

## What the Church teaches

"The teaching of the Church affirms the existence of hell and its eternity" (CCC 1035). Souls who die in a state of mortal sin go there immediately after death. The Catechism calls the chief punishment "eternal separation from God, in whom alone man can possess the life and happiness for which he was created" (CCC 1035).

Hell is called a "state of definitive self-exclusion from communion with God and the blessed" (CCC 1033). God does not predestine anyone to go there (CCC 1037). To end up there, a person must turn away from God by grave sin and persist in it until death.

The Church has never declared that any particular person is in hell. It has also never said hell is empty. Jesus warns that the road there is wide and that many take it (Matthew 7:13).

## Scripture

> "Then he shall say to them also that shall be on his left hand: Depart from me, you cursed, into everlasting fire which was prepared for the devil and his angels... And these shall go into everlasting punishment: but the just, into life everlasting."
*Matthew 25:41, 46 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "Where their worm dieth not, and the fire is not extinguished."
*Mark 9:47 (Douay-Rheims; Mark 9:48 in modern Bibles)*

> "Enter ye in at the narrow gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the way that leadeth to destruction, and many there are who go in thereat."
*Matthew 7:13 (Douay-Rheims)*

In the story of the rich man and Lazarus (Luke 16:19-31), the rich man is in torment, he is fully conscious, he remembers his life, and there is "a great chaos" fixed so no one can cross. He was not condemned for being rich. He was condemned for stepping over a starving man at his gate every day.

## The Fathers and saints

St. Augustine devotes an entire book of *The City of God* (Book XXI) to defending the eternity of hell against people in his own day who said it must end eventually. His argument: the same word "everlasting" in Matthew 25:46 describes both heaven and hell. If one ends, so does the other.

St. Teresa of Avila (Doctor of the Church) describes in her autobiography (*Life*, ch. 32) being shown the place in hell her sins would have earned her. She says the external torments were not the worst of it. The worst was an interior fire and a despair of the soul she could not put into words. She says the vision drove her to pray and work for souls for the rest of her life.

> "Most of the souls there are those who disbelieved that there is a hell."
*St. Faustina Kowalska, Diary, 741*

At Fatima on July 13, 1917, the three children were shown hell. Sister Lucia records that Our Lady then said: "You have seen hell where the souls of poor sinners go."
*Sr. Lucia, Memoirs*

Note: Faustina and Fatima are private revelations. The Church has approved them as worthy of belief, but Catholics are not required to accept them (CCC 67). The existence and eternity of hell is required belief, because it comes from Christ himself.

## Why this matters today

Most people today assume everyone goes to heaven unless they are Hitler. Many sermons never mention hell at all. The result is that the Gospel sounds optional. If there is nothing to be saved from, there is no reason for a Savior. Jesus did not die on a cross to make nice people slightly nicer.

## What's at stake

Everything. Hell is not a timeout. It is permanent. The damned do not repent there, because the will is fixed at death. There is no second chance after death (Hebrews 9:27). The time to choose is now.

## How to respond

- Take your sins seriously. Go to Confession.
- Pray for the dying and for sinners. Many Catholics add the Fatima prayer after each decade of the Rosary, which asks Jesus to forgive our sins, save us from the fires of hell and lead all souls to heaven, especially those most in need of his mercy.
- Remember why hell is possible: because love has to be freely chosen. God respects your freedom to the end.
- Fear of hell is a real start (imperfect contrition, CCC 1453). Love of God is the goal.
