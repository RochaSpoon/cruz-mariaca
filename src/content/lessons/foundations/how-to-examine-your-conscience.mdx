## The short version

The Catechism says to prepare for confession with an examination of conscience "made in the light of the Word of God," using passages like the Sermon on the Mount (CCC 1454).

## What the Church teaches

"The reception of this sacrament ought to be prepared for by an examination of conscience made in the light of the Word of God. The passages best suited to this can be found in the moral catechesis of the Gospels and the apostolic Letters, such as the Sermon on the Mount and the apostolic teachings" (CCC 1454).

Paul tells the Corinthians to examine themselves (2 Corinthians 13:5).

Mortal sins must be confessed, even the most secret, and everyday faults are strongly recommended (CCC 1456, 1458).

One simple pattern: ask for light, go through the Ten Commandments or the Beatitudes, name what you find, ask forgiveness, and plan the next step.

Fear is not the point. The Catechism says confession "frees us and facilitates our reconciliation with others" (CCC 1455).

## Scripture

> "Try your own selves if you be in the faith; prove ye yourselves. Know you not your own selves, that Christ Jesus is in you, unless perhaps you be reprobates?"
*2 Corinthians 13:5 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul is asking the Corinthians to look honestly at their own lives.

## Why this matters today

Many Catholics avoid confession because they cannot remember what to say. A short list helps.

Others have an examen they do at night. That is a good habit too.

## What's at stake

If you do not look at your life, you cannot repent. If you look with despair, you stop.

## How to live it

- **Pray first** for light.
- **Go through the Ten Commandments** one at a time.
- **Write down** what you find.
- **Keep it short** and go to confession.
