## The short version

A mortal sin is a sin that cuts off the life of grace in your soul. If you die in it without repenting, you go to hell. A venial sin weakens you and offends God, but it does not destroy charity in the soul. Both matter. Only one is fatal.

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism says a sin is mortal when three conditions are all met (CCC 1857):

1. **Grave matter.** The act itself is seriously wrong. The Ten Commandments spell out most of it.
2. **Full knowledge.** You know it is seriously wrong.
3. **Deliberate consent.** You choose it freely anyway.

If all three are present, the sin is mortal. It "destroys charity in the heart of man" (CCC 1855). It needs the Sacrament of Confession to be forgiven in the ordinary way (CCC 1856). If you die unrepentant in mortal sin, you remain separated from God forever by your own free choice (CCC 1033, 1861).

Venial sin is when the matter is less serious, or when the matter is grave but knowledge or consent is incomplete (CCC 1862). It does not break the covenant with God, but deliberate venial sin that you never fight "disposes us little by little to commit mortal sin" (CCC 1863).

## The Fathers and saints

> "For of a perverse will was a lust made; and a lust served became custom; and custom not resisted became necessity."
*St. Augustine, Confessions VIII.5.10 (Pusey translation)*

Augustine is describing how venial habits become chains. You do not usually fall into mortal sin out of nowhere. You walk there, one small yes at a time.

## Why this matters today

Two errors are everywhere. The first says nothing is really a sin, so nobody needs to worry. The second, common among serious young Catholics, says everything is a mortal sin, so they live in fear and stop receiving Communion over things that are not grave. Both are lies. The first leads to hell. The second leads to despair and burnout. The answer is to know the actual teaching.

## What's at stake

Mortal sin is not a technicality. It is choosing something over God, knowingly, in a serious matter. Scripture says plainly that people who live this way "shall not obtain the kingdom of God." The danger is not that God is looking for a reason to condemn you. The danger is that you get exactly what you chose.

## How to fight it

- Learn what counts as grave matter. Read CCC 2052-2557 (the Ten Commandments) over a few weeks.
- Do a nightly examination of conscience. Two minutes. What did I do well, where did I fall, what will I change tomorrow.
- Fight venial sins on purpose. They are the training ground.
- Go to Confession regularly, even when you have only venial sins. It gives grace to fight.

## If you've fallen

If you are aware of mortal sin, go to Confession as soon as you can. Until then, make an act of perfect contrition (sorrow because you offended God, not only out of fear), resolve to confess, and do not receive Communion (CCC 1452, 1457). Then go. Do not put it off.
