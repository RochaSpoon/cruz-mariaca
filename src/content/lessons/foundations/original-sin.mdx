## The short version

Original sin is the condition of fallen human nature that we inherit from Adam. The Catechism says it "does not have the character of a personal fault" in us, but is a deprivation of original holiness (CCC 405).

## What the Church teaches

God made man in his friendship. The first sin was a failure of trust: "Man, tempted by the devil, let his trust in his Creator die in his heart and, abusing his freedom, disobeyed God's command" (CCC 397).

"In that sin man preferred himself to God and by that very act scorned him" (CCC 398). Adam and Eve "immediately lose the grace of original holiness" (CCC 399).

Paul says "sin came into the world through one man and death through sin, and so death spread to all men because all men sinned." The Church "has always taught" that our misery and inclination to evil cannot be understood apart from this (CCC 402-403).

How the sin is transmitted is "a mystery that we cannot fully understand." But the whole human race is "in Adam as one body of one man" (CCC 404).

Original sin "does not have the character of a personal fault in any of Adam's descendants. It is a deprivation of original holiness and justice, but human nature has not been totally corrupted." It is "wounded in the natural powers proper to it" (CCC 405).

The doctrine is "closely connected with that of redemption by Christ" (CCC 407).

## Scripture

> "Wherefore as by one man sin entered into this world, and by sin death; and so death passed upon all men, in whom all have sinned."
*Romans 5:12 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "For as by the disobedience of one man, many were made sinners; so also by the obedience of one, many shall be made just."
*Romans 5:19 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul sets Adam against Christ: through one man's disobedience many were made sinners, and through one man's obedience many will be made just.

## Why this matters today

Many people say they would never blame a baby for what Adam did. The Church agrees. Original sin is not personal guilt (CCC 405).

Some Protestant traditions teach that human nature is totally corrupted. The Catechism says it is wounded, but not totally corrupted (CCC 405). That is a real difference.

## What's at stake

If there is no such thing as a wound, everything is education and willpower. The Church says we need a Savior because the wound is real, and that the world's ills cannot be explained by social conditions alone (CCC 403).

## How to live it

- **Be honest about your own inclination to evil.** You do not need to be shocked at it.
- **Do not despair.** The same Church that teaches the wound teaches Baptism, which forgives it (CCC 1263).
- **Read Romans 5.**
- **Be patient with other people's failures.**
