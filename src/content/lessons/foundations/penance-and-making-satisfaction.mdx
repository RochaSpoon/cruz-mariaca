## The short version

Forgiveness removes the guilt, but sin also wrongs your neighbor and wounds you. The Church asks for reparation, and the penance a confessor gives is part of that (CCC 1459-1460).

## What the Church teaches

"Many sins wrong our neighbor. One must do what is possible in order to repair the harm (e.g., return stolen goods, restore the reputation of someone slandered, pay compensation for injuries). Simple justice requires as much" (CCC 1459).

"But sin also injures and weakens the sinner himself, as well as his relationships with God and neighbor" (CCC 1459).

"The penance the confessor imposes must take into account the penitent's personal situation and must seek his spiritual good." It can consist of "prayer, an offering, works of mercy, service of neighbor, voluntary self-denial, sacrifices, and above all the patient acceptance of the cross we must bear" (CCC 1460).

The Catechism says that forgiveness brings the remission of the eternal punishment of sin, "but temporal punishment of sin remains" (CCC 1473).

## Scripture

> "But Zacheus standing, said to the Lord: Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have wronged any man of any thing, I restore him fourfold. Jesus said to him: This day is salvation come to this house, because he also is a son of Abraham."
*Luke 19:8-9 (Douay-Rheims)*

Zacchaeus says he will give half his goods to the poor and repay fourfold anyone he has cheated. Jesus says salvation has come to his house.

## Why this matters today

Many people think forgiveness means the consequences disappear. The Catechism says you still have repair to do (CCC 1459).

Others treat penance as a punishment. The Church treats it as a remedy that heals (CCC 1460).

## What's at stake

If forgiveness cancels all repair, the person you wronged is left with the harm. The Church asks for restitution because justice requires it.

## How to live it

- **Pay back** what you owe.
- **Apologize** to the person you wronged.
- **Do your penance** soon after confession.
- **Accept consequences** with patience.
