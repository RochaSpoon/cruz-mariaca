## The short version

Presumption is a sin against hope. The Catechism names two kinds: trusting your own capacities to save yourself, and expecting God's forgiveness without conversion (CCC 2092).

## What the Church teaches

"There are two kinds of presumption. Either man presumes upon his own capacities, (hoping to be able to save himself without help from on high), or he presumes upon God's almighty power or his mercy (hoping to obtain his forgiveness without conversion and glory without merit)" (CCC 2092).

Presumption is listed with despair as the sins against hope (CCC 2091).

Paul asks whether we despise the riches of God's goodness and patience, and reminds us that his kindness is meant to lead us to repentance (Romans 2:4).

The right attitude is hope. We hope "to persevere 'to the end,'" trusting in grace (CCC 1821).

## Scripture

> "Or despisest thou the riches of his goodness, and patience, and longsuffering? Knowest thou not, that the benignity of God leadeth thee to penance? But according to thy hardness and impenitent heart, thou treasurest up to thyself wrath, against the day of wrath, and revelation of the just judgment of God."
*Romans 2:4-5 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul warns against treating God's patience as permission, as if it meant there is nothing to repent of.

## Why this matters today

A common version is that God is love, so everyone goes to heaven and nothing we do matters. The Catechism calls hoping for forgiveness "without conversion" a presumption (CCC 2092).

Another version is self-reliance, the claim that being a good person is enough. The Catechism names that too, as hoping to save yourself without help (CCC 2092).

## What's at stake

Presumption feels safe because it asks nothing. The Church warns that it asks nothing and gives nothing.

## How to live it

- **Ask what you are counting on** for your salvation.
- **Repent specifically.**
- **Trust God's mercy** and cooperate with it.
- **Read Romans 2.**
