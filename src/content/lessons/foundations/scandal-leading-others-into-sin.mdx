## The short version

Scandal is behavior that leads another to do evil. The Catechism says the one who gives it "becomes his neighbor's tempter," and quotes Jesus' warning about the millstone (CCC 2284-2285).

## What the Church teaches

"Scandal is an attitude or behavior which leads another to do evil. The person who gives scandal becomes his neighbor's tempter. He damages virtue and integrity; he may even draw his brother into spiritual death" (CCC 2284).

"Scandal takes on a particular gravity by reason of the authority of those who cause it or the weakness of those who are scandalized" (CCC 2285).

Jesus says, "Whoever causes one of these little ones who believe in me to sin, it would be better for him to have a great millstone fastened round his neck and to be drowned in the depth of the sea" (CCC 2285).

"Scandal can be provoked by laws or institutions, by fashion or opinion" (CCC 2286).

"Anyone who uses the power at his disposal in such a way that it leads others to do wrong becomes guilty of scandal" (CCC 2287).

## Scripture

> "But he that shall scandalize one of these little ones that believe in me, it were better for him that a millstone should be hanged about his neck, and that he should be drowned in the depth of the sea. Woe to the world because of scandals. For it must needs be that scandals come: but nevertheless woe to that man by whom the scandal cometh."
*Matthew 18:6-7 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus says it must needs be that scandals come, but woe to the man by whom they come.

## Why this matters today

Scandal in everyday speech means a shocking story. In the Church's sense it means giving someone else a push toward sin.

Christian leaders who fail do particular harm, which is why the Church treats their scandal as graver (CCC 2285).

## What's at stake

Your example does more than you think. Jesus ranks leading others astray among the worst things you can do.

## How to live it

- **Notice who is watching** you, especially children and new believers.
- **Do not mock** someone's attempt to change.
- **Be honest** when you fail, so that others do not copy you.
- **Ask what your online habits** teach others.
