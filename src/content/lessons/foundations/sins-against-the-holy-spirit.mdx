## The short version

The sin against the Holy Spirit is deliberately refusing God's mercy by refusing to repent. The Catechism says "there are no limits to the mercy of God" (CCC 1864).

## What the Church teaches

"'Whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit never has forgiveness, but is guilty of an eternal sin.' There are no limits to the mercy of God, but anyone who deliberately refuses to accept his mercy by repenting, rejects the forgiveness of his sins and the salvation offered by the Holy Spirit" (CCC 1864).

"Such hardness of heart can lead to final impenitence and eternal loss" (CCC 1864).

So the unforgivable sin is not a particular act or word. It is the refusal to be forgiven.

If you are worried you have committed it, the worry itself is a sign that you have not hardened your heart.

## Scripture

> "Therefore I say to you: Every sin and blasphemy shall be forgiven men, but the blasphemy of the Spirit shall not be forgiven. And whosoever shall speak a word against the Son of man, it shall be forgiven him: but he that shall speak against the Holy Ghost, it shall not be forgiven him, neither in this world, nor in the world to come."
*Matthew 12:31-32 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus says this after Pharisees say that he drives out demons by the prince of demons. He says every sin and blasphemy shall be forgiven, but not the blasphemy against the Spirit.

## Why this matters today

Many people fear they have already done it. The Catechism's definition is about a settled refusal to repent, and not about a stray thought.

Others use the phrase carelessly as a threat. The Church uses it to teach about mercy.

## What's at stake

If there were an unforgivable act, nobody could be sure. The Church says the only thing that blocks mercy is refusing it.

## How to live it

- **If you are afraid,** talk to a priest.
- **Repent.** The door is open.
- **Do not use the phrase** as a weapon.
- **Read Luke 15.**
