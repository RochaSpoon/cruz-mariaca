## The short version

The tradition calls death, judgment, heaven and hell the four last things. The Catechism treats them together in its teaching on the Christian's final end (CCC 1020-1060).

## What the Church teaches

**Death.** "Death is the end of earthly life." Remembering our mortality "helps us realize that we have only a limited time in which to bring our lives to fulfillment" (CCC 1007).

**Judgment.** "Each man receives his eternal retribution in his immortal soul at the very moment of his death, in a particular judgment that refers his life to Christ" (CCC 1022). A general judgment follows at the end of time (CCC 1038-1041).

**Heaven.** "Those who die in God's grace and friendship and are perfectly purified live for ever with Christ" (CCC 1023).

**Hell.** "To die in mortal sin without repenting and accepting God's merciful love means remaining separated from him for ever by our own free choice" (CCC 1033).

Purgatory is the purification of those who die in God's friendship but are not perfectly purified (CCC 1030-1031).

## Scripture

> "In all thy works remember thy last end, and thou shalt never sin."
*Ecclesiasticus 7:40 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Douay-Rheims reads "In all thy works remember thy last end, and thou shalt never sin." Sirach is the same book.

## Why this matters today

Our culture avoids talking about death. The Church says remembering it is a help (CCC 1007).

Many people believe that everyone goes straight to heaven. The Catechism says our choice matters, and that God does not predestine anyone to hell (CCC 1037).

## What's at stake

If life is all there is, nothing you do is final. The Church says it is, and that the stakes are high and hopeful.

## How to fight it

- **Think about your death** for a minute each day.
- **Prepare.** The Church asks us to pray for a happy death (CCC 1014).
- **Read the four lessons** that follow this one in order.
- **Go to Confession.** It is the way to get ready.
