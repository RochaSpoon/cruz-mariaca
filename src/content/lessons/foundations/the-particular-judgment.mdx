## The short version

The Church teaches that at the moment of death each person is judged, and receives either heaven (through purification or immediately) or damnation (CCC 1021-1022).

## What the Church teaches

"Death puts an end to human life as the time open to either accepting or rejecting the divine grace manifested in Christ" (CCC 1021).

The New Testament speaks of judgment mainly as the final encounter with Christ at his second coming, "but also repeatedly affirms that each will be rewarded immediately after death in accordance with his works and faith" (CCC 1021).

"Each man receives his eternal retribution in his immortal soul at the very moment of his death, in a particular judgment that refers his life to Christ: either entrance into the blessedness of heaven - through a purification or immediately - or immediate and everlasting damnation" (CCC 1022).

The Letter to the Hebrews says that men are appointed to die once, and that after this comes judgment (Hebrews 9:27; see CCC 1013).

## Scripture

> "And as it is appointed unto men once to die, and after this the judgment:"
*Hebrews 9:27 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And he said to Jesus: Lord, remember me when thou shalt come into thy kingdom. And Jesus said to him: Amen I say to thee, this day thou shalt be with me in paradise."
*Luke 23:42-43 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus tells the good thief on the cross that he will be with him in paradise "today." The Church takes this as one sign that something happens at death, before the end of time.

## Why this matters today

Many people picture judgment as a courtroom scene. The Catechism says it is "a particular judgment that refers his life to Christ" (CCC 1022).

Others believe in reincarnation or in an endless second chance. The Church says life is the time for accepting or refusing grace (CCC 1021).

## What's at stake

If death were not decisive, nothing you do would be final. The Church says it is, and that you meet Christ.

## How to live it

- **Do not put off** reconciling with anyone.
- **Pray for a happy death** (CCC 1014).
- **Trust his mercy** and take your choices seriously.
- **Pray for the dead.**
