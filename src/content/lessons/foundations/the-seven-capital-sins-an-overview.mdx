## The short version

The Church names seven capital sins, pride, avarice, envy, wrath, lust, gluttony and sloth. They are called "capital" because they "engender other sins" (CCC 1866).

## What the Church teaches

"Vices can be classified according to the virtues they oppose, or also be linked to the capital sins which Christian experience has distinguished, following St. John Cassian and St. Gregory the Great" (CCC 1866).

"They are called 'capital' because they engender other sins, other vices. They are pride, avarice, envy, wrath, lust, gluttony, and sloth or acedia" (CCC 1866).

The Catechism cites Gregory the Great's Moralia on Job for the list (CCC 1866, note).

Each capital sin has a virtue that opposes it. The Catechism's account of the virtues gives temperance for gluttony (CCC 1809), justice and charity for avarice and envy (CCC 1807, 1822), and fortitude for sloth (CCC 1808).

Sin repeats and reinforces itself: "Sin creates a proclivity to sin" (CCC 1865).

## Scripture

> "Six things there are, which the Lord hateth, and the seventh his soul detesteth: Haughty eyes, a lying tongue, hands that shed innocent blood, A heart that deviseth wicked plots, feet that are swift to run into mischief, A deceitful witness that uttereth lies, and him that soweth discord among brethren."
*Proverbs 6:16-19 (Douay-Rheims)*

Proverbs lists seven things the Lord hates. The Church's list of seven capital sins is a different list, but the number echoes it.

## Why this matters today

The seven deadly sins are famous from art and film. In the Church's usage they are a way of tracing the roots of other sins, not a list of the worst ones.

Naming the root habit often helps more than naming the act.

## What's at stake

If you only deal with the acts, the habit stays. Knowing which root is yours lets you work on it.

## How to live it

- **Pick the one that sounds most like you** and read its lesson.
- **Pair it with the opposite virtue.**
- **Ask a friend** which one they see.
- **Go to Confession** with the pattern in mind.
