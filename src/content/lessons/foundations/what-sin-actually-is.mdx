## The short version

The Catechism says sin is an offense against reason, truth and conscience, and a "failure in genuine love for God and neighbor" (CCC 1849). It is more than breaking a rule.

## What the Church teaches

"Sin is an offense against reason, truth, and right conscience; it is failure in genuine love for God and neighbor caused by a perverse attachment to certain goods. It wounds the nature of man and injures human solidarity" (CCC 1849).

"Sin is an offense against God: 'Against you, you alone, have I sinned, and done that which is evil in your sight.' Sin sets itself against God's love for us and turns our hearts away from it" (CCC 1850).

Like the first sin, it is "disobedience, a revolt against God through the will to become 'like gods'" (CCC 1850).

The Catechism says sin "most clearly manifests its violence" in the Passion, where it shows itself as unbelief, hatred, cowardice, cruelty, betrayal and denial (CCC 1851).

Sin also repeats itself: "Sin creates a proclivity to sin; it engenders vice by repetition of the same acts" (CCC 1865).

## Scripture

> "To thee only have I sinned, and have done evil before thee: that thou mayst be justified in thy words and mayst overcome when thou art judged."
*Psalms 50:6 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Douay-Rheims numbers this psalm 50. It is the psalm of repentance traditionally attributed to David.

## Why this matters today

Many people think of sin as a list of rules that the Church made up. The Catechism puts the weight on love and truth, and on the damage sin does to us and to others.

Others think the word is just a way to make people feel guilty. The Church's reply is that guilt is a signal, and that the aim is forgiveness (CCC 1851).

## What's at stake

If sin is only rule-breaking, a good enough record cancels it. If it is a failure of love, you need forgiveness and healing, not just a better record.

## How to fight it

- **Ask what love required** in the last argument you had, not only what the rule said.
- **Name the pattern,** not only the act.
- **Take it to Confession.** The Church treats it as the place where sin is forgiven.
- **Read Psalm 50** as your own prayer.
