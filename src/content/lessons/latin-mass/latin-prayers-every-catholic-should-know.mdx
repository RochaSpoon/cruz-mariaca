## The short version

The Compendium of the Catechism prints common prayers in Latin so that Catholics can pray them in the Church's own language. Here are five.

## What the Church teaches

The Compendium of the Catechism of the Catholic Church (2005) gives these prayers in English and Latin in its Appendix A, "Common Prayers." The Latin texts below are copied from it.

> "In nómine Patris et Fílii et Spíritus Sancti. Amen."
> *Signum Crucis (Compendium of the Catechism, Appendix A)*

> "Pater noster, qui es in cælis: sanctificétur Nomen Tuum: advéniat Regnum Tuum: fiat volúntas Tua, sicut in cælo, et in terra. Panem nostrum cotidiánum da nobis hódie, et dimítte nobis débita nostra, sicut et nos dimíttimus debitóribus nostris. et ne nos indúcas in tentatiónem; sed líbera nos a Malo."
> *Pater Noster (Compendium of the Catechism, Appendix A)*

> "Ave, María, grátia plena, Dóminus tecum. Benedícta tu in muliéribus, et benedíctus fructus ventris tui, Iesus. Sancta María, Mater Dei, ora pro nobis peccatóribus, nunc et in hora mortis nostræ. Amen."
> *Ave Maria (Compendium of the Catechism, Appendix A)*

> "Glória Patri et Fílio et Spirítui Sancto. Sicut erat in princípio, et nunc et semper et in sæcula sæculórum. Amen."
> *Gloria Patri (Compendium of the Catechism, Appendix A)*

> "Ángele Dei, qui custos es mei, me, tibi commíssum pietáte supérna, illúmina, custódi, rege et gubérna. Amen."
> *Angele Dei (Compendium of the Catechism, Appendix A)*

The same Appendix gives the Salve Regina, the Anima Christi, the Creeds and more. All of them are on the prayers page of this site, with English and Spanish beside the Latin.

## Scripture

> "And it came to pass, that as he was in a certain place praying, when he ceased, one of his disciples said to him: Lord, teach us to pray, as John also taught his disciples. And he said to them: When you pray, say: Father, hallowed be thy name. Thy kingdom come. Give us this day our daily bread. And forgive us our sins, for we also forgive every one that is indebted to us. And lead us not into temptation."
*Luke 11:1-4 (Douay-Rheims)*

The disciples ask Jesus to teach them to pray, and He gives them the Our Father.

## Why this matters today

Latin prayers are easy to learn by saying them aloud. Learn one line a day.

The accent marks in the Compendium show where the stress falls. They are not part of the old spelling, but they help.

## What's at stake

A shared set of prayers lets Catholics from any country pray together. The Second Vatican Council asked that the faithful be able to say and sing the Latin parts of the Ordinary together (Sacrosanctum Concilium 54).

## How to live it

- **Pray** the Sign of the Cross and Our Father in Latin each day for a week.
- **Say** the Hail Mary in Latin at the end of a Rosary decade.
- **Print** the prayers page and keep it in your missal.
- **Ask** a priest or choir director about pronunciation.
