## The short version

Pope Leo XIV's Magnifica Humanitas says the principles to guide AI are "the inalienable dignity of the human person, the common good" and the other principles of Church teaching. It calls for caution and for care to see others "as a face not merely as a function" (Magnifica Humanitas 96, 106, 114).

## What the Church teaches

Faced with concentrated power in the digital world, "the criteria for judgment and discernment... are the noble principles of Social Doctrine: the inalienable dignity of the human person, the common good, the universal destination of goods" (Magnifica Humanitas 96).

"Calling for prudence, rigorous evaluation and even, at times, a slower pace in adopting AI does not mean opposing progress; instead, it is an exercise of responsible care for the human family" (Magnifica Humanitas 106).

"The quality of a civilization is measured not by the power of its means, but by the care it is able to offer, by its ability to recognize the other as a face not merely as a function" (Magnifica Humanitas 114).

He addresses developers directly: "Developers... bear a particular ethical and spiritual responsibility, for every design choice reflects a vision of humanity" (Magnifica Humanitas 111).

He also warns that "a more moral AI is not enough" and asks that the ethical frameworks themselves be open to discussion and shared standards of justice (Magnifica Humanitas 107).

## Scripture

> "For I will behold thy heavens, the works of thy fingers: the moon and the stars which thou hast founded. What is man that thou art mindful of him? or the son of man that thou visitest him? Thou hast made him a little less than the angels, thou hast crowned him with glory and honour:"
*Psalms 8:4-6 (Douay-Rheims)*

The psalmist asks what man is that God is mindful of him. The Church takes the answer as the basis of human dignity.

## Why this matters today

AI is advancing fast, and many people feel both excited and uneasy. The Church does not reject it, and asks for prudence.

The Pope's concern is mainly with who controls it, what it does to attention, work and care, and what it says about what a person is.

## What's at stake

If a person is only a function, a machine can replace one. The Church says a person is a face.

## How to live it

- **Use** it as a tool, not an authority.
- **Notice** what it does to your attention.
- **Keep** human care in human hands.
- **Read** Magnifica Humanitas 96-114.
