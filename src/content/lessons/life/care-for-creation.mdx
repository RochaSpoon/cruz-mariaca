## The short version

Caring for creation is a moral duty. The Catechism says use of creation "cannot be divorced from respect for moral imperatives," and Laudato Si' says other creatures "have a value of their own in God's eyes" (CCC 2415; Laudato Si' 69).

## What the Church teaches

"The seventh commandment enjoins respect for the integrity of creation. Animals, like plants and inanimate beings, are by nature destined for the common good of past, present, and future humanity" (CCC 2415).

"Use of the mineral, vegetable, and animal resources of the universe cannot be divorced from respect for moral imperatives" (CCC 2415).

Pope Francis writes: "We are called to recognize that other living beings have a value of their own in God's eyes: 'by their mere existence they bless him and give him glory'" (Laudato Si' 69).

"This responsibility for God's earth means that human beings, endowed with intelligence, must respect the laws of nature and the delicate equilibria existing between the creatures of this world" (Laudato Si' 68).

The Catechism says "we are stewards, not owners" of the goods entrusted to us (CCC 2280 on life, and CCC 2404 on goods).

## Why this matters today

Environmental questions are political, and Catholics disagree about solutions. The Church's contribution is the principle: creation is a gift and we are stewards.

Small habits count: waste, consumption, how you treat places.

## What's at stake

If the world is only a resource, it will be used up. The Church says it is a gift for those who come after us.

## How to live it

- **Waste** less.
- **Spend** time outdoors and give thanks.
- **Read** Laudato Si' 65-69.
- **Join** a local cleanup.
