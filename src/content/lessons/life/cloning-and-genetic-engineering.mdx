## The short version

The Church holds that cloning a human being is a grave offense against dignity and that "therapeutic" cloning is "even more serious" because it creates embryos to destroy them (Dignitas Personae 28-30).

## What the Church teaches

"Human cloning refers to the asexual or agametic reproduction of the entire human organism in order to produce one or more 'copies' which, from a genetic perspective, are substantially identical to the single original" (Dignitas Personae 28).

"The fact that someone would arrogate to himself the right to determine arbitrarily the genetic characteristics of another person represents a grave offense to the dignity of that person as well as to the fundamental equality of all people" (Dignitas Personae 29).

"To create embryos with the intention of destroying them, even with the intention of helping the sick, is completely incompatible with human dignity" (Dignitas Personae 30).

The Catechism says that procedures on the embryo are licit if they respect its life and integrity and are directed toward its healing or survival. It adds "it is immoral to produce human embryos intended for exploitation as disposable biological material" (CCC 2275).

## Scripture

> "And God created man to his own image: to the image of God he created him: male and female he created them."
*Genesis 1:27 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Church grounds the argument in the claim that every human being is made in God's image.

## Why this matters today

Gene editing to cure disease is a different matter from cloning or creating designer children. The Church would ask what is being done to whom.

The Church does not oppose science. She opposes treating human beings as material.

## What's at stake

If a person can be made to specification, the person is a product.

## How to live it

- **Ask** whether a proposed treatment uses embryos.
- **Support** research that does not destroy life.
- **Read** Dignitas Personae.
- **Keep** the conversation honest.
