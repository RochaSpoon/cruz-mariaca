## The short version

The Church permits cremation "provided that it does not demonstrate a denial of faith in the resurrection of the body" (CCC 2301). It asks that ashes be kept in a sacred place and not scattered (Ad Resurgendum cum Christo).

## What the Church teaches

"The Church permits cremation, provided that it does not demonstrate a denial of faith in the resurrection of the body" (CCC 2301).

The 2016 instruction Ad Resurgendum cum Christo says "following the most ancient Christian tradition, the Church insistently recommends that the bodies of the deceased be buried in cemeteries or other sacred places" (Ad Resurgendum 3).

Burial "adequately corresponds to the piety and respect owed to the bodies of the faithful departed who through Baptism have become temples of the Holy Spirit" (Ad Resurgendum 3).

The instruction asks that ashes be kept in a sacred place such as a cemetery or church, and not be scattered or kept at home. A grave or niche gives the community a place to pray and remember.

"The bodies of the dead must be treated with respect and charity, in faith and hope of the Resurrection" (CCC 2300).

## Scripture

> "So also is the resurrection of the dead. It is sown in corruption, it shall rise in incorruption. It is sown in dishonour, it shall rise in glory. It is sown in weakness, it shall rise in power. It is sown a natural body, it shall rise a spiritual body. If there be a natural body, there is also a spiritual body, as it is written:"
*1 Corinthians 15:42-44 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says the body is sown in corruption and raised in incorruption.

## Why this matters today

Cremation is now common, partly for cost. The Church allows it, and asks that the ashes be treated as the body of a person.

Scattering and keeping ashes on the mantel are common practices that the instruction asks Catholics not to follow.

## What's at stake

How we treat the body of the dead says what we believe about the body.

## How to live it

- **Plan** a Catholic funeral.
- **Choose** burial of the ashes in a cemetery.
- **Ask** your parish about options.
- **Pray** for the dead.
