## The short version

Obtaining stem cells from a living embryo "invariably causes the death of the embryo and is consequently gravely illicit." Adult stem cells, cord blood and similar sources raise no such problem (Dignitas Personae 32).

## What the Church teaches

"Methods which do not cause serious harm to the subject from whom the stem cells are taken are to be considered licit. This is generally the case when tissues are taken from: a) an adult organism; b) the blood of the umbilical cord at the time of birth; c) fetuses who have died of natural causes" (Dignitas Personae 32).

"The obtaining of stem cells from a living human embryo, on the other hand, invariably causes the death of the embryo and is consequently gravely illicit" (Dignitas Personae 32).

"Research initiatives involving the use of adult stem cells, since they do not present ethical problems, should be encouraged and supported" (Dignitas Personae 32).

The Catechism says "since it must be treated from conception as a person, the embryo must be defended in its integrity, cared for, and healed, as far as possible, like any other human being" (CCC 2274).

## Why this matters today

The Church is not against stem cell research. She supports it where it does not kill an embryo.

Many therapies in use today come from adult or cord-blood sources.

## What's at stake

The question is whether a human embryo is a person or a resource. The Church says person.

## How to live it

- **Support** ethical stem cell research.
- **Check** where a therapy's cells come from.
- **Learn** the difference between sources.
- **Talk** with scientists you know.
