## The short version

Euthanasia is deliberately killing a sick, disabled, or dying person to end their suffering. Assisted suicide is helping them kill themselves. Both are gravely wrong, no matter how compassionate the motive. The Church is not against easing suffering. It's against killing the sufferer.

## What the Church teaches

"Whatever its motives and means, direct euthanasia consists in putting an end to the lives of handicapped, sick, or dying persons. It is morally unacceptable" (CCC 2277). John Paul II confirmed that euthanasia is "a grave violation of the law of God, since it is the deliberate and morally unacceptable killing of a human person" (*Evangelium Vitae* 65).

But the Church also teaches:
- **You don't have to use extraordinary treatments.** Stopping medical procedures that are "burdensome, dangerous, extraordinary, or disproportionate to the expected outcome" can be legitimate (CCC 2278). That's accepting death, not causing it.
- **Pain relief is good,** even if it may shorten life as a side effect, as long as death isn't intended (CCC 2279).
- **Ordinary care is still owed.** Food, water, hygiene, and comfort should normally continue (CCC 2279; *Samaritanus Bonus*).

## Why this hits hard today

Assisted suicide is legal in California (since 2016) and a growing number of states and countries. Canada's "Medical Assistance in Dying" program has grown fast, and a significant share of deaths there now come through it. [VERIFY latest figures] There have been reports of people requesting it because of poverty, loneliness, or lack of care. When killing becomes a treatment option, the vulnerable feel pressure to "not be a burden."

## What's at stake

Murder is grave matter. Doctors, family members, and lawmakers who participate share in it. A society that kills its suffering teaches everyone that a life is only worth living if it's useful and comfortable. That's the opposite of the cross.

## What to do

- **Support hospice and palliative care.** Real pain management takes away most of the demand for euthanasia.
- **Visit the sick and elderly.** Loneliness is one of the top reasons people ask to die.
- **Write an advance directive** consistent with Catholic teaching. The Catholic Medical Association and your diocese have templates.
