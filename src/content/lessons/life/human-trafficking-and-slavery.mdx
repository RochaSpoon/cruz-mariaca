## The short version

The Catechism says the seventh commandment forbids acts that "lead to the enslavement of human beings, to their being bought, sold and exchanged like merchandise." It calls this "a sin against the dignity of persons" (CCC 2414).

## What the Church teaches

"The seventh commandment forbids acts or enterprises that for any reason - selfish or ideological, commercial, or totalitarian - lead to the enslavement of human beings, to their being bought, sold and exchanged like merchandise, in disregard for their personal dignity. It is a sin against the dignity of persons and their fundamental rights to reduce them by violence to their productive value or to a source of profit" (CCC 2414).

Paul's Letter to Philemon asks a master to receive his runaway slave Onesimus no longer as a slave but as a beloved brother (Philemon 16).

The Catechism lists "the cry of the foreigner, the widow, and the orphan" among the sins that cry to heaven (CCC 1867).

"Everyone should look upon his neighbor (without any exception) as 'another self'" (CCC 1931).

## Scripture

> "For perhaps he therefore departed for a season from thee, that thou mightest receive him again for ever: Not now as a servant, but instead of a servant, a most dear brother, especially to me: but how much more to thee both in the flesh and in the Lord? If therefore thou count me a partner, receive him as myself."
*Philemon 1:15-17 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul writes from prison, and asks Philemon to receive his slave as a brother.

## Why this matters today

Trafficking is still common, in labor and in sex work. Some of it is hidden in supply chains.

If you suspect someone is being trafficked, call local emergency services or a national hotline. This lesson does not list numbers.

## What's at stake

Treating people as property is one of the oldest sins and it is not gone.

## How to live it

- **Learn** the signs.
- **Ask** where your goods come from.
- **Support** groups that help survivors.
- **Report** what you suspect.
