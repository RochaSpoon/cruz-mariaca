## The short version

The Catechism says the more prosperous nations are "obliged, to the extent they are able, to welcome the foreigner." It also says authorities may regulate immigration for the common good (CCC 2241).

## What the Church teaches

"The more prosperous nations are obliged, to the extent they are able, to welcome the foreigner in search of the security and the means of livelihood which he cannot find in his country of origin" (CCC 2241).

"Public authorities should see to it that the natural right is respected that places a guest under the protection of those who receive him" (CCC 2241).

The Catechism adds that "political authorities, for the sake of the common good for which they are responsible, may make the exercise of the right to immigrate subject to various juridical conditions," and immigrants "are obliged to respect with gratitude the material and spiritual heritage of the country that receives them" (CCC 2241).

Leviticus says the stranger who dwells with you shall be as one born among you, and you shall love him as yourself (Leviticus 19:33-34).

Jesus says at the judgment, "I was a stranger and you took me in" (Matthew 25:35).

## Scripture

> "If a stranger dwell in your land, and abide among you, do not upbraid him: But let him be among you as one of the same country: and you shall love him as yourselves: for you were strangers in the land of Egypt. I am the Lord your God."
*Leviticus 19:33-34 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me to drink; I was a stranger, and you took me in:"
*Matthew 25:35 (Douay-Rheims)*

Both passages put the stranger under God's care.

## Why this matters today

Immigration is an argument with decent people on both sides. The Catechism holds two things together: a duty to welcome, and a right of states to regulate (CCC 2241).

The Church does not give a policy. She gives principles.

## What's at stake

If you only see the border, you may lose sight of the person. If you only see the person, you may lose sight of the common good.

## How to live it

- **Meet** an immigrant in your community.
- **Welcome** newcomers at your parish.
- **Learn** the facts and the law.
- **Vote** with both principles in mind.
