## The short version

Research to reduce infertility "is to be encouraged," and techniques that assist the conjugal act without replacing it are permitted. Adoption is also encouraged (CCC 2375, 2379; Dignitas Personae 13).

## What the Church teaches

"Couples who discover that they are sterile suffer greatly" (CCC 2374).

"Research aimed at reducing human sterility is to be encouraged, on condition that it is placed 'at the service of the human person, of his inalienable rights, and his true and integral good'" (CCC 2375).

Dignitas Personae says that "techniques which act as an aid to the conjugal act and its fertility are permitted," while those which substitute for the conjugal act are excluded (Dignitas Personae 12).

"Adoption should be encouraged, promoted and facilitated by appropriate legislation so that the many children who lack parents may receive a home" (Dignitas Personae 13).

"Spouses who still suffer from infertility after exhausting legitimate medical procedures should unite themselves with the Lord's Cross, the source of all spiritual fecundity. They can give expression to their generosity by adopting abandoned children or performing demanding services for others" (CCC 2379).

The Douay-Rheims calls 1 Samuel "1 Kings." Hannah prays in bitterness of soul for a child, and God hears her.

## Why this matters today

Many couples find that there are more options than they were told. A doctor who works within the Church's teaching can explain them. This lesson cannot give medical advice.

Infertility is a grief. The Church asks for patience, support and real options.

## What's at stake

A couple should not be told the only path is the one the Church rejects.

## How to live it

- **Ask** your doctor about treatments that work with the body.
- **Look** into adoption and foster care.
- **Find** a parish couple who has walked this road.
- **Pray** with Hannah.
