## The short version

The Church forbids direct abortion, and also teaches that an act can have two effects, one intended and one not. This principle, called double effect, is why some treatments to save a mother are allowed (CCC 2263; Aquinas ST II-II q.64 a.7).

## What the Church teaches

Direct abortion, "abortion willed either as an end or a means, is gravely contrary to the moral law" (CCC 2271; Evangelium Vitae 62).

The principle of double effect is stated in the Catechism for self-defense: "The act of self-defense can have a double effect: the preservation of one's own life; and the killing of the aggressor... The one is intended, the other is not" (CCC 2263).

Aquinas gives the root of the idea: "Nothing hinders one act from having two effects, only one of which is intended, while the other is beside the intention" (ST II-II, q.64, a.7).

Applied to pregnancy, this means a treatment aimed at curing a serious condition in the mother is a different act from deliberately ending the child's life, even if the child dies as an unintended effect. The conditions are strict.

Catholic hospitals use detailed directives, and a doctor and an ethicist should be consulted case by case. This lesson cannot settle a real case.

## Scripture

> "Be not therefore solicitous for tomorrow; for the morrow will be solicitous for itself. Sufficient for the day is the evil thereof."
*Matthew 6:34 (Douay-Rheims)*

The verse is a reminder that a hard decision is made one day at a time, and with prayer.

## Why this matters today

People often say the Church would let a mother die. The Church's actual position is that a mother's life may be saved by treatment aimed at her illness, even where the child is lost as a foreseen but unintended result.

The line the Church draws is between treating the mother and deliberately killing the child.

## What's at stake

Both lives matter. The Church asks that no one ever be treated as a means.

## How to live it

- **Talk** to a Catholic ethicist or a priest in a real case.
- **Ask** the doctors what each option does and intends.
- **Pray** and ask for prayer.
- **Be gentle** with families who have faced this.
