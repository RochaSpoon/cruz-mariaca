## The short version

Stopping treatment that is "burdensome, dangerous, extraordinary, or disproportionate to the expected outcome" can be legitimate. It is not euthanasia, because the aim is not to cause death (CCC 2278).

## What the Church teaches

"Discontinuing medical procedures that are burdensome, dangerous, extraordinary, or disproportionate to the expected outcome can be legitimate; it is the refusal of 'over-zealous' treatment. Here one does not will to cause death; one's inability to impede it is merely accepted" (CCC 2278).

"The decisions should be made by the patient if he is competent and able or, if not, by those legally entitled to act for the patient, whose reasonable will and legitimate interests must always be respected" (CCC 2278).

"Even if death is thought imminent, the ordinary care owed to a sick person cannot be legitimately interrupted" (CCC 2279).

Samaritanus Bonus (2020) speaks of the duty to care for the person to the end and warns against both abandonment and therapeutic obstinacy.

Direct euthanasia, "putting an end to the lives of handicapped, sick, or dying persons," is "morally unacceptable" (CCC 2277).

## Scripture

> "Honour the physician for the need thou hast of him: for the most High hath created him. For all healing is from God, and he shall receive gifts of the king. The skill of the physician shall lift up his head, and in the sight of great men he shall be praised. The most High hath created medicines out of the earth, and a wise man will not abhor them."
*Ecclesiasticus 38:1-4 (Douay-Rheims)*

Ecclesiasticus is the Douay-Rheims name for Sirach. The passage honors the physician as one whom God has created.

## Why this matters today

Families often face the question of when to stop. The Church says you may stop what is disproportionate, and must keep giving ordinary care, such as food, water and comfort, in general.

Each case needs a doctor, a priest and the family. This lesson cannot decide one.

## What's at stake

Two mistakes are possible: killing, and clinging to treatment that only prolongs dying. The Church warns against both.

## How to live it

- **Ask** doctors what a treatment can and cannot do.
- **Involve** a priest or a Catholic ethicist.
- **Write** down your wishes.
- **Stay** at the bedside.
