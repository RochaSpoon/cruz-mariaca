## The short version

The Catechism says organ transplants are in conformity with the moral law when risks are proportionate, and that donation after death "is a noble and meritorious act" (CCC 2296).

## What the Church teaches

"Organ transplants are in conformity with the moral law if the physical and psychological dangers and risks incurred by the donor are proportionate to the good sought for the recipient" (CCC 2296).

"Donation of organs after death is a noble and meritorious act and is to be encouraged as a manifestation of generous solidarity" (CCC 2296).

"It is not morally acceptable if the donor or those who legitimately speak for him have not given their explicit consent" (CCC 2296).

"It is furthermore morally inadmissible directly to bring about the disabling mutilation or death of a human being, even in order to delay the death of other persons" (CCC 2296).

"The free gift of organs after death is legitimate and can be meritorious" (CCC 2301).

## Scripture

> "Greater love than this no man hath, that a man lay down his life for his friends."
*John 15:13 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus says there is no greater love than to lay down one's life for one's friends.

## Why this matters today

Many Catholics worry that donation conflicts with the resurrection of the body. The Catechism says it does not (CCC 2296, 2301).

The conditions are consent and proportion. Medical practice and national law vary, and a doctor can explain them.

## What's at stake

A donor can give life. The Church treats that as a form of love.

## How to live it

- **Register** as a donor if you are willing.
- **Tell** your family.
- **Give** blood.
- **Pray** for donors and their families.
