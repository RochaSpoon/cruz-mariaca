## The short version

The Catechism says terrorism is "gravely against justice and charity" and torture "is contrary to respect for the person and for human dignity" (CCC 2297).

## What the Church teaches

"Kidnapping and hostage taking bring on a reign of terror; by means of threats they subject their victims to intolerable pressures. They are morally wrong" (CCC 2297).

"Terrorism threatens, wounds, and kills indiscriminately; it is gravely against justice and charity" (CCC 2297).

"Torture which uses physical or moral violence to extract confessions, punish the guilty, frighten opponents, or satisfy hatred is contrary to respect for the person and for human dignity" (CCC 2297).

Every human being, including the guilty, keeps dignity (CCC 2267, 2018 revision).

## Scripture

> "But I say to you, Love your enemies: do good to them that hate you: and pray for them that persecute and calumniate you: That you may be the children of your Father who is in heaven, who maketh his sun to rise upon the good, and bad, and raineth upon the just and the unjust."
*Matthew 5:44-45 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus tells his followers to love their enemies and pray for those who persecute them.

## Why this matters today

Arguments for torture usually appeal to a ticking-bomb scenario. The Catechism does not make exceptions.

Pressure on hostages and kidnappers also falls under this teaching.

## What's at stake

If a cause allows torture, it will not stop at one case.

## How to live it

- **Refuse** to excuse it.
- **Support** victims.
- **Pray** for those held.
- **Learn** the facts before you argue.
