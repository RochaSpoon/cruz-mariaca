## The short version

Since 2018 the Catechism teaches that "the death penalty is inadmissible because it is an attack on the inviolability and dignity of the person" (CCC 2267, revised 2018).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism once said capital punishment "was long considered an appropriate response to the gravity of certain crimes and an acceptable, albeit extreme, means of safeguarding the common good" (CCC 2267).

In 2018 Pope Francis approved a revised paragraph. It says "there is an increasing awareness that the dignity of the person is not lost even after the commission of very serious crimes."

It adds that more effective systems of detention have been developed "which ensure the due protection of citizens but, at the same time, do not definitively deprive the guilty of the possibility of redemption."

"Consequently, the Church teaches, in the light of the Gospel, that 'the death penalty is inadmissible because it is an attack on the inviolability and dignity of the person,' and she works with determination for its abolition worldwide" (CCC 2267, 2018 revision).

John Paul II had already written that execution should not be used "except in cases of absolute necessity: in other words, when it would not be possible otherwise to defend society," and that today "such cases are very rare, if not practically non-existent" (Evangelium Vitae 56).

## Scripture

> "Say to them: As I live, saith the Lord God, I desire not the death of the wicked, but that the wicked turn from his way, and live. Turn ye, turn ye from your evil ways: and why will you die, O house of Israel?"
*Ezechiel 33:11 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Douay-Rheims calls Ezekiel "Ezechiel." God says he does not desire the death of the wicked, but that the wicked turn from his way and live.

## Why this matters today

Catholics disagree about this. Some argue that the earlier teaching shows the Church always permitted it. The Church's present teaching is that it is inadmissible.

Victims' families and crime survivors have a real claim on justice. The Church says justice can be served by other means (CCC 2266).

## What's at stake

The question is whether a person can ever lose the dignity God gave him. The Church says no.

## How to live it

- **Read** the revised text of CCC 2267.
- **Listen** to families of victims, and of the executed.
- **Pray** for those on death row and for their victims.
- **Visit** prisoners where you can.
