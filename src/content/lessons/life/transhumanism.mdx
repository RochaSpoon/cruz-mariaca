## The short version

Transhumanism treats progress as surpassing the human condition. Magnifica Humanitas calls it an ideology in "some centers of technological power," and the Catechism says science and technology "by themselves... cannot disclose the meaning of existence" (Magnifica Humanitas 115; CCC 2293).

## What the Church teaches

Pope Leo XIV writes of "certain currents of thought that interpret progress as surpassing the human condition, and which are often grouped under the labels of transhumanism and posthumanism" (Magnifica Humanitas 115).

He says these perspectives "form the ideological background present in some centers of technological power" (Magnifica Humanitas 115).

"Science and technology are precious resources when placed at the service of man and promote his integral development for the benefit of all. By themselves however they cannot disclose the meaning of existence and of human progress" (CCC 2293).

"It is an illusion to claim moral neutrality in scientific research and its applications" (CCC 2294).

Dignitas Infinita insists the human body shares the dignity of the person (Dignitas Infinita 60).

## Scripture

> "For God doth know that in what day soever you shall eat thereof, your eyes shall be opened: and you shall be as Gods, knowing good and evil."
*Genesis 3:5 (Douay-Rheims)*

The serpent's promise in Genesis is that you will be like gods. The Church reads the same temptation in claims to engineer our way out of being human.

## Why this matters today

Medicine that heals is not transhumanism. The Church welcomes it. The question is whether the goal is healing or replacing human nature.

Many people are simply hopeful about technology. The Church asks what it is for.

## What's at stake

If being human is a problem to be solved, then people who are weak, old or disabled are the problem.

## How to live it

- **Ask** what a technology is for.
- **Welcome** healing, and be wary of upgrading.
- **Value** limits, such as rest and dependence.
- **Read** Magnifica Humanitas 115-120.
