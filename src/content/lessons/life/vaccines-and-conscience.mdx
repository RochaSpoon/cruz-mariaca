## The short version

In 2020 the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith said it is morally acceptable to receive Covid-19 vaccines tested or produced with cell lines from aborted fetuses when no alternative is available, and that vaccination "must be voluntary."

## What the Church teaches

The note of 21 December 2020 says that when ethically irreproachable vaccines are not available, "it is morally acceptable to receive Covid-19 vaccines that have used cell lines from aborted fetuses in their research and production process."

The reason: the cooperation in evil on the part of those who use the vaccines is "passive material cooperation" and is "remote."

"The licit use of such vaccines does not and should not in any way imply that there is a moral endorsement of the use of cell lines proceeding from aborted fetuses."

The note adds that "vaccination is not, as a rule, a moral obligation and that, therefore, it must be voluntary."

It also says that those who refuse "must do their utmost to avoid, by other prophylactic means and appropriate behavior, becoming vehicles for the transmission of the infectious agent."

## Scripture

> "For one judgeth between day and day: and another judgeth every day: let every man abound in his own sense."
*Romans 14:5 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says each person should be fully convinced in his own mind. The verse is about a different matter, and shows that conscience matters.

## Why this matters today

The note is about one set of vaccines from 2020. This lesson makes no medical claims.

Catholics differ in how they apply it. The Church says to form your conscience and to ask for ethically sourced options.

## What's at stake

The note asks you to take both the dignity of the unborn and the common good seriously.

## How to live it

- **Read** the note itself, it is short.
- **Ask** your doctor for the facts.
- **Ask** for ethically sourced alternatives.
- **Respect** those who decide differently.
