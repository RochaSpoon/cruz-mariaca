## The short version

Caring for your body is a duty, and it is not an idol. The Catechism says life and health "are precious gifts entrusted to us by God," and rejects "the cult of the body" (CCC 2288-2289).

## What the Church teaches

"Life and physical health are precious gifts entrusted to us by God. We must take reasonable care of them, taking into account the needs of others and the common good" (CCC 2288).

"If morality requires respect for the life of the body, it does not make it an absolute value. It rejects a neo-pagan notion that tends to promote the cult of the body, to sacrifice everything for its sake, to idolize physical perfection and success at sports" (CCC 2289).

Paul says your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit and that you are not your own (1 Corinthians 6:19-20).

The Catechism says "the human body shares in the dignity of 'the image of God'" (CCC 364).

The same text says rest is part of life: "human life has a rhythm of work and rest" (CCC 2184).

## Scripture

> "Or know you not, that your members are the temple of the Holy Ghost, who is in you, whom you have from God; and you are not your own? For you are bought with a great price. Glorify and bear God in your body."
*1 Corinthians 6:19-20 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says you were bought at a price, so glorify God in your body.

## Why this matters today

Wellness culture pushes both obsession and neglect. The Church asks for reasonable care.

Taking care of yourself is not selfish. It is a way of keeping a gift.

## What's at stake

A body in good order can serve others. A body worshiped serves no one.

## How to live it

- **Sleep, eat and move** in ways that keep you well.
- **See** a doctor when you should.
- **Rest** on Sunday.
- **Thank** God for your body.
