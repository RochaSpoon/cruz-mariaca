## The short version

Giving to the poor is a matter of justice as well as charity. The Catechism says "the goods we possess are not ours, but theirs," and lists almsgiving among the works of mercy (CCC 2446-2447).

## What the Church teaches

"God blesses those who come to the aid of the poor and rebukes those who turn away from them" (CCC 2443).

The Catechism quotes Chrysostom: "Not to enable the poor to share in our goods is to steal from them and deprive them of life. The goods we possess are not ours, but theirs" (CCC 2446).

It adds: "The demands of justice must be satisfied first of all; that which is already due in justice is not to be offered as a gift of charity" (CCC 2446).

"Giving alms to the poor is a witness to fraternal charity: it is also a work of justice pleasing to God" (CCC 2462).

Jesus says to give in secret, so that the left hand does not know what the right hand is doing (Matthew 6:2-4).

## Scripture

> "Give alms out of thy substance, and turn not away thy face from any poor person: for so it shall come to pass that the face of the Lord shall not be turned from thee. According to thy ability be merciful. If thou have much give abundantly: if thou have a little, take care even so to bestow willingly a little. For thus thou storest up to thyself a good reward for the day of necessity. For alms deliver from all sin, and from death, and will not suffer the soul to go into darkness."
*Tobias 4:7-11 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "Therefore when thou dost an almsdeed, sound not a trumpet before thee, as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and in the streets, that they may be honoured by men. Amen I say to you, they have received their reward. But when thou dost alms, let not thy left hand know what thy right hand doth. That thy alms may be in secret, and thy Father who seeth in secret will repay thee."
*Matthew 6:2-4 (Douay-Rheims)*

Tobias, a book left out of most Protestant Bibles, is the classic Old Testament text on almsgiving. Jesus' teaching in Matthew 6 is the New Testament parallel.

## Why this matters today

Many people give leftovers. The Catechism says that what is due in justice comes first and is not a gift of charity (CCC 2446).

Giving to a charity is good. So is giving your time and attention, which are among the spiritual works of mercy.

## What's at stake

If you only give what you will not miss, you are not giving. The Church says the poor have a claim on what you have.

## How to live it

- **Pick an amount** and give it before you spend on anything else.
- **Give in person** at least sometimes.
- **Do it quietly.**
- **Learn the works of mercy.**
