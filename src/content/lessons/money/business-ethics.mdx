## The short version

The Catechism says business leaders are "responsible to society" for their effects, and must consider "the good of persons and not only the increase of profits." Profits are "necessary" (CCC 2432).

## What the Church teaches

"Economic life is not meant solely to multiply goods produced and increase profit or power; it is ordered first of all to the service of persons" (CCC 2426).

"Those responsible for business enterprises are responsible to society for the economic and ecological effects of their operations. They have an obligation to consider the good of persons and not only the increase of profits. Profits are necessary, however" (CCC 2432).

"Everyone has the right of economic initiative; everyone should make legitimate use of his talents to contribute to the abundance that will benefit all" (CCC 2429).

"Access to employment and to professions must be open to all without unjust discrimination" (CCC 2433).

Fraud, unjust wages and taking advantage of "the ignorance or hardship of another" are against the seventh commandment (CCC 2409).

## Scripture

> "A deceitful balance is an abomination before the Lord: and a just weight is his will."
*Proverbs 11:1 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Douay-Rheims version of this proverb says a false balance is an abomination. Honest weights and measures are a biblical theme.

## Why this matters today

Many people think business is amoral. The Catechism says it has moral responsibilities.

The same text defends private initiative and profit. It is neither anti-business nor pro-profit-at-any-cost (CCC 2429, 2432).

## What's at stake

A business that ignores persons will hurt workers, customers and itself in the end.

## How to live it

- **Pay fairly** and deal honestly.
- **Check your supply chain.**
- **Count more than profit** when you decide.
- **Hire without discrimination.**
