## The short version

Catholic social teaching rests on the dignity of the person, the common good, subsidiarity and solidarity. The Catechism sets them out in its teaching on society (CCC 1877-1948).

## What the Church teaches

**Dignity.** "The human person... is and ought to be the principle, the subject and the end of all social institutions" (CCC 1881).

**The common good.** "The sum total of social conditions which allow people, either as groups or as individuals, to reach their fulfillment more fully and more easily" (CCC 1906).

**Subsidiarity.** "A community of a higher order should not interfere in the internal life of a community of a lower order, depriving the latter of its functions" (CCC 1883).

**Solidarity.** It "is a direct demand of human and Christian brotherhood" and shows itself "in the first place by the distribution of goods and remuneration for work" (CCC 1939-1940).

**Participation.** "It is necessary that all participate, each according to his position and role, in promoting the common good" (CCC 1913).

The Catechism also says "there exist also sinful inequalities that affect millions of men and women. These are in open contradiction of the Gospel" (CCC 1938).

## Scripture

> "And the king answering, shall say to them: Amen I say to you, as long as you did it to one of these my least brethren, you did it to me."
*Matthew 25:40 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus says that what you did for the least of his brethren, you did for him.

## Why this matters today

The Church's social teaching does not fit a party. It criticizes unjust inequality and also state overreach (CCC 1883, 1938).

It speaks in principles. People of good will apply them differently.

## What's at stake

If society is only individuals, nobody owes anyone. If it is only the state, persons disappear. The Church's picture is of persons and communities in order.

## How to live it

- **Learn the four terms** and use them.
- **Ask about each policy** what it does to the person.
- **Get involved** locally.
- **Read a short document,** such as part of Rerum Novarum.
