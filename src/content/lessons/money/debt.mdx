## The short version

Pay what you owe. Paul says "owe no man any thing, but to love one another" (Romans 13:8), and the Catechism says promises and contracts must be kept (CCC 2410).

## What the Church teaches

Paul says to owe no one anything except love (Romans 13:8).

Psalm 36 says the sinner borrows and does not pay back, while the just man shows mercy and gives (Psalm 36:21, Douay-Rheims numbering).

"Promises must be kept and contracts strictly observed to the extent that the commitments made in them are morally just" (CCC 2410).

Among the sins against justice the Catechism lists "paying unjust wages" and "business fraud," and it says taking and keeping what belongs to others is theft even if the civil law allows it (CCC 2409).

The Old Testament contained "the jubilee year of forgiveness of debts" and a "prohibition of loans at interest" as measures for the poor (CCC 2449).

## Scripture

> "Owe no man any thing, but to love one another. For he that loveth his neighbour, hath fulfilled the law."
*Romans 13:8 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "The sinner shall borrow, and not pay again; but the just sheweth mercy and shall give."
*Psalms 36:21 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Douay-Rheims numbers this psalm 36. In other Bibles it is 37.

## Why this matters today

Borrowing is not itself a sin. The question is whether you can and will repay, and whether the terms are just.

Predatory lending is a real injustice, and the Catechism condemns taking advantage of "the ignorance or hardship of another" (CCC 2409).

## What's at stake

Debt you cannot pay takes away your freedom, and it can do harm to the lender.

## How to live it

- **Borrow only** what you can repay.
- **Read the terms.**
- **If you are behind,** talk to the lender early.
- **Ask for help** from a counselor if you are overwhelmed.
