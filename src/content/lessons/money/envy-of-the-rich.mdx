## The short version

The Catechism says envy is "sadness at the sight of another's goods and the immoderate desire to acquire them for oneself, even unjustly." The tenth commandment forbids it (CCC 2538-2539).

## What the Church teaches

"The tenth commandment requires that envy be banished from the human heart" (CCC 2538).

"Envy is a capital sin. It refers to the sadness at the sight of another's goods and the immoderate desire to acquire them for oneself, even unjustly. When it wishes grave harm to a neighbor it is a mortal sin" (CCC 2539).

"Envy represents a form of sadness and therefore a refusal of charity; the baptized person should struggle against it by exercising good will. Envy often comes from pride" (CCC 2540).

The Church also says the rich must act justly: "love for the poor is incompatible with immoderate love of riches or their selfish use" (CCC 2445).

So the Church does not treat envy as a response to injustice. She says to oppose injustice and resist envy.

## Scripture

> "Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's house: neither shalt thou desire his wife, nor his servant, nor his handmaid, nor his ox, nor his ass, nor any thing that is his."
*Exodus 20:17 (Douay-Rheims)*

The tenth commandment forbids coveting a neighbor's house or goods.

## Why this matters today

Social media shows other people's wealth all day. That is a recipe for envy.

There is a real difference between envy and the desire for justice. The Church asks for the second and condemns the first.

## What's at stake

Envy poisons the person who has it first, and gets you nothing.

## How to live it

- **Notice** when you scroll and feel worse.
- **Thank God** for one thing you have.
- **Congratulate** someone who has what you want.
- **Give** to someone who has less.
