## The short version

An honest day's work for honest pay is a matter of justice. The Catechism lists "business fraud" and "paying unjust wages" as sins against the seventh commandment (CCC 2409).

## What the Church teaches

Among the forms of unjustly taking or keeping another's property the Catechism lists "business fraud," "paying unjust wages," and "forcing up prices by taking advantage of the ignorance or hardship of another" (CCC 2409).

"Promises must be kept and contracts strictly observed to the extent that the commitments made in them are morally just" (CCC 2410). That includes labor contracts.

Work is a duty: "If any one will not work, let him not eat." It "honors the Creator's gifts and the talents received from him" (CCC 2427).

Paul tells servants to work "as to the Lord" and not just to please men (Colossians 3:22-24).

## Scripture

> "Servants, obey in all things your masters according to the flesh, not serving to the eye, as pleasing men, but in simplicity of heart, fearing God. Whatsoever you do, do it from the heart, as to the Lord, and not to men: Knowing that you shall receive of the Lord the reward of inheritance. Serve ye the Lord Christ."
*Colossians 3:22-24 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Douay-Rheims addresses "servants." The principle, work as for the Lord, applies to all workers.

## Why this matters today

Padding time sheets, doing personal things on the clock and taking supplies are small thefts.

The same duty applies to employers: pay what was promised, on time (CCC 2434).

## What's at stake

Work is where most of us spend our days. If it is dishonest, a large part of life is too.

## How to live it

- **Do what you are paid for.**
- **Report** your hours accurately.
- **Keep your word** to customers and coworkers.
- **Offer your work** to God.
