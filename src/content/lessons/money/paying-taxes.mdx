## The short version

The Catechism says it is "morally obligatory to pay taxes, to exercise the right to vote, and to defend one's country" (CCC 2240). Jesus says to render to Caesar what is Caesar's.

## What the Church teaches

"Submission to authority and co-responsibility for the common good make it morally obligatory to pay taxes, to exercise the right to vote, and to defend one's country" (CCC 2240).

Jesus answers about the tribute: "Render therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar's: and to God the things that are God's" (Matthew 22:21).

Paul says to render to all their dues: tribute to whom tribute is due (Romans 13:7).

The Catechism also says "the citizen is obliged in conscience not to follow the directives of civil authorities when they are contrary to the demands of the moral order" (CCC 2242).

## Scripture

> "Tell us therefore what dost thou think, is it lawful to give tribute to Caesar, or not? But Jesus knowing their wickedness, said: Why do you tempt me, ye hypocrites? Shew me the coin of the tribute. And they offered him a penny. And Jesus saith to them: Whose image and inscription is this? They say to him: Caesar's. Then he saith to them: Render therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar's; and to God, the things that are God's."
*Matthew 22:17-21 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "Render therefore to all men their dues. Tribute, to whom tribute is due: custom, to whom custom: fear, to whom fear: honour, to whom honour."
*Romans 13:7 (Douay-Rheims)*

Both passages are about paying what is due to lawful authority.

## Why this matters today

Many people feel taxes are theft. The Catechism says paying them is a moral obligation, as part of the common good.

Evading taxes by lying is also dishonest (CCC 2409).

## What's at stake

A society where no one pays cannot support roads, courts, hospitals or the poor.

## How to live it

- **File honestly.**
- **Ask where your money goes,** and vote.
- **Give beyond taxes** to the poor.
- **Do not use cash** to avoid reporting income.
