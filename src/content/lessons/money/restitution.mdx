## The short version

If you took something that was not yours, the Church teaches that you must give it back. Restitution is a requirement of justice (CCC 2412).

## What the Church teaches

"In virtue of commutative justice, reparation for injustice committed requires the restitution of stolen goods to their owner" (CCC 2412).

"Jesus blesses Zacchaeus for his pledge: 'If I have defrauded anyone of anything, I restore it fourfold'" (CCC 2412).

"Those who, directly or indirectly, have taken possession of the goods of another, are obliged to make restitution of them, or to return the equivalent in kind or in money, if the goods have disappeared, as well as the profit or advantages their owner would have legitimately obtained from them" (CCC 2412).

The same applies to everyone who "ordered it, assisted in it, or received the stolen goods" (CCC 2412).

Confession alone does not cancel the debt. The Catechism says "many sins wrong our neighbor. One must do what is possible in order to repair the harm... Simple justice requires as much" (CCC 1459).

## Scripture

> "But Zacheus standing, said to the Lord: Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have wronged any man of any thing, I restore him fourfold."
*Luke 19:8 (Douay-Rheims)*

Zacchaeus does more than the law required. The Catechism holds him up as the model.

## Why this matters today

A lot of people feel forgiven and leave it there. The Church says the owner is still owed.

If you cannot find the owner or the owner would be hurt by it, a priest can help you work out how to make amends.

## What's at stake

If forgiveness meant no repair, the victim would pay for your sin.

## How to live it

- **List what you owe,** and to whom.
- **Start paying** in installments if you have to.
- **Ask a priest** if the situation is complicated.
- **Do it quietly** if public repayment would do harm.
