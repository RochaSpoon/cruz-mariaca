## The short version

Shoplifting is theft whatever the size of the store. The Catechism says theft is "usurping another's property against the reasonable will of the owner," and requires restitution (CCC 2408, 2412).

## What the Church teaches

"The seventh commandment forbids theft, that is, usurping another's property against the reasonable will of the owner" (CCC 2408). A store has an owner, even when the owner is a company.

"Those who, directly or indirectly, have taken possession of the goods of another, are obliged to make restitution of them, or to return the equivalent in kind or in money, if the goods have disappeared" (CCC 2412).

The obligation falls on "all who in some manner have taken part in a theft or who have knowingly benefited from it - for example, those who ordered it, assisted in it, or received the stolen goods" (CCC 2412).

Zacchaeus is the model. He says "If I have defrauded anyone of anything, I restore it fourfold," and Jesus blesses him (CCC 2412; Luke 19:8).

## Scripture

> "But Zacheus standing, said to the Lord: Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have wronged any man of any thing, I restore him fourfold. Jesus said to him: This day is salvation come to this house, because he also is a son of Abraham."
*Luke 19:8-9 (Douay-Rheims)*

Zacchaeus gives half of his goods to the poor and repays fourfold, and Jesus says salvation has come to his house.

## Why this matters today

People often say that everyone does it, or that the store builds it into the price. The Catechism does not accept either as a reason. The owner's will counts, not the owner's size.

You can make it right quietly. Pay for the item, or give the equivalent anonymously, and take it to Confession.

## What's at stake

The harm lands on workers, on honest customers and on you. Making it right is the way out.

## How to live it

- **Stop.**
- **Pay it back,** even anonymously (CCC 2412, 2487).
- **Confess it.**
- **Tell a friend** who is tempted that you changed.
