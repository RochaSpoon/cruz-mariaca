## The short version

The seventh commandment forbids theft, "usurping another's property against the reasonable will of the owner." It covers small things as well as big ones (CCC 2408-2409).

## What the Church teaches

"The seventh commandment forbids theft, that is, usurping another's property against the reasonable will of the owner" (CCC 2408).

"There is no theft if consent can be presumed or if refusal is contrary to reason and the universal destination of goods. This is the case in obvious and urgent necessity when the only way to provide for immediate, essential needs (food, shelter, clothing...) is to put at one's disposal and use the property of others" (CCC 2408).

"Even if it does not contradict the provisions of civil law, any form of unjustly taking and keeping the property of others is against the seventh commandment: thus, deliberate retention of goods lent or of objects lost; business fraud; paying unjust wages; forcing up prices by taking advantage of the ignorance or hardship of another" (CCC 2409).

Keeping what you found, or what was delivered to you by mistake, is a form of theft.

## Scripture

> "Thou shalt not steal."
*Exodus 20:15 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Douay-Rheims numbers this as the seventh commandment, as the Catholic catechism does.

## Why this matters today

Many people treat small theft as nothing: a pen from work, a streaming password, a song. The Catechism's definition does not set a minimum amount.

The Catechism does recognize extreme need. A starving person is not stealing when he takes food (CCC 2408).

## What's at stake

Stealing wrongs a person, and it shapes you. A small habit makes a bigger one easier.

## How to fight it

- **Return** what you borrowed or found.
- **Pay for what you use.**
- **Tell the truth** about change and billing errors.
- **Make restitution** for what you took. See that lesson.
