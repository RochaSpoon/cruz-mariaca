## The short version

The Catechism says human work comes from persons made in God's image and "called to prolong the work of creation." "Work is for man, not man for work" (CCC 2427-2428).

## What the Church teaches

"Human work proceeds directly from persons created in the image of God and called to prolong the work of creation by subduing the earth, both with and for one another. Hence work is a duty" (CCC 2427).

"Work honors the Creator's gifts and the talents received from him. It can also be redemptive" (CCC 2427).

"In work, the person exercises and fulfills in part the potential inscribed in his nature. The primordial value of labor stems from man himself, its author and its beneficiary. Work is for man, not man for work" (CCC 2428).

John Paul II wrote in Laborem Exercens that the primary basis of the value of work is the human person himself, who is its subject (Laborem Exercens 6).

Economic life "is ordered first of all to the service of persons" (CCC 2426).

## Scripture

> "And the Lord God took man, and put him into the paradise of pleasure, to dress it, and to keep it."
*Genesis 2:15 (Douay-Rheims)*

God places Adam in the garden "to dress it, and to keep it." Work comes before the fall.

## Why this matters today

Many people treat work as a necessary evil or as their whole identity. The Church avoids both.

Unemployment is a harm to dignity, not only to income (CCC 2436).

## What's at stake

If work is only a means to money, it feels empty. If it is the whole of life, it eats the rest.

## How to live it

- **Offer your work** each morning.
- **Do it well,** even when no one is checking.
- **Rest.** The Church treats Sunday rest as a part of this (CCC 2185).
- **Care for the unemployed** near you.
