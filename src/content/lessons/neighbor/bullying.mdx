## The short version

Bullying is a sin against the dignity of another person. The Catechism says the one who gives scandal "becomes his neighbor's tempter," and Jesus says what you do to the least of his brethren you do to him (CCC 2284; Matthew 25:40).

## What the Church teaches

"Respect for the reputation of persons forbids every attitude and word likely to cause them unjust injury" (CCC 2477).

The Catechism says that scandal, leading another to do evil, "takes on a particular gravity by reason of the authority of those who cause it or the weakness of those who are scandalized" (CCC 2285).

Jesus says that what you do to one of the least of his brethren, you do to him (Matthew 25:40).

Everyone is made in the image of God and has equal dignity (CCC 1700, 2334).

Responsibility is shared. We answer for sins we join "by ordering, advising, praising, or approving them" (CCC 1868). A bystander who laughs is part of it.

## Scripture

> "And the king answering, shall say to them: Amen I say to you, as long as you did it to one of these my least brethren, you did it to me."
*Matthew 25:40 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus says this about the hungry, the sick and the imprisoned. The Church extends it to anyone treated as less than human.

## Why this matters today

Bullying now reaches into the home through phones. The Church's standard has not changed.

Some who bully were bullied. Understanding that does not excuse it. It does point toward help for both people.

## What's at stake

Bullying can cause lasting harm, and the person doing it shapes themselves too.

## How to fight it

- **If you are being bullied,** tell an adult you trust.
- **If you see it,** do not join in and stand with the target when you safely can.
- **If you have done it,** apologize and stop.
- **Pray** for both the target and the one doing it.
