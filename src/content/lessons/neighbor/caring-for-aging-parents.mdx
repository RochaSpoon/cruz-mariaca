## The short version

The Catechism says grown children must give their parents "material and moral support in old age and in times of illness, loneliness, or distress" (CCC 2218).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism says the fourth commandment "reminds grown children of their responsibilities toward their parents. As much as they can, they must give them material and moral support in old age and in times of illness, loneliness, or distress" (CCC 2218).

"Filial respect promotes harmony in all of family life." It concerns "relationships between brothers and sisters" too (CCC 2219).

"The family should live in such a way that its members learn to care and take responsibility for the young, the old, the sick, the handicapped, and the poor" (CCC 2208).

Ecclesiasticus has a long passage on honoring father and mother, including in old age (Ecclesiasticus 3:1-16).

Some people "forgo marriage in order to care for their parents or brothers and sisters" and "can contribute greatly to the good of the human family" (CCC 2231).

## Scripture

> "Son, support the old age of thy father, and grieve him not in his life; And if his understanding fail, have patience with him, and despise him not when thou art in thy strength: for the relieving of the father shall not be forgotten."
*Ecclesiasticus 3:14-15 (Douay-Rheims)*

Sirach 3 is a long passage on honoring parents, including in their old age.

## Why this matters today

Caring for parents is often hard, expensive and exhausting. The Church recognizes this. The Catechism says families sometimes cannot provide the help and then others, and society, must (CCC 2208).

Care sometimes means a nursing home or paid help. Doing what is best for a parent is not abandonment.

## What's at stake

The way we treat the old shows what we think a person is worth.

## How to live it

- **Call** regularly.
- **Visit** and listen.
- **Share** the work among siblings.
- **Ask** for help when you need it.
