## The short version

Pope Francis's letter Patris Corde presents Joseph as a "tender and loving," "obedient," "accepting," "creatively courageous," "working" father, "a father in the shadows" (Patris Corde 2-7).

## What the Church teaches

Patris Corde (2020) describes Joseph in seven ways: a beloved father, a tender and loving father, an obedient father, an accepting father, a creatively courageous father, a working father, and a father in the shadows.

On tenderness the Pope writes: "Pointing fingers and judging others are frequently signs of an inability to accept our own weaknesses, our own frailty" (Patris Corde 2).

Paul tells husbands to love their wives as Christ loved the Church (Ephesians 5:25).

The Catechism says parents are "the first representatives of God for man," though human fatherhood only imperfectly reflects his (CCC 239).

It also says fathers must not "provoke" their children, and that parents should create "a home where tenderness, forgiveness, respect, fidelity, and disinterested service are the rule" (CCC 2223).

## Scripture

> "Husbands, love your wives, as Christ also loved the church, and delivered himself up for it:"
*Ephesians 5:25 (Douay-Rheims)*

The verse is addressed to husbands. The Church reads it as a standard of self-giving.

## Why this matters today

Some people grew up without a good father or none. The Church says God is the model, and that human fathers only partly show him (CCC 239).

Joseph is a good patron for anyone who works quietly and keeps faith.

## What's at stake

Children need to know that someone stays.

## How to live it

- **Show up.**
- **Work** honestly and quietly.
- **Pray** for your children by name.
- **Read Patris Corde.**
