## The short version

Correcting a friend's sin is part of charity. The Catechism says charity "demands beneficence and fraternal correction" (CCC 1829), and Jesus gives the steps (Matthew 18:15-17).

## What the Church teaches

"The fruits of charity are joy, peace, and mercy; charity demands beneficence and fraternal correction" (CCC 1829).

Jesus says: if your brother sins against you, go and tell him his fault between you and him alone. If he listens, you have gained your brother. If not, take one or two others, and then tell the Church (Matthew 18:15-17).

Paul says to restore a fallen person in a spirit of meekness and to watch yourself (Galatians 6:1).

The spiritual works of mercy include "instructing, advising, consoling, comforting... forgiving and bearing wrongs patiently" (CCC 2447).

The Catechism also says we share in another's sin if we fail to hinder it when we have a duty (CCC 1868).

## Scripture

> "But if thy brother shall offend against thee, go, and rebuke him between thee and him alone. If he shall hear thee, thou shalt gain thy brother. And if he will not hear thee, take with thee one or two more: that in the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may stand. And if he will not hear them: tell the church. And if he will not hear the church, let him be to thee as the heathen and publican."
*Matthew 18:15-17 (Douay-Rheims)*

The steps are private first, then small, then the Church. Public shaming is not among them.

## Why this matters today

Many people avoid correcting anyone because they do not want to judge. The Church says silence can be a failure of love.

Correction done with pride or anger does harm. Done with humility it can save a friend.

## What's at stake

If no one will tell you the truth, you stay where you are.

## How to live it

- **Pray first.**
- **Speak privately** and kindly.
- **Start with** what you admire.
- **Be ready** to be corrected yourself.
