## The short version

If you refuse to forgive, God will not forgive you. That isn't a church rule. It's what Jesus said, right after teaching the Our Father. Forgiveness doesn't mean what they did was okay, and it doesn't mean you have to trust them again. It means you give up your right to revenge and hand them over to God.

## What the Church teaches

The Our Father asks God to forgive us "as we forgive those who trespass against us." The Catechism warns that this outpouring of mercy can't reach a heart that hasn't forgiven those who trespass against us (CCC 2840). It also says forgiving isn't about feelings: "It is not in our power not to feel or to forget an offense; but the heart that offers itself to the Holy Spirit turns injury into compassion and purifies the memory in transforming hurt into intercession" (CCC 2843).

Deliberately hating a neighbor and wishing them serious harm is a grave sin (CCC 2303).

## Scripture

> "For if you will forgive men their offences, your heavenly Father will forgive you also your offences. But if you will not forgive men, neither will your Father forgive you your offences."
*Matthew 6:14-15 (Douay-Rheims)*

Peter asked if seven times was enough. Jesus said "seventy times seven" and told the parable of the servant who was forgiven a fortune and then strangled a man over pocket change. The master handed him "to the torturers until he paid all the debt. So also shall my heavenly Father do to you, if you forgive not every one his brother from your hearts" (Matthew 18:21-35).

> "Revenge not yourselves, my dearly beloved; but give place unto wrath, for it is written: Revenge is mine, I will repay, saith the Lord."
*Romans 12:19 (Douay-Rheims)*

From the cross: "Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do" (Luke 23:34).

## The saints

St. Maria Goretti was 11 when Alessandro Serenelli stabbed her 14 times during an attempted rape in 1902. Before she died, she forgave him and said she wanted him in heaven with her. Years later, in prison, he repented. After his release he asked her mother's forgiveness, and they received Communion together. He attended her canonization in 1950 and spent the rest of his life as a lay brother with the Capuchins.

In 1983, St. John Paul II visited Mehmet Ali Ağca in prison, the man who shot him in St. Peter's Square in 1981, and forgave him in person.

## What forgiveness is NOT

- **Not saying it didn't matter.** It mattered. That's why it needs forgiving.
- **Not trust.** You can forgive someone and still not let them back in. Trust has to be rebuilt.
- **Not staying in danger.** If someone is abusing you, forgiving them doesn't mean staying. Get safe and tell someone.
- **Not skipping justice.** Crimes can and should be reported.
- **Not a feeling.** It's a decision you may have to make again every day.

## Why this hits hard today

Grudges are easy to feed now. You can keep scrolling their profile, rereading old texts, and rehashing it with friends. Family feuds go on for years. Cancel culture rarely makes room for repentance. Christians can be different.

## What's at stake

Your own forgiveness. Jesus could not have been clearer. A grudge you refuse to drop is a door you're closing on God's mercy for yourself.

## How to forgive

1. **Name the hurt honestly.** Write it down.
2. **Decide to forgive.** Say it, even if you don't feel it: "In the name of Jesus, I forgive ___ for ___."
3. **Pray for them by name.** Every time the anger comes back.
4. **Stop rehearsing it.** Don't feed it. Mute, unfollow, stop retelling the story.
5. **Bring it to Confession** if you've held hatred.
6. **Give it time.** Feelings follow decisions slowly.
