## The short version

You can bring your anger at God to him. Job, the psalms and Lamentations do. The Catechism says Jesus himself cried out from the cross, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" (CCC 603).

## What the Church teaches

"Jesus did not experience reprobation as if he himself had sinned. But in the redeeming love that always united him to the Father, he assumed us in the state of our waywardness of sin, to the point that he could say in our name from the cross: 'My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?'" (CCC 603).

The Bible includes honest complaint. Job accuses God and God answers him (Job 38-42). Lamentations 3 is a long cry.

Psalm 21 in the Douay-Rheims numbering begins with the words Jesus prayed on the cross.

The Catechism says "prayer is both a gift of grace and a determined response on our part" and that it is a battle (CCC 2725).

It describes dryness as a moment of "sheer faith clinging faithfully to Jesus" (CCC 2731).

## Scripture

> "O God my God, look upon me: why hast thou forsaken me? Far from my salvation are the words of my sins. O my God, I shall cry by day, and thou wilt not hear: and by night, and it shall not be reputed as folly in me."
*Psalms 21:2-3 (Douay-Rheims)*

The psalm begins with abandonment and ends with praise. The Church prays it on Good Friday.

## Why this matters today

Many people leave faith because they think anger at God is the end of it. The Bible says it can be the start of a more honest prayer.

If your anger comes from real suffering, talk to someone you trust as well.

## What's at stake

If you cannot be honest with God, you cannot speak to him at all.

## How to live it

- **Tell him** exactly what you feel.
- **Read** a lament psalm aloud.
- **Keep** showing up.
- **Tell** a priest or friend.
