## The short version

"It is not good for man to be alone" (Genesis 2:18). The Catechism says society is "a requirement of his nature" (CCC 1879), and Hebrews tells believers not to forsake the assembly.

## What the Church teaches

God says in the beginning that it is not good for the man to be alone (Genesis 2:18).

"The human person needs to live in society. Society is not for him an extraneous addition but a requirement of his nature. Through the exchange with others, mutual service and dialogue with his brethren, man develops his potential" (CCC 1879).

Hebrews tells us to consider one another so as to stir up charity and good works, and not to forsake our assembly (Hebrews 10:24-25).

The Catechism says the Sunday gathering "dates from the beginnings of the apostolic age" (CCC 2178).

Christ himself felt abandonment on the cross, and the Catechism says he took our state upon himself (CCC 603).

## Scripture

> "And the Lord God said: It is not good for man to be alone: let us make him a help like unto himself."
*Genesis 2:18 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And let us consider one another, to provoke unto charity and to good works: Not forsaking our assembly, as some are accustomed; but comforting one another, and so much the more as you see the day approaching."
*Hebrews 10:24-25 (Douay-Rheims)*

The first passage is about the human need for company. The second is the Church's reason for gathering.

## Why this matters today

Many people are lonely while surrounded by contacts. The Church's answer is a community with faces: a parish, a group, a family.

If loneliness becomes heavy or hopeless, tell someone. This lesson is not therapy.

## What's at stake

You were made for communion. Isolation starves something real.

## How to live it

- **Go** to Mass and stay five minutes.
- **Join** one small group.
- **Call** one person this week.
- **Pray** the psalms of the lonely.
