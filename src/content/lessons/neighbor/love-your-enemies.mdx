## The short version

Jesus' hardest command is to love your enemies. The Catechism says "the Lord asks us to love as he does, even our enemies" (CCC 1825).

## What the Church teaches

"Christ died out of love for us, while we were still 'enemies.' The Lord asks us to love as he does, even our enemies, to make ourselves the neighbor of those farthest away, and to love children and the poor as Christ himself" (CCC 1825).

"Christian prayer extends to the forgiveness of enemies, transfiguring the disciple by configuring him to his Master" (CCC 2844).

The Catechism says it is "impossible to keep the Lord's commandment by imitating the divine model from outside." It takes "a vital participation, coming from the depths of the heart." Only the Spirit makes it possible (CCC 2842).

"It is not in our power not to feel or to forget an offense; but the heart that offers itself to the Holy Spirit turns injury into compassion and purifies the memory in transforming the hurt into intercession" (CCC 2843).

Loving an enemy does not mean approving what they do. It means wishing them good and refusing to hate (CCC 2303).

## Scripture

> "You have heard that it hath been said, Thou shalt love thy neighbour, and hate thy enemy. But I say to you, Love your enemies: do good to them that hate you: and pray for them that persecute and calumniate you: That you may be the children of your Father who is in heaven, who maketh his sun to rise upon the good, and bad, and raineth upon the just and the unjust. For if you love them that love you, what reward shall you have? do not even the publicans this? And if you salute your brethren only, what do you more? do not also the heathens this? Be you therefore perfect, as also your heavenly Father is perfect."
*Matthew 5:43-48 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus says the Father makes his sun rise on the good and the evil. The command is to be like the Father.

## Why this matters today

Many people hear this as meaning you must let others hurt you. It does not. Love for an enemy still allows boundaries, justice and defense (CCC 2265).

The hardest part is not feeling it. The Catechism says forgiveness starts with offering the hurt to the Spirit (CCC 2843).

## What's at stake

If you only love people who love you, you are no different from anyone else.

## How to live it

- **Pray for** one person you resent, every day for a week.
- **Do one small good** for them if it is safe.
- **Keep** your boundaries.
- **Ask for the grace** and do not wait to feel it.
