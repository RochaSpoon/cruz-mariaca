## The short version

Disagree on policy without hating people. The Catechism says citizens have "co-responsibility for the common good" and should serve it "in a spirit of truth, justice, solidarity, and freedom," and that hatred is contrary to charity (CCC 2239, 2303).

## What the Church teaches

"It is the duty of citizens to contribute along with the civil authorities to the good of society in a spirit of truth, justice, solidarity, and freedom" (CCC 2239).

"Deliberate hatred is contrary to charity" (CCC 2303). Jesus says to love enemies and pray for persecutors (CCC 2303).

"Every good Christian ought to be more ready to give a favorable interpretation to another's statement than to condemn it" (CCC 2478).

"The citizen is obliged in conscience not to follow the directives of civil authorities when they are contrary to the demands of the moral order" (CCC 2242).

Paul tells believers to be subject to governing authorities (Romans 13:1-7), and the Catechism says Christians owe the community participation and respect.

## Scripture

> "Let every soul be subject to higher powers: for there is no power but from God: and those that are, are ordained of God. Therefore he that resisteth the power, resisteth the ordinance of God. And they that resist, purchase to themselves damnation. For princes are not a terror to the good work, but to the evil. Wilt thou then not be afraid of the power? Do that which is good: and thou shalt have praise from the same. For he is God's minister to thee, for good. But if thou do that which is evil, fear: for he beareth not the sword in vain. For he is God's minister: an avenger to execute wrath upon him that doth evil. Wherefore be subject of necessity, not only for wrath, but also for conscience' sake. For therefore also you pay tribute. For they are the ministers of God, serving unto this purpose. Render therefore to all men their dues. Tribute, to whom tribute is due: custom, to whom custom: fear, to whom fear: honour, to whom honour."
*Romans 13:1-7 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul wrote this under Roman rule. The Church reads it together with the duty to refuse what is contrary to conscience (CCC 2242).

## Why this matters today

Politics is often treated as a war. The Church asks for the opposite habit toward persons.

You can think a policy is gravely wrong and still not hate those who hold it.

## What's at stake

If every disagreement is hatred, a society cannot hold together.

## How to live it

- **Argue** the issue, not the person.
- **Talk** to someone on the other side.
- **Pray** for leaders by name.
- **Vote** and then keep the peace.
