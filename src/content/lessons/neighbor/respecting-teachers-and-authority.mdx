## The short version

The fourth commandment extends to "all who for our good have received authority in society from God." Authority is a service, and does not extend to commanding what is "contrary to the dignity of persons" (CCC 2234-2235).

## What the Church teaches

"God's fourth commandment also enjoins us to honor all who for our good have received authority in society from God. It clarifies the duties of those who exercise authority as well as those who benefit from it" (CCC 2234).

"Those who exercise authority should do so as a service." And "no one can command or establish what is contrary to the dignity of persons and the natural law" (CCC 2235).

Those subject to authority "should regard those in authority as representatives of God." They have "the right, and at times the duty, to voice their just criticisms" (CCC 2238).

Hebrews tells believers to obey their leaders, who keep watch over their souls (Hebrews 13:17).

"The citizen is obliged in conscience not to follow the directives of civil authorities when they are contrary to the demands of the moral order" (CCC 2242).

## Scripture

> "Obey your prelates, and be subject to them. For they watch as being to render an account of your souls; that they may do this with joy, and not with grief. For this is not expedient for you."
*Hebrews 13:17 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul also tells the Romans to be subject to the higher powers (Romans 13:1-7).

## Why this matters today

Many students resent teachers, and some teachers are poor. The Church asks for respect and also allows honest criticism.

Respect is not the same as agreement. It shows in tone and effort.

## What's at stake

If no one respects authority, no one can teach or lead.

## How to live it

- **Listen** and do the work.
- **Disagree** respectfully.
- **Thank** a teacher.
- **Report** abuse of authority to the proper person.
