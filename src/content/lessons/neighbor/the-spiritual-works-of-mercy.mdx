## The short version

The spiritual works of mercy are the ways we help a neighbor's soul. The Catechism names instructing, advising, consoling, comforting, forgiving and bearing wrongs patiently (CCC 2447).

## What the Church teaches

"Instructing, advising, consoling, comforting are spiritual works of mercy, as are forgiving and bearing wrongs patiently" (CCC 2447).

The traditional list of seven adds admonishing sinners and praying for the living and the dead. The Catechism's own wording lists the ones quoted here.

Charity "demands beneficence and fraternal correction" (CCC 1829). Admonishing in love is a work of mercy.

The Church has "from the beginning" offered prayers for the dead (CCC 1032).

Forgiveness is "the fundamental condition of the reconciliation of the children of God with their Father and of men with one another" (CCC 2844).

## Scripture

> "Put ye on therefore, as the elect of God, holy, and beloved, the bowels of mercy, benignity, humility, modesty, patience: Bearing with one another, and forgiving one another, if any have a complaint against another: even as the Lord hath forgiven you, so do you also. But above all these things have charity, which is the bond of perfection:"
*Colossians 3:12-14 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul lists mercy, kindness, humility, meekness and patience, bearing with one another and forgiving, over all of which is charity.

## Why this matters today

Many people think mercy means money. The Church says the soul matters as much as the body.

A kind word, a listening ear and a prayer are works of mercy.

## What's at stake

The body can be fed and the person still be lost. Mercy aims at the whole person.

## How to live it

- **Listen** to someone for ten minutes without fixing them.
- **Forgive** a debt of the heart.
- **Pray** for someone by name.
- **Share** what you know, kindly.
