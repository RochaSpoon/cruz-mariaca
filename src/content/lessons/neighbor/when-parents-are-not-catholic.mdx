## The short version

The Catechism says "family ties are important but not absolute." You still honor your parents, and you can follow your own conscience and call (CCC 2217, 2232-2233).

## What the Church teaches

"As long as a child lives at home with his parents, the child should obey his parents in all that they ask of him when it is for his good or that of the family" (CCC 2217).

"But if a child is convinced in conscience that it would be morally wrong to obey a particular order, he must not do so" (CCC 2217).

"Family ties are important but not absolute. Just as the child grows to maturity and human and spiritual autonomy, so his unique vocation which comes from God asserts itself more clearly and forcefully" (CCC 2232).

"Becoming a disciple of Jesus means accepting the invitation to belong to God's family" (CCC 2233).

Jesus says whoever loves father or mother more than him is not worthy of him (Matthew 10:37).

Respect for parents continues, and so does honor, even when you disagree (CCC 2214-2215).

## Scripture

> "Do not think that I came to send peace upon earth: I came not to send peace, but the sword. For I came to set a man at variance against his father, and the daughter against her mother, and the daughter in law against her mother in law. And a man's enemies shall be they of his own household. He that loveth father or mother more than me, is not worthy of me; and he that loveth son or daughter more than me, is not worthy of me."
*Matthew 10:34-37 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus is not telling people to despise their families. He is saying he comes first.

## Why this matters today

Some people come to faith against their family's wishes. Others stay Catholic when their parents have left. Both can be hard.

Gentleness, patience and honesty are the Church's advice. Do not use your faith as a weapon.

## What's at stake

Faith should make you a better child, not a worse one.

## How to live it

- **Be** respectful and honest.
- **Keep** showing up for family events.
- **Pray** for your parents.
- **Be patient.**
