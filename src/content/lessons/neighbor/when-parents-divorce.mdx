## The short version

When parents divorce, it is not the children's fault. The Catechism says divorce brings grave harm to "children traumatized by the separation of their parents" (CCC 2385).

## What the Church teaches

"Divorce is immoral also because it introduces disorder into the family and into society. This disorder brings grave harm to the deserted spouse, to children traumatized by the separation of their parents and often torn between them, and because of its contagious effect which makes it truly a plague on society" (CCC 2385).

The Church's concern is for the children, "traumatized by the separation of their parents" (CCC 2385). It does not lay the blame on them.

The Catechism says that in some situations separation is permitted, such as when living together has become practically impossible (CCC 1649). A person who is the innocent victim of a divorce is not committing a sin (CCC 2386).

The psalmist says that even if his father and mother forsake him, the Lord will take him up (Psalm 26:10, in the Douay-Rheims numbering).

## Scripture

> "For my father and my mother have left me: but the Lord hath taken me up."
*Psalms 26:10 (Douay-Rheims)*

This is Psalm 26 in the Douay-Rheims numbering and 27 in other Bibles.

## Why this matters today

Many children carry guilt for something they did not cause. The Church says it is not theirs.

If your parents divorced, you are not less loved by God or by the Church. Parishes and counselors can help.

## What's at stake

A child who believes it is their fault carries a burden that is not theirs.

## How to live it

- **Tell someone** how you feel.
- **Keep** loving both parents.
- **Do not** carry messages between them.
- **Pray** the Psalm.
