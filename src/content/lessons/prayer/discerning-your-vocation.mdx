## The short version

Your vocation is what God is calling you to do with your life. For Catholics there are four paths: marriage, priesthood, consecrated life, or the single life lived for God. Each is a way of holiness.

## What the Church teaches

Everyone is called to holiness. The question is the form. The Catechism treats marriage (CCC 1601-1666), holy orders (CCC 1533-1600) and consecrated life (CCC 914-933) as the paths that shape a life.

The young Samuel hears his name called at night and answers, "Speak, Lord, for thy servant heareth" (1 Kings 3:10, in the Douay numbering).

You do not find a vocation by thinking alone. It comes through prayer, honest self-knowledge, trusted counsel and what you are drawn toward over time.

Being single is not a failure. For some people it is a calling, and for many it is a stage.

## Scripture

> "And the Lord came and stood: and he called, as he had called the other times: Samuel, Samuel. And Samuel said: Speak, Lord, for thy servant heareth."
*1 Kings 3:10 (Douay-Rheims)*

## Why this matters today

Everyone asks what you will do for a living. Few ask what you are called to be. The questions are different.

## What's at stake

Many people drift into a life by default. The Church asks you to choose on purpose, with God in the question.

## How to live it

- **Pray a simple line daily:** "Lord, what do you want of me?"
- **Talk to people living each vocation.** A married couple, a priest, a sister.
- **Try a retreat** or a discernment weekend.
- **Do not hold off on holiness** until you decide. Start now, where you are.
