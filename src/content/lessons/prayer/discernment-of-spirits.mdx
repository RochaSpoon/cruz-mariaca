## The short version

Discernment of spirits is the practice of telling which inner movements come from God and which do not. St. Ignatius wrote rules for it (Spiritual Exercises 313-336).

## What the Church teaches

John says not to believe every spirit, but to test whether they are from God (1 John 4:1).

Ignatius noticed two patterns. Consolation is a lift toward God, with peace, hope and love. Desolation is a pull away, with heaviness, doubt and restlessness (Spiritual Exercises, Rules for the Discernment of Spirits).

His practical advice: in desolation, do not make big decisions. Do not change what you decided in a time of consolation. Wait. This is his fifth rule: in desolation never make a change, but stay firm in the decisions made before it.

A good test is whether the thing agrees with Scripture and the Church's teaching, and whether wise people who know you see it too.

## Scripture

> "Dearly beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits if they be of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world."
*1 John 4:1 (Douay-Rheims)*

## Why this matters today

People say "I felt God telling me to" about everything from a job to a breakup. Some of it is God. Some is wishful thinking. Some is not good at all.

## What's at stake

A voice in your head that always agrees with what you already wanted is worth doubting. So is one that tells you to cut off the people who love you.

## How to live it

- **Keep a journal.** Note when you feel peace and when you feel agitation.
- **Wait on big decisions.** Two weeks at least.
- **Run it past a mature Catholic.** Not only friends who will agree.
- **Check it against Scripture and the Church.** If it contradicts them, drop it.
