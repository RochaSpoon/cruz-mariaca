## The short version

Dry prayer is when you pray and feel nothing. It happens to nearly everyone, saints included. The Church says keep going (CCC 2731).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism lists dryness among the main struggles in prayer and says the answer is perseverance and humility (CCC 2731). [VERIFY]

St. John of the Cross wrote about the dark night, a stretch where God seems absent and the soul is being drawn deeper. His book, Dark Night of the Soul, is the standard reading. [VERIFY]

Jesus himself asked his disciples to watch and pray with him in Gethsemane, and warned that the spirit is willing but the flesh is weak (Matthew 26:40-41).

Dryness is not always spiritual. Poor sleep, depression, grief and plain tiredness can all feel the same. If it lasts and affects the rest of life, see a doctor or a counselor as well as a priest.

## Scripture

> "And he cometh to his disciples, and findeth them asleep, and he saith to Peter: What? Could you not watch one hour with me? Watch ye, and pray that ye enter not into temptation. The spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh weak."
*Matthew 26:40-41 (Douay-Rheims)*

## Why this matters today

We expect prayer to deliver a feeling. When it does not, we assume it is broken.

## What's at stake

Many people quit in this stretch, right before it would have changed. The Church's advice is not to quit.

## How to live it

- **Show up anyway.** Cut it to five minutes if you must, but do not skip.
- **Say it honestly:** "I feel nothing, and I am here."
- **Change the form.** Walk, use a psalm, use the Rosary.
- **Tell a priest or a spiritual director.**
- **Rule out the body.** Sleep and health matter.
