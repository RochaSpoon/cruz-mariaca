## The short version

Gratitude is the habit of noticing what you have been given. The word Eucharist itself means thanksgiving (CCC 1328). Paul says to give thanks in all things (1 Thessalonians 5:18).

## What the Church teaches

Ten lepers were healed, and only one came back to thank Jesus. Jesus asks where the other nine are (Luke 17:11-19).

Thanks is not pretending all is well. Paul writes it from prison. It means finding something real to thank God for, even in a hard season.

At Mass, the central prayer is called the Eucharistic Prayer, from the Greek for thanksgiving. The Church's whole worship is built on it.

And Jesus answering, said, Were not ten made clean? and where are the nine? There is no one found to return and give glory to God, but this stranger."
*Luke 17:15-18 (Douay-Rheims)*

## Why this matters today

Advertising works by making you feel you lack something. Gratitude pushes back by noticing what you already have.

## What's at stake

An ungrateful person is hard to please and hard to live with. A grateful one often has less, and enjoys it more.

## How to live it

- **Write three things each night.** Not general. Specific.
- **Say grace before meals,** even in a restaurant.
- **Thank someone in person** this week. Say why.
- **Thank God for the hard thing,** if you can. Or at least for the people around you in it.
