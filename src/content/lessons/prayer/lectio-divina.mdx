## The short version

Lectio divina means "divine reading." It is a slow way of praying with a short passage of Scripture, in four steps: read, reflect, respond, rest.

## What the Church teaches

The four steps are *lectio* (read), *meditatio* (reflect), *oratio* (respond in prayer) and *contemplatio* (rest in God). Monks have used this method for centuries.

The Second Vatican Council urges all the faithful to read Scripture and to let prayer go with it, so that a conversation takes place between God and the reader (Dei Verbum 25).

The Catechism lists reading Scripture among the main ways of meditation (CCC 2708).

Many Protestants practice something very similar. It is common ground.

## Scripture

> "But Mary kept all these words, pondering them in her heart."
*Luke 2:19 (Douay-Rheims)*

## Why this matters today

Most people read a lot but take in very little. Lectio is the opposite: very little, taken in deeply.

## What's at stake

If the Bible stays a book you only argue about, you will miss what it is for. Lectio is a way to let it speak first.

## How to live it

- **Pick one short passage.** Five to ten verses.
- **Read it twice,** slowly. Out loud if you can.
- **Stop at one word or line that catches you.** Stay there.
- **Talk to God about it.** What does it stir up?
- **Sit in silence** for a minute. Do not fill it.
