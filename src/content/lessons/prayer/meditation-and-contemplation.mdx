## The short version

Christian meditation is thinking with God about his word. Contemplation is simply being with him, with few words (CCC 2705-2724). Both differ from Eastern techniques of emptying the mind.

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism says meditation is a quest, engaging thought, imagination, emotion and desire to deepen faith (CCC 2705). It seeks to understand why and how the Lord is asking you to live.

Contemplation is a gaze of faith fixed on Jesus, "a gift" and not a technique you can force (CCC 2709-2719). [VERIFY]

Mary is the model. The Gospel says she kept all these things, pondering them in her heart (Luke 2:19).

The Church has warned that methods borrowed from other religions can confuse prayer with a technique for calming the self. The Vatican letter on this was issued in 1989. [VERIFY]

## Scripture

> "But Mary kept all these words, pondering them in her heart."
*Luke 2:19 (Douay-Rheims)*

## Why this matters today

Apps now sell "meditation" as stress relief. That is not the same thing. Christian prayer is aimed at someone, not at a calm feeling.

## What's at stake

If meditation is only self-improvement, it will end with you. If it is meeting someone, it will change you in ways you did not plan.

## How to live it

- **Begin with Scripture,** not a blank mind.
- **Picture the scene.** Be a bystander at the Gospel.
- **Then be quiet.** Do not rush to fill it.
- **Do not chase experiences.** If it is dry, stay anyway.
