## The short version

A novena is nine days of prayer, usually for a specific intention. The tradition goes back to the nine days the apostles prayed between the Ascension and Pentecost (Acts 1:14).

## What the Church teaches

After Jesus ascended, the apostles stayed together in prayer with Mary and the women, waiting for the Spirit (Acts 1:14). The Church looks back on those days of waiting as the model for a novena.

The word novena comes from the Latin for nine.  It became a popular devotion for any pressing need.

A novena is not a way to bargain with God. You are not twisting his arm by praying nine times instead of once. You are making a decision to keep asking.

The Church encourages novenas but does not require them. They are optional.

## Scripture

> "All these were persevering with one mind in prayer with the women, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brethren."
*Acts 1:14 (Douay-Rheims)*

## Why this matters today

Many people only ask for things once and then give up. A novena builds persistence into the asking.

## What's at stake

If you use a novena like a spell, you will be disappointed. If you use it to keep praying, it works on you.

## How to live it

- **Pick one intention.** Something real.
- **Choose a prayer** and set a time for nine days.
- **Do not skip.** If you miss one, keep going.
- **Pray for what is good for the person,** not only what you want.
