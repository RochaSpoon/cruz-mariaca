## The short version

A pilgrimage is a trip to a holy place made on purpose, as prayer. The Catechism names shrines and pilgrimages as places and times where the Church helps people pray (CCC 2691).

## What the Church teaches

Pilgrimage is old and Jewish. Joseph and Mary went to Jerusalem every year for the Passover, and they took Jesus with them (Luke 2:41-42).

Christians went to Jerusalem, Rome, and the tombs of apostles and martyrs. Others followed the road to Santiago de Compostela in Spain.

The point is not the place. It is the intention to leave your routine, walk, pray and be changed along the way. The place helps. It is not magic.

You do not need to cross an ocean. A nearby shrine, cathedral or abbey will do.

## Scripture

> "And his parents went every year to Jerusalem, at the solemn day of the pasch, And when he was twelve years old, they going up into Jerusalem, according to the custom of the feast,"
*Luke 2:41-42 (Douay-Rheims)*

## Why this matters today

Most travel is about comfort and photos. A pilgrimage is the opposite. It is slow, it costs something, and you go to receive.

## What's at stake

A pilgrimage that turns into tourism is a nice trip. That is fine. But it is a different thing.

## How to live it

- **Pick a nearby shrine** and go for a day.
- **Fast or walk part of it.** Make it cost something small.
- **Go to Confession** before or during.
- **Bring intentions** for other people.
- **Leave your phone** in the bag for the hours that count.
