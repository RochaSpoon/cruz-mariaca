## The short version

Sacramentals are blessed objects and actions, like holy water, medals, rosaries and the Sign of the Cross. They are not sacraments, but they help you turn toward God (CCC 1667-1679).

## What the Church teaches

The Church says sacramentals prepare us to receive grace and make different circumstances of life holy (CCC 1667). [VERIFY] They are set up by the Church, not by Christ.

They work by the Church's prayer and by your faith. They are not magic. A medal in your pocket does not protect you the way a seatbelt does.

Acts records that people carried handkerchiefs that had touched Paul to the sick, and they were healed (Acts 19:11-12). Catholics see an early echo of how God can use material things.

Protestants often worry that this slides into superstition. The worry is fair, and the Church shares it.

## Scripture

> "And God wrought by the hand of Paul more than common miracles. So that even there were brought from his body to the sick, handkerchiefs and aprons, and the diseases departed from them, and the wicked spirits went out of them."
*Acts 19:11-12 (Douay-Rheims)*

## Why this matters today

A lot of people keep medals, holy cards and crucifixes without thinking about them. They can be small reminders, or they can be good-luck charms.

## What's at stake

The line between devotion and superstition is thin. The Church says faith in God is what matters, not the object.

## How to live it

- **Bless yourself with holy water** when you enter a church.
- **Keep a crucifix** in your room.
- **Ask a priest to bless** a home or an object.
- **Do not treat them as lucky charms.** If it starts to feel like that, put it away.
