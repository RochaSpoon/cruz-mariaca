## The short version

God often speaks in quiet. Elijah did not find him in the wind or the fire but in a gentle whisper (3 Kings 19:11-13). If you never have silence, it is hard to hear.

## What the Church teaches

Elijah stood on the mountain. A great wind came, then an earthquake, then fire. The Lord was in none of them. Then came "a whistling of a gentle air," and Elijah covered his face (3 Kings 19:11-13, called 1 Kings 19 in modern Bibles).

The Psalm says, "Be still, and see that I am God" (Psalm 45:11 in the Douay numbering).

Christian tradition has always valued silence, from the desert monks to the Trappists. Silence is not empty. It is room.

## Scripture

> "And after the earthquake a fire: the Lord is not in the fire, and after the fire a whistling of a gentle air."
*3 Kings 19:12 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "Be still and see that I am God; I will be exalted among the nations, and I will be exalted in the earth."
*Psalms 45:11 (Douay-Rheims)*

## Why this matters today

Most people are never alone with their thoughts. Earbuds, screens and background noise fill every gap.

## What's at stake

If you cannot be quiet, you cannot hear much else, including your own conscience.

## How to live it

- **Start with five minutes** of no input. No phone, no music.
- **Turn off notifications** for one hour a day.
- **Walk without earbuds.**
- **Go to a church when it is empty** and sit.
