## The short version

A spiritual director is a wise person who meets with you regularly to help you notice what God is doing in your life. It is not therapy and it is not Confession (CCC 2690).

## What the Church teaches

The Bible has long praised wise counsel. "If thou see a man of understanding, go to him early in the morning, and let thy foot wear the steps of his door" (Ecclesiasticus 6:36).

In Acts, Philip asks the Ethiopian if he understands what he reads. The man answers, "How can I, unless some man show me?" (Acts 8:30-31).

A director can be a priest, a religious sister or brother, or a trained layperson. You usually meet once a month. They listen, ask questions and help you test what you think you are hearing.

The Catechism lists spiritual direction among the places where prayer is learned (CCC 2690). [VERIFY]

## Why this matters today

Most people take advice from friends, podcasts and social media. Few have anyone who knows them well and has no stake in what they decide.

## What's at stake

Going it alone is a common way to fool yourself. A good director can save you years of circling.

## How to live it

- **Ask your pastor** who offers direction in the parish or diocese.
- **Try a retreat house.** Many have directors on staff.
- **Be honest.** A director cannot help with what you will not say.
- **Do not treat them as an oracle.** They help you discern. The decision is yours.
