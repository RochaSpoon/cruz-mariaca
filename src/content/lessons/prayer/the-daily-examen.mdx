## The short version

The Examen is a short nightly prayer from St. Ignatius of Loyola. You look back over your day with God, thank him, notice where you fell short, and plan to do better (Spiritual Exercises 43).

## What the Church teaches

Ignatius sets out the general examen in five points in the Spiritual Exercises (no. 43), starting with thanks to God for the gifts received.

A common pattern has five steps: give thanks, ask for light, review the day, say sorry for what needs it, and look ahead to tomorrow.

It takes ten or fifteen minutes. It is not an interrogation. The point is to notice where God was at work, and where you turned away.

Christians of other traditions use the Examen too, and it is often taught in non-Catholic schools. It is not a distinctly Catholic technique.

## Why this matters today

Most days run together. You end them with a phone in your hand and no sense of what happened.

## What's at stake

Someone who never looks back at their day tends to repeat the same mistakes for years. A small review changes that.

## How to live it

- **Do it in bed or at the kitchen table.** Same time each night.
- **Start with thanks.** Name three good things.
- **Go through the day** from morning to now. Where were you kind? Where were you not?
- **Say sorry plainly,** then drop it. Do not wallow.
- **Pick one thing for tomorrow.** Just one.
