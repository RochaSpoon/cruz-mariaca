## The short version

The Liturgy of the Hours is the Church's daily prayer, built mostly from the Psalms, at set times through the day (CCC 1174-1178). Priests and religious must pray it. Anyone can.

## What the Church teaches

The practice goes back to the early Church and to Jewish prayer. The Psalmist says, "Seven times a day I have given praise to thee" (Psalm 118:164, Douay numbering).

Morning Prayer and Evening Prayer are the two main hours (CCC 1175). [VERIFY] The others are the Office of Readings, Daytime Prayer and Night Prayer.

The Psalms are spread over a four-week cycle, so you pray the whole Psalter every month.

Anglicans have a similar practice in the Daily Office, and the Orthodox have their own cycle of daily services. This is shared Christian ground.

## Scripture

> "Seven times a day I have given praise to thee, for the judgments of thy justice."
*Psalms 118:164 (Douay-Rheims)*

## Why this matters today

The hours are easy to find now. There are apps and websites that put the day's prayers on your phone.

## What's at stake

It is a discipline. You will skip days. The point is to pray in step with the whole Church, even when you are alone.

## How to live it

- **Start with Evening Prayer.** It takes about fifteen minutes.
- **Use an app or a book.** Do not try to piece it together yourself.
- **Pray the Psalms as they come.** Even the angry ones.
- **Pray it with others** if you can. A parish or a friend.
