## The short version

The Church has no neat answer to why God allows suffering. The Catechism says plainly that no quick answer will satisfy, and that the whole of Christian faith is the response (CCC 309).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism says God permits evil and brings a greater good from it, but does not explain each case (CCC 311-312). [VERIFY] That can sound hollow when a child is dying. The Church knows it.

Job lost everything, and God never gave him a reason. He gave him himself (Job 38 to 42). Job also said, "The Lord gave, and the Lord hath taken away" (Job 1:21).

Paul says that to those who love God, all things work together unto good (Romans 8:28). He does not say all things are good.

Christians point to the cross: God did not stand outside suffering. He entered it.

## Scripture

> "And said: Naked came I out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return thither: the Lord gave, and the Lord hath taken away: as it hath pleased the Lord so is it done: blessed be the name of the Lord."
*Job 1:21 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And we know that to them that love God, all things work together unto good, to such as, according to his purpose, are called to be saints."
*Romans 8:28 (Douay-Rheims)*

## Why this matters today

This is the most common reason people leave faith. It is also the best reason to take faith seriously, because every worldview has to face it.

## What's at stake

If you answer too fast, you hurt people. If you never answer, you leave them alone with it. The honest middle is hard.

## How to live it

- **Do not offer platitudes.** "Everything happens for a reason" is not helpful to a grieving person.
- **Sit with them.** Show up. Say little.
- **Be honest with God.** The Psalms are full of complaint.
- **Read Job.** Not a quick fix. A companion.
