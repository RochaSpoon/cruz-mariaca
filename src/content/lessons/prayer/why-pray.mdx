## The short version

Prayer is not a vending machine. It is a relationship with a God who wants to be known (CCC 2558-2565). You pray because he is there, not because it gets results.

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism describes prayer as the encounter of God's thirst with ours (CCC 2560). [VERIFY] God goes first. You are answering.

Jesus tells his disciples that they ought always to pray and not lose heart (Luke 18:1), and he tells them to ask, seek and knock (Luke 11:9). The point is not that asking always gets the thing you asked for. It is that asking keeps you in the conversation.

Most Christians agree on this much. Catholics, Orthodox and Protestants all pray, and most of them pray the Our Father. The differences come later, in whether you ask the saints to join you.

## Why this matters today

A lot of people only pray when something goes wrong. That is normal and not wrong, but it makes God sound like an emergency line.

Others quit praying because it did not "work." If prayer is a relationship, then silence is not the same as failure.

## What's at stake

If there is a God who loves you, ignoring him is a strange way to treat him. If there is not, praying costs you a few quiet minutes. Either way, an honest try is cheap.

## How to live it

- **Pick a time and keep it.** Ten minutes beats an hour you never do.
- **Talk to him like a person.** Tell him what you are angry about.
- **Stop grading your prayers.** You do not need to feel anything.
- **Pray when it is dull.** That is when it counts.
