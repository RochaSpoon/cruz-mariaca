## The short version

Anointing of the Sick is for anyone seriously ill, not only the dying. A priest anoints the forehead and hands with blessed oil and prays for healing and strength (CCC 1499-1532).

## What the Church teaches

James tells the sick to call the priests of the Church, who will pray over them and anoint them with oil in the Lord's name (James 5:14-15). The Church sees the sacrament in that passage. Mark also records the apostles anointing the sick (Mark 6:13).

The Catechism says it can be given to anyone whose health is in danger from illness or old age, before major surgery, and again if the illness gets worse (CCC 1514-1515).

Only a priest or a bishop can give it (CCC 1516). It can forgive sins when the person cannot go to Confession (CCC 1520). [VERIFY]

**Viaticum** is Communion given to someone who is dying. Together with Confession and the Anointing, it is what people mean by the last rites (CCC 1524-1525). [VERIFY]

## Scripture

> "Is any man sick among you? Let him bring in the priests of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord. And the prayer of faith shall save the sick man: and the Lord shall raise him up: and if he be in sins, they shall be forgiven him."
*James 5:14-15 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And they cast out many devils, and anointed with oil many that were sick, and healed them."
*Mark 6:13 (Douay-Rheims)*

## Why this matters today

Many families wait until the last hour to call a priest, because they think calling him means giving up. It does not. It is a prayer for healing and strength first.

## What's at stake

Delay can mean the sick person is unconscious or gone when the priest arrives. The Church wants this sacrament received while the person can take part in it.

## How to live it

- **Do not wait.** Call the parish when the diagnosis is serious, not when it is hopeless.
- **Ask a hospital chaplain** if you cannot reach your own priest.
- **Go to Confession first,** if the person can. Then the Anointing, then Communion.
- **Be there.** Pray with them. Your presence is part of the sacrament.
