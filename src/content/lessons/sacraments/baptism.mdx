## The short version

Baptism is the first sacrament. It washes away original sin and every sin before it, makes you a child of God, and joins you to the Church (CCC 1213, 1263). You get it once.

## What the Church teaches

Water and the words "I baptize you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit" make a valid baptism (CCC 1239-1240). In an emergency, anyone can do it, even someone who is not baptized, if they intend what the Church intends (CCC 1256).

Baptism leaves a permanent mark on the soul, called a character. That is why it cannot be repeated (CCC 1272). The Church also recognizes baptisms in other Christian communities that use water and the Trinitarian words (CCC 1271).

Baptism does not make you perfect. You still struggle. It gives you grace and a new identity to fight from.

## Why this matters today

Plenty of Catholics were baptized as babies and never thought about it again. Plenty of people outside the Church were baptized too, and it still counts. If that is you, you are already closer to the Church than you may feel.

## What's at stake

The Church teaches that baptism is the door to the other sacraments and to life in Christ (CCC 1213). Without it, the rest of the sacramental life is closed. The Church also teaches that God is not bound by his sacraments, and she entrusts those who die unbaptized to his mercy (CCC 1257-1261). [VERIFY]

## How to live it

- **Find your baptism date.** Ask your parents or the parish. Celebrate it like a birthday.
- **Remember what it means when you feel like a fraud.** You were claimed before you earned anything.
- **If you are not baptized and want to be,** start with a parish and ask about OCIA.
- **If you were baptized and have drifted,** you do not need to be baptized again. You need to come back.
