## The short version

Confirmation completes baptism. A bishop, usually, anoints you with chrism and the Holy Spirit seals you for the Christian life (CCC 1285-1288, 1312). It is not a graduation from church.

## What the Church teaches

The Church traces Confirmation to the apostles laying hands on the baptized so that they would receive the Holy Spirit (Acts 8:14-17).

The ordinary minister in the Latin Church is the bishop (CCC 1312-1313). A priest may confirm in certain cases: when he baptizes an adult or receives a baptized person into full communion, when the bishop gives him the faculty, or when a person is in danger of death (Code of Canon Law, can. 883-884; CCC 1314). In the Eastern Churches, babies are confirmed right after baptism by the priest (CCC 1290-1292).

Like baptism, Confirmation marks the soul and cannot be repeated (CCC 1304-1305).

Confirmation gives gifts that are meant to be used: strength to live the faith and to speak about it (CCC 1303).

## Why this matters today

A lot of teens are confirmed and then quit going to church the next month. That is common, and the sacrament is not to blame. It was meant as the start of adult faith, not the end of religious ed.

## What's at stake

If Confirmation is only a ritual to finish, it changes nothing. The Church says it gives a real strengthening of the Spirit (CCC 1303). The question is whether you ever use it.

## How to live it

- **If you have not been confirmed,** ask your parish. Adults can be confirmed too.
- **If you were confirmed and drifted,** pray the Veni Creator or just say: Holy Spirit, I was sealed. Help me act like it.
- **Pick a patron saint** and read about their life. Confirmation names are not decoration.
- **Do one thing this week that takes courage,** like praying in front of someone, or refusing a joke you know is wrong.
