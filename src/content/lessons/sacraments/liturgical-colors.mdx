## The short version

The color of the priest's vestments tells you where you are in the Church year. Green is ordinary time, violet is Advent and Lent, white is joy and the great feasts, red is the Spirit and martyrs, and rose may be used on Gaudete Sunday and Laetare Sunday where that is the practice (GIRM 346).

## What the Church teaches

**Green** is used in Ordinary Time, the long stretch outside the big seasons. It stands for hope and growth.

**Violet** is for Advent and Lent, and sometimes for funerals. It stands for waiting and repentance.

**White** is for Christmas and Easter, feasts of the Lord, of Mary, of the angels, and of saints who were not martyrs. It stands for joy and purity.

**Red** is for Palm Sunday, Good Friday, Pentecost, and feasts of apostles, evangelists and martyrs. It stands for the Spirit and for blood.

**Rose** may be used on the third Sunday of Advent (Gaudete) and the fourth Sunday of Lent (Laetare), as a break in the penance.

This is discipline, not doctrine. The rules can change, and colors vary slightly by place. The site's accent color follows the same calendar.

## Why this matters today

You may notice how much the Church year shapes the Mass and not how much the news shapes it. That is a reminder that she measures time differently.

## What's at stake

Nothing here changes what you believe. It is a visual language that helps you pay attention to the season you are in.

## How to live it

- **Check the color when you walk in.** Ask yourself what season it is and what that asks of you.
- **Do not skip Ordinary Time.** Ordinary does not mean unimportant. Most of life is the long stretch.
- **Watch the shift** at Gaudete and Laetare. Notice what it feels like to get one day of ease.
