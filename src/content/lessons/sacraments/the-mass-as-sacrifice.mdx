## The short version

The Church does not teach that Jesus is killed again at every Mass. She teaches that the one sacrifice of Calvary is made present, so that we can share in it (CCC 1362-1367).

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism says the sacrifice of Christ and the sacrifice of the Eucharist are one single sacrifice (CCC 1367). Only the manner of offering differs.

Catholics point to Malachi, who says a clean offering will be made among the nations from the rising of the sun to its going down (Malachi 1:11), and to Hebrews, where Christ offers himself once and for all (Hebrews 7:24-27).

The key word is anamnesis, a Greek word for remembering that makes the past present (CCC 1362-1363). Jews at Passover do the same: the meal does not repeat the Exodus, it enters it.

The strongest Protestant objection comes from Hebrews itself. Christ was offered once, and there is no more offering for sin (Hebrews 10:10-14). Catholics agree. They say the Mass adds nothing to Calvary. It is Calvary, made present.

## Why this matters today

If you grew up Catholic, you may have heard that Protestants think the Mass is a re-sacrifice. Some do. Many Catholics also hear "sacrifice" and picture something gory. Neither version is what the Church says.

## What's at stake

This is the biggest dividing line of the Reformation. If the Mass is only a meal, Catholics have added something. If it is a sacrifice, Protestants have lost something. The honest move is to read both sides.

## How to live it

- **Offer something at Mass.** A person, a worry, a sin, a thing you gave up. Name it in your head.
- **Learn the word anamnesis.** It will change how Mass sounds.
- **Read Hebrews 9 and 10 with a Catholic and a Protestant commentary.** Compare them.
- **Do not wave this away** with "it's just a ritual."
