## The short version

The Mass has two main parts, the Liturgy of the Word and the Liturgy of the Eucharist, with an opening and a closing around them (CCC 1345-1355). The same shape goes back to the second century.

## What the Church teaches

Justin Martyr, around 155, describes a Sunday gathering: readings, a talk by the one presiding, prayers, bread and wine brought forward, thanks, and Communion (First Apology 67; CCC 1345).

Today it runs like this: Introductory Rites, Liturgy of the Word, Liturgy of the Eucharist, Communion Rite, Concluding Rites. The details are set out in the General Instruction of the Roman Missal. [VERIFY]

Parts like the Gloria and the Creed change by day. The shape does not. You can walk into a Mass in a language you do not speak and still know where you are.

There is a full walkthrough, step by step, on the Mass page of this site.

## Why this matters today

Many people show up late and leave early. A parish with a good homily and a bad parking lot can feel like an event. The structure is what holds it together when the music or the preaching does not.

## What's at stake

The Church treats the Mass as the center of Catholic life (CCC 1324). It is hard to give your attention to something you do not understand. Learning the parts is a practical way to start.

## How to live it

- **Get there early.** The Mass starts before the first hymn.
- **Put your phone away.** Not on silent. Away.
- **Follow along in a missal** or the readings on bible.usccb.org, once.
- **Stay to the end.** The dismissal is part of the Mass.
