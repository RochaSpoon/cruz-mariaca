## The short version

Jesus said not to call anyone father (Matthew 23:9). Catholics call priests Father anyway. The Church answers that the New Testament uses the word for people in the Church, so the verse cannot be a ban on the word.

## What the Church teaches

Matthew 23 criticizes scribes who love titles and seats of honor. The warning is about pride and about taking the place that belongs to God.

Paul says he became the Corinthians' father in Christ through the Gospel (1 Corinthians 4:15). John writes to "fathers" in the Church (1 John 2:13).

The title is a custom of respect and of spiritual relationship, not a doctrine. It could be dropped without changing any teaching. [VERIFY]

A fuller treatment, with the objection in the words of an evangelical, is in the Myths section of this site.

## Why this matters today

The question comes up at family tables and in online arguments. It is a good test of whether you can read a verse in context instead of treating it as a one-liner.

## What's at stake

Nothing about salvation depends on it. The lesson is about how to read Scripture fairly, which matters more than the title does.

## How to live it

- **Read all of Matthew 23** before you quote verse 9.
- **Do not lecture a priest** about it in the parking lot.
- **If it bothers you,** you can say "Father" as a courtesy and nothing else. The Church is not asking for more.
