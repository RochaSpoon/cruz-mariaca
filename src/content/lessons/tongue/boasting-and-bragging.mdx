## The short version

The Catechism says "boasting or bragging is an offense against truth" (CCC 2481). Paul says "charity... is not puffed up" (1 Corinthians 13:4).

## What the Church teaches

"Boasting or bragging is an offense against truth" (CCC 2481).

Paul says that charity is patient and kind, does not envy, and is not puffed up (1 Corinthians 13:4).

The Catechism says lying is "to speak or act against the truth in order to lead into error someone who has the right to know the truth" (CCC 2483). Exaggeration to make yourself look better does this in a small way.

The related virtue is humility, which Catholic tradition treats as truth about oneself. See the lesson on humility.

## Scripture

> "Charity is patient, is kind: charity envieth not, dealeth not perversely; is not puffed up;"
*1 Corinthians 13:4 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Douay-Rheims renders the word as "charity." It is the same word often translated "love."

## Why this matters today

Social media rewards a polished version of yourself. Bragging is part of the format.

Telling the truth about something good you did is not boasting. The sin is exaggeration or showing off.

## What's at stake

If you need to be admired, you depend on other people's attention. The Church says that makes you unfree.

## How to live it

- **Let others praise you,** and stay quiet.
- **Give credit** where it is due.
- **Check what you post** for exaggeration.
- **Thank God** for what you have done well.
