## The short version

Calumny is harming someone's reputation by saying things contrary to the truth. The Catechism says it offends against justice and charity and requires reparation (CCC 2477, 2479, 2487).

## What the Church teaches

A person is guilty "of calumny who, by remarks contrary to the truth, harms the reputation of others and gives occasion for false judgments concerning them" (CCC 2477).

"Detraction and calumny destroy the reputation and honor of one's neighbor. Honor is the social witness given to human dignity, and everyone enjoys a natural right to the honor of his name and reputation and to respect. Thus, detraction and calumny offend against the virtues of justice and charity" (CCC 2479).

"Every offense committed against justice and truth entails the duty of reparation, even if its author has been forgiven" (CCC 2487).

The eighth commandment forbids bearing false witness against your neighbor (Exodus 20:16).

## Scripture

> "Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour."
*Exodus 20:16 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Douay-Rheims lists this as the eighth commandment, and so does the Catholic catechism.

## Why this matters today

Rumors, screenshots and posts can reach thousands of people in an hour. A false claim can do far more damage than it once could.

The Catechism says repairing the reputation is a duty, even after forgiveness (CCC 2487). Taking down a post is the start, not the end.

## What's at stake

You can forgive the one who lied about you, but you cannot take back what people heard. The Church treats reputation as something real.

## How to live it

- **Check before you share.**
- **If you passed on a lie,** correct it where you said it.
- **Apologize in the same place** you did the harm.
- **Go to Confession** and make repair part of it.
