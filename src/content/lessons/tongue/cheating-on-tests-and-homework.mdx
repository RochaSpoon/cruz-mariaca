## The short version

Cheating is lying and taking what is not yours. The Catechism calls lying "the most direct offense against the truth" and forbids unjustly taking others' goods (CCC 2408, 2483).

## What the Church teaches

"Lying is the most direct offense against the truth" (CCC 2483). Handing in work you did not do says something false about yourself.

"Any form of unjustly taking and keeping the property of others is against the seventh commandment," including "business fraud" (CCC 2409).

Proverbs says that a false balance is an abomination to the Lord, and that a just weight is his delight (Proverbs 11:1).

Cheating also wrongs the classmates who did not cheat. Justice requires giving each person their due (CCC 1807).

If you have cheated, the Catechism says every offense against justice and truth "entails the duty of reparation, even if its author has been forgiven" (CCC 2487).

## Scripture

> "A deceitful balance is an abomination before the Lord: and a just weight is his will."
*Proverbs 11:1 (Douay-Rheims)*

The proverb is about false weights in the marketplace. Cheating on tests is a similar false balance.

## Why this matters today

Many students feel they must cheat to keep up. The pressure is real. The Church does not say the pressure is not.

A grade you did not earn means nothing to you or to anyone who relies on it.

## What's at stake

If you cheat to qualify as a doctor, an engineer or a driver, other people pay for it later.

## How to live it

- **Ask for an extension** or help instead.
- **Study with a friend,** where it is allowed.
- **If you have cheated,** talk to the teacher.
- **Confess it** and make it right.
