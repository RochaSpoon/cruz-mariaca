## The short version

Constant complaining is a form of ingratitude. Paul says "do ye all things without murmurings and hesitations" (Philippians 2:14), and Numbers shows Israel's grumbling displeasing God (Numbers 11:1).

## What the Church teaches

Paul tells the Philippians to do everything without murmuring and arguing, so that they may be blameless (Philippians 2:14-15).

In Numbers, the people murmured against the Lord as if complaining of their hardships, and when the Lord heard it he was angry (Numbers 11:1).

The Catechism names ingratitude among the sins against God's love: it "fails or refuses to acknowledge divine charity and to return him love for love" (CCC 2094).

Gratitude is part of prayer. "The Eucharist" itself means thanksgiving (CCC 1328).

## Scripture

> "And do ye all things without murmurings and hesitations; That you may be blameless, and sincere children of God, without reproof, in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation; among whom you shine as lights in the world."
*Philippians 2:14-15 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "In the mean time there arose a murmuring of the people against the Lord, as it were repining at their fatigue. And when the Lord heard it he was angry. And the fire of the Lord being kindled against them, devoured them that were at the uttermost part of the camp."
*Numbers 11:1 (Douay-Rheims)*

Israel's grumbling in the desert is a long story in the Bible, and Paul uses it as a warning.

## Why this matters today

Everyone has real reasons to complain, and honesty about suffering is not a sin. The psalms lament too.

The sin is the habit: complaining about everything, to everyone, with no thanks.

## What's at stake

A complaining heart cannot notice what it has been given.

## How to live it

- **Say one thing you are thankful for** before you complain.
- **Take your complaint to God** first.
- **Fix what you can,** and accept what you cannot.
- **Keep a short list** of gratitude each night.
