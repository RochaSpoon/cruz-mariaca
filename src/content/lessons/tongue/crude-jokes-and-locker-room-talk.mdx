## The short version

Paul says that "fornication, and all uncleanness... let it not so much as be named among you," and lists "obscenity... foolish talking" as out of place (Ephesians 5:3-4). The Catechism asks for modesty of speech (CCC 2521-2522).

## What the Church teaches

Paul tells the Ephesians that fornication, impurity and greed should not even be named among saints, and that obscenity, foolish talk and coarse joking are out of place (Ephesians 5:3-4).

He tells the Colossians to put away anger, malice, blasphemy and filthy talk from their mouths (Colossians 3:8).

"Purity requires modesty, an integral part of temperance. Modesty protects the intimate center of the person" (CCC 2521).

Modesty "protects the mystery of persons and their love" (CCC 2522).

Respect for reputation also applies: jokes that expose or demean real people injure them (CCC 2477).

## Scripture

> "But fornication, and all uncleanness, or covetousness, let it not so much as be named among you, as becometh saints: Or obscenity, or foolish talking, or scurrility, which is to no purpose; but rather giving of thanks."
*Ephesians 5:3-4 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "But now put you also all away: anger, indignation, malice, blasphemy, filthy speech out of your mouth."
*Colossians 3:8 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Douay-Rheims lists "scurrility," which means coarse joking.

## Why this matters today

Locker-room talk is common and often meant to bond. The cost is that it trains you to see people as objects, and it can hurt the people it targets.

The pressure to laugh is real. Staying quiet or changing the subject is a start.

## What's at stake

What you say shapes what you see. Crude talk about real people can turn them into things in your mind.

## How to live it

- **Do not join in** the jokes about real people.
- **Change the subject** or walk away.
- **Be the one** who says that is enough.
- **Replace it** with something you actually enjoy.
