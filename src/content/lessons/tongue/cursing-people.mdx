## The short version

Wishing harm or damnation on someone is a sin against charity. Paul says "bless them that persecute you: bless, and curse not" (Romans 12:14).

## What the Church teaches

Paul says to bless those who persecute you, to bless and not to curse (Romans 12:14).

James says that blessing and cursing come out of the same mouth, and that this ought not to be so (James 3:10).

The Catechism says "deliberate hatred is contrary to charity" (CCC 2303), and that Christ asks us to love even our enemies (CCC 1825).

Cursing is different from lament. The psalms of the Bible cry out for justice, and the Church prays them. But the Catechism does not permit hatred of the person (CCC 2303).

## Scripture

> "Bless them that persecute you: bless, and curse not."
*Romans 12:14 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "By it we bless God and the Father: and by it we curse men, who are made after the likeness of God. Out of the same mouth proceedeth blessing and cursing. My brethren, these things ought not so to be."
*James 3:9-10 (Douay-Rheims)*

James says that with the tongue we bless God and curse men made in his likeness, and that this should not be.

## Why this matters today

"Go to hell" is casual speech. The Church asks us to take the words seriously, since they say what we wish for another person.

Some people curse in anger and regret it right away. That is human, and the remedy is repentance, not scruples.

## What's at stake

Cursing trains the heart toward hatred. Blessing trains it toward love.

## How to live it

- **Replace the curse** with a prayer for the person, even a short one.
- **Notice** what you say when you are angry.
- **Apologize** if you said something you did not mean.
- **Read Romans 12.**
