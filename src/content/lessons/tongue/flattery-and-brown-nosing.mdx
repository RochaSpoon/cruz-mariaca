## The short version

The Catechism forbids "every word or attitude... which by flattery, adulation, or complaisance encourages and confirms another in malicious acts." Flattery that only tries to be agreeable is a venial sin (CCC 2480).

## What the Church teaches

"Every word or attitude is forbidden which by flattery, adulation, or complaisance encourages and confirms another in malicious acts and perverse conduct. Adulation is a grave fault if it makes one an accomplice in another's vices or grave sins" (CCC 2480).

"Neither the desire to be of service nor friendship justifies duplicitous speech" (CCC 2480).

"Adulation is a venial sin when it only seeks to be agreeable, to avoid evil, to meet a need, or to obtain legitimate advantages" (CCC 2480).

Proverbs says that a man who flatters his neighbor spreads a net for his feet (Proverbs 29:5).

## Scripture

> "A man that speaketh to his friend with flattering and dissembling words, spreadeth a net for his feet."
*Proverbs 29:5 (Douay-Rheims)*

The proverb warns that flattery is a trap, both for the person flattered and for the speaker.

## Why this matters today

Praise is good when it is true. Telling your boss what they want to hear is different.

The Catechism makes a useful distinction: a small courtesy is a venial matter, and flattery that helps someone do wrong is grave (CCC 2480).

## What's at stake

If everyone tells you what you want to hear, you never learn anything. If you tell others only that, you are not their friend.

## How to live it

- **Praise what is actually good.**
- **Be kind, and say the true thing** when it matters.
- **Do not use praise** to get something.
- **Ask a friend** to tell you the truth.
