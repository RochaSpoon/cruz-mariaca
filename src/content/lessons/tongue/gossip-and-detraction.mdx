## The short version

There are three sins of the tongue against another person's reputation:

1. **Rash judgment:** assuming the worst about someone without enough evidence.
2. **Detraction:** telling someone's real faults to people who have no need to know.
3. **Calumny (slander):** saying false things that damage someone's reputation.

All three are sins against justice and charity. They can be grave.

## What the Church teaches

"Respect for the reputation of persons forbids every attitude and word likely to cause them unjust injury" (CCC 2477). The Catechism defines all three sins: rash judgment, detraction ("without objectively valid reason, discloses another's faults and failings to persons who did not know them"), and calumny ("by remarks contrary to the truth, harms the reputation of others") (CCC 2477).

"Detraction and calumny destroy the reputation and honor of one's neighbor" and offend against justice and charity (CCC 2479). Because they're sins against justice, they require reparation: you have to try to repair the damage (CCC 2487).

Aquinas teaches that detraction is of its nature a mortal sin when it seriously damages someone's good name, because a reputation is worth more than money (*ST* II-II, q. 73, a. 2).

## The saints

St. Ignatius of Loyola asks every Christian to be "more ready to save his neighbor's proposition than to condemn it." In other words, give people the most generous reading of what they said before you criticize (*Spiritual Exercises* 22, in an older English translation; newer ones say "put a good interpretation on a neighbor's statement").

There's an old story told about St. Philip Neri. A woman confessed gossiping. He told her to take a feather pillow to the top of a bell tower, cut it open, and come back. Then he told her to go collect every feather. She said it was impossible. He said that's what gossip is like. (Traditional story; not from a primary source.)

## Why this hits hard today

Group chats, screenshots, finstas, anonymous accounts, call-out posts. A rumor at school can reach hundreds of people in an hour and live forever online. Cancel culture is often mass detraction: digging up someone's faults and spreading them as widely as possible.

## When is it okay to talk about someone's faults?

When there's a real reason: protecting someone from harm, getting advice from a counselor or priest, reporting abuse or a crime to the right people. Reporting abuse is never gossip. It's justice.

## What's at stake

A reputation, once destroyed, is very hard to rebuild. If you spread lies, you're obligated to correct them. If the damage is serious, so is the sin.

## How to fight it

- **Before you speak about someone absent, ask:** Is it true? Is it necessary? Is it kind? Would I say it to their face?
- **Don't listen to gossip either.** Change the subject or say something good about the person.
- **Don't screenshot and forward.**
- **Repair it.** If you spread something false, go back to the same people and correct it.
