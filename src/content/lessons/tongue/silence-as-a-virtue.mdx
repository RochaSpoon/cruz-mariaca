## The short version

There is a time to be silent and a time to speak (Ecclesiastes 3:7). The Church's teaching on speech asks us to be slow to speak and to keep silence about what ought not be known (CCC 2489; James 1:19).

## What the Church teaches

Ecclesiastes says there is a time to keep silence and a time to speak (Ecclesiastes 3:7).

James says that everyone should be quick to hear, slow to speak and slow to anger (James 1:19).

Proverbs says that sin is not absent where there are many words, and that the one who restrains his lips is wise (Proverbs 10:19).

The Catechism says charity and respect for the truth "should dictate the response to every request for information," and that silence can be right "about what ought not be known" (CCC 2489).

Silence is also part of prayer. The Catechism describes contemplative prayer as "silent love" and a gaze fixed on Jesus (CCC 2715-2717).

## Scripture

> "A time to rend, and a time to sew. A time to keep silence, and a time to speak."
*Ecclesiastes 3:7 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "You know, my dearest brethren. And let every man be swift to hear, but slow to speak, and slow to anger."
*James 1:19 (Douay-Rheims)*

Both verses are practical. They ask for timing and restraint, not permanent silence.

## Why this matters today

Many people feel they must have an opinion on everything, immediately. The Church's tradition says not to.

Silence is not the same as cowardice. When the truth must be said, the Catechism says to witness without equivocation (CCC 2471).

## What's at stake

Words you have not said can still be taken back. Words you have said cannot.

## How to live it

- **Wait a day** before answering something that stung.
- **Listen first.**
- **Spend ten minutes in silence** with God.
- **Know when not to speak,** and when you must.
