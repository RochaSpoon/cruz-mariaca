## The short version

Passing off work an AI wrote as your own is a lie. The Catechism says a lie is speaking or acting against the truth to lead someone into error (CCC 2482-2486).

## What the Church teaches

"A lie consists in speaking a falsehood with the intention of deceiving" (CCC 2482). To lie is "to speak or act against the truth in order to lead into error someone who has the right to know the truth" (CCC 2483).

A teacher or employer who asks for your work has the right to know whose it is. Presenting someone else's, or a machine's, as yours leads them into error.

"The gravity of a lie is measured against the nature of the truth it deforms, the circumstances, the intentions of the one who lies, and the harm suffered by its victims" (CCC 2484).

Cheating can also be theft: taking credit or marks that were not earned is "unjustly taking and keeping" what belongs to others (CCC 2409).

Paul tells the Colossians not to lie to one another, since they have put off the old man (Colossians 3:9).

## Scripture

> "Lie not one to another: stripping yourselves of the old man with his deeds, And putting on the new, him who is renewed unto knowledge, according to the image of him that created him."
*Colossians 3:9-10 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says to put off the old man with his deeds and put on the new man.

## Why this matters today

Many schools and workplaces have their own rules about AI. Break them and you are also breaking a promise (CCC 2410).

Using a tool openly and as allowed is not lying. Hiding it when you are asked not to is.

## What's at stake

If you cheat, you become someone who cheats. The marks do not teach you anything.

## How to live it

- **Read the rules** for each class or job.
- **Say how you used it** if asked.
- **Ask for help,** not for a finished product.
- **If you cheated,** tell the teacher and accept the consequences.
