## The short version

Fortitude is the virtue that gives firmness in difficulties and constancy in pursuing the good. It lets you face fear, even fear of death (CCC 1808).

## What the Church teaches

"Fortitude is the moral virtue that ensures firmness in difficulties and constancy in the pursuit of the good." It "strengthens the resolve to resist temptations and to overcome obstacles in the moral life" (CCC 1808).

It "enables one to conquer fear, even fear of death, and to face trials and persecutions." It disposes a person "even to renounce and sacrifice his life in defense of a just cause" (CCC 1808).

The Catechism quotes Scripture: "The Lord is my strength and my song" and "In the world you have tribulation, but be of good cheer, I have overcome the world" (CCC 1808).

Martyrdom is the supreme example: the martyr "endures death through an act of fortitude" (CCC 2473).

## Scripture

> "These things I have spoken to you, that in me you may have peace. In the world you shall have distress: but have confidence, I have overcome the world."
*John 16:33 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "The Lord is my strength and my praise: and he is become my salvation."
*Psalms 117:14 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Douay-Rheims numbers this psalm 117. It is the same verse the Catechism quotes: "The Lord is my strength and my praise."

## Why this matters today

Courage is sometimes pictured as loudness or toughness. The Church describes it as steadiness, and says it sometimes takes the form of patience.

In ordinary life most of us need fortitude to say a small true thing when everyone else is quiet.

## What's at stake

Without fortitude, every other virtue breaks when it costs you something.

## How to live it

- **Do one hard thing you have been putting off.**
- **Notice when fear is making your choices.**
- **Read a martyr's story** (CCC 2473-2474).
- **Pray for strength,** not for the problem to vanish.
