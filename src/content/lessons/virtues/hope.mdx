## The short version

Hope is the virtue by which we desire heaven as our happiness, trusting in Christ's promises and not in our own strength (CCC 1817). It is not optimism.

## What the Church teaches

"Hope is the theological virtue by which we desire the kingdom of heaven and eternal life as our happiness, placing our trust in Christ's promises and relying not on our own strength, but on the help of the grace of the Holy Spirit" (CCC 1817).

Hope "responds to the aspiration to happiness which God has placed in the heart of every man." It "keeps man from discouragement," "sustains him during times of abandonment," and "opens up his heart in expectation of eternal beatitude" (CCC 1818).

The model is Abraham: "Hoping against hope, he believed, and thus became the father of many nations" (CCC 1819).

The Beatitudes raise our hope toward heaven. Through Christ's merits, God keeps us "in the hope that does not disappoint" (CCC 1820).

We can hope to persevere "to the end" and to obtain the joy of heaven. In hope, the Church prays "for all men to be saved" (CCC 1821).

## Scripture

> "And not only so; but we glory also in tribulations, knowing that tribulation worketh patience; And patience trial; and trial hope; And hope confoundeth not: because the charity of God is poured forth in our hearts, by the Holy Ghost, who is given to us."
*Romans 5:3-5 (Douay-Rheims)*

Paul says tribulation works patience and patience works hope, and that hope "confoundeth not."

## Why this matters today

Optimism says things will probably work out. Hope says God is faithful even if they do not.

Despair is the opposite, and the Catechism treats it as a sin against hope (CCC 2091).

## What's at stake

If there is no hope beyond this life, then suffering is only waste. Christian hope says it is not the last word.

## How to live it

- **Name what you are hoping for** and whether you are trusting God or yourself for it.
- **Pray the Beatitudes** (Matthew 5) when you are discouraged.
- **Talk to someone who is struggling** and offer something more than cheerfulness.
- **Read Romans 5:1-5.**
