## The short version

Justice is "the constant and firm will to give their due to God and neighbor" (CCC 1807).

## What the Church teaches

"Justice is the moral virtue that consists in the constant and firm will to give their due to God and neighbor." Justice toward God is called "the virtue of religion" (CCC 1807).

Toward other people, justice "disposes one to respect the rights of each and to establish in human relationships the harmony that promotes equity with regard to persons and to the common good" (CCC 1807).

The Catechism says "the just man, often mentioned in the Sacred Scriptures," is "distinguished by habitual right thinking and the uprightness of his conduct toward his neighbor" (CCC 1807).

Justice is one of the four cardinal virtues (CCC 1805).

## Scripture

> "Blessed are they that hunger and thirst after justice: for they shall have their fill."
*Matthew 5:6 (Douay-Rheims)*

> "And if a man love justice: her labours have great virtues; for she teacheth temperance, and prudence, and justice, and fortitude, which are such things as men can have nothing more profitable in life."
*Wisdom 8:7 (Douay-Rheims)*

Jesus calls blessed those who hunger and thirst after justice. In the Bible the word covers both fair dealing and a right relationship with God.

## Why this matters today

People often use "justice" to mean getting what they think they deserve. The Church starts with what we owe to others.

Some talk about justice only as a political matter, and some as only private honesty. The Catechism includes both (CCC 1807).

## What's at stake

If you do not give people their due, you take from them. That is true of wages, of reputation, and of what you owe to God.

## How to live it

- **List what you owe** to God, to your family, to your boss, to your neighbor.
- **Pay what you owe** on time.
- **Speak up** when someone is treated unfairly.
- **Give God his due.** Prayer and worship are part of justice (CCC 1807).
