## The short version

Prudence is "right reason in action." It is the virtue that sees what is good in a situation and picks the right way to do it (CCC 1806).

## What the Church teaches

"Prudence is the virtue that disposes practical reason to discern our true good in every circumstance and to choose the right means of achieving it." Aquinas, following Aristotle, calls it "right reason in action" (CCC 1806).

The Catechism says it is "not to be confused with timidity or fear, nor with duplicity or dissimulation" (CCC 1806).

It is also called the "charioteer of the virtues." It guides the others (CCC 1806).

The Catechism lists prudence among the four cardinal virtues (CCC 1805) and says it disposes us "to discern, in every circumstance, our true good" (CCC 1835).

## Scripture

> "The innocent believeth every word: the discreet man considereth his steps. No good shall come to the deceitful son: but the wise servant shall prosper in his dealings, and his way shall be made straight."
*Proverbs 14:15 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Catechism's phrase "the prudent man looks where he is going" comes from the same book of Proverbs (CCC 1806).

## Why this matters today

Prudence sounds like caution, and many people equate it with playing it safe. The Church says that is the wrong idea.

Another mistake is to think it means being clever. The Catechism says it has nothing to do with duplicity (CCC 1806).

## What's at stake

Good intentions without prudence do harm. You can mean well and still do the wrong thing.

## How to live it

- **Ask before you act** what is actually good in this situation.
- **Get advice from someone wiser** than you before big decisions.
- **Think about the means,** not only the goal.
- **Pray first.** Prudence is aided by grace.
