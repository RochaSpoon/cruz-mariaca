## The short version

The Beatitudes are the heart of Jesus' preaching. The Catechism says they respond to our desire for happiness and point to the Kingdom and the vision of God (CCC 1716-1721).

## What the Church teaches

"The Beatitudes are at the heart of Jesus' preaching. They take up the promises made to the chosen people since Abraham" and order them "to the Kingdom of heaven" (CCC 1716).

"The Beatitudes depict the countenance of Jesus Christ and portray his charity." They are "the paradoxical promises that sustain hope in the midst of tribulations" (CCC 1717).

They "respond to the natural desire for happiness," a desire "of divine origin" (CCC 1718).

The New Testament describes beatitude as the Kingdom, the vision of God, joy and rest (CCC 1720).

"God put us in the world to know, to love, and to serve him, and so to come to paradise" (CCC 1721).

The Catechism says true happiness "is not found in riches or well-being, in human fame or power, or in any human achievement," but "in God alone" (CCC 1723).

## Scripture

> "Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed are the meek: for they shall possess the land. Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted. Blessed are they that hunger and thirst after justice: for they shall have their fill. Blessed are the merciful: for they shall obtain mercy. Blessed are the clean of heart: for they shall see God. Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called children of God. Blessed are they that suffer persecution for justice' sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed are ye when they shall revile you, and persecute you, and speak all that is evil against you, untruly, for my sake: Be glad and rejoice, for your reward is very great in heaven. For so they persecuted the prophets that were before you."
*Matthew 5:3-12 (Douay-Rheims)*

The word "blessed" means happy in the deepest sense. Jesus gives eight of them, and the first and last promise the same thing: the kingdom of heaven.

## Why this matters today

Many people read the Beatitudes as advice for nice people. The Catechism reads them as a portrait of Jesus and a promise.

They also run against how we normally judge a good life. Poverty, mourning and persecution are not the usual goals.

## What's at stake

If happiness is what you can get, you will always want more. The Church says it is a gift that can only be received from God (CCC 1727).

## How to live it

- **Read all eight** and pick the one you most avoid.
- **Ask what you are really chasing** when you chase happiness.
- **Choose one beatitude** and live it for a week.
- **Notice who in your life** already lives them.
