## The short version

Four virtues are called "cardinal" because the others are grouped around them: prudence, justice, fortitude and temperance (CCC 1805).

## What the Church teaches

"A virtue is an habitual and firm disposition to do the good." It lets a person "not only to perform good acts, but to give the best of himself" (CCC 1803).

Human virtues are "habitual perfections of intellect and will that govern our actions, order our passions, and guide our conduct." They make possible "ease, self-mastery, and joy in leading a morally good life" (CCC 1804).

"Four virtues play a pivotal role and accordingly are called 'cardinal'; all the others are grouped around them" (CCC 1805).

**Prudence** discerns our true good and chooses the right means. **Justice** gives God and neighbor their due. **Fortitude** gives firmness in difficulties. **Temperance** moderates the attraction of pleasures (CCC 1806-1809).

These virtues are acquired "by education, by deliberate acts and by a perseverance ever-renewed." With God's help they are "purified and elevated by divine grace" (CCC 1810).

## Scripture

> "And if a man love justice: her labours have great virtues; for she teacheth temperance, and prudence, and justice, and fortitude, which are such things as men can have nothing more profitable in life."
*Wisdom 8:7 (Douay-Rheims)*

The Catechism quotes this verse from Wisdom to show that the four virtues are named in Scripture, though not by the word "cardinal" (CCC 1805).

## Why this matters today

People talk about values as though they were opinions. The Church talks about virtues as habits you build, like a muscle.

Some people think that if salvation is grace, effort does not matter. The Catechism says grace purifies and elevates the virtues. It does not replace them (CCC 1810).

## What's at stake

If character is accidental, you cannot change it. If virtues are habits, you can practice them. The Church bets on the second.

## How to fight it

- **Pick one of the four** and do one small deliberate act of it today.
- **Repeat it.** The Catechism says virtue comes from "repeated efforts" (CCC 1810).
- **Ask which one you are weakest in.** Start there.
- **Pray for the help.** The virtues are lifted by grace (CCC 1810).
